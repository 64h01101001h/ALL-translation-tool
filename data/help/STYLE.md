# Diamond Cutter Classics — House Style

*Source: the Diamond Cutter Classics Style Guide, 25 August 2023,
received from the editorial team 2026-08-14 (original banked at
docs/standards/DCC_Style_Guide_2023-08-25.docx). This is the
publishing standard the Draft pane's "House style check…" button
enforces mechanically where a machine can; everything subtler
stays your judgment. Chicago Manual of Style + Merriam-Webster
(US English) govern anything not listed.*

## Style: Mechanics

- **Curly quotes, never straight** — and never fix them with a
  global Find/Replace All (it introduces wrong-direction marks;
  change individual marks only).
- **En dash (–) for number ranges** (ff. 25a–27b), never a
  hyphen.
- **Em dash (—) in text**, never `--` and never an en dash.
- **No tabs or spaces for indentation** (the manually-built table
  of contents is the one exception).
- **Two spaces after sentence-ending punctuation**; one space
  after a colon, comma, or semicolon.
- **Serial commas** (red, white, and blue).
- **Ampersand (&) for pairs only** (cause & effect), never in a
  serial list (red, white, and blue — not red, white, & blue).
- **Spell out single-digit numbers**, numerals for the rest — but
  keep the immediate context consistent ("most were between 13
  and 18, two were 11 and one was 9").
- **ad / ce / bc in small caps, no preceding space**: 500bc,
  650ad.
- Body text is **Palatino Linotype 12pt, fully justified**;
  section numbers 10pt (removed from English-only manuscripts).
- Sanskrit letters Palatino Linotype lacks (ṝ ḹ) are set in
  Times New Roman 12pt; the rest (ṛ ḷ ṃ ḥ ṅ ṭ ḍ ṇ ṣ ṁ ṡ) stay
  Palatino.

## Style: Word Use (the house list)

- **accurate perception** — never "valid perception"
- **autocommentary** — not "auto-commentary"
- **ebook** — not "eBook"
- **Enlightened Being** (ruling of 11/22)
- **the Jewel of Realizations** — not "Ornament of Realizations"
  (12/15/21), and not "The Jewel of Realizations" mid-sentence
  (6/28/23)
- **Independent branch / Consequence branch** — not "group"
- **Middle Way School / Mind Only School** — no hyphens
- **mindstream** — one word, no hyphen
- **partless** — one word
- **wood-carver** — hyphenated
- **in between / between** as adverb or preposition;
  **in-between** as adjective or noun
- **aka** — not "a.k.a."
- Prefix compounds are normally closed; hyphenate before a
  capital or numeral (sub-Saharan, pre-1950), before a compound
  term (non-self-sustaining), to prevent misreading
  (anti-intellectual), in double prefixes (sub-subentry), and
  when a prefix stands alone (over- and underused).

## Style: Foreign Words & Titles

- Foreign words (including names) appear in **closest normal
  English pronunciation** — *mahayana*, *jukpa*. Only where a
  technical word is treated in detail do **diacritics**
  (*mahāyāna*) and **ACIP transliteration** (*'jug-pa*) appear.
- **Sanskrit titles**: divide words by the GMR standard (the
  smallest words that don't force diacritic changes); capitalize
  every word.
- **Tibetan titles**: join word parts with dashes; capitalize the
  root letter of main words (GMR standard).
- English titles follow Chicago headline style: first and last
  words capitalized; articles, short conjunctions, and
  prepositions lowercased (unless used adverbially — Look Up);
  a subtitle after a colon always opens with a capital; after an
  em dash it need not.

## Style: Italics & Quotation Marks

- Punctuation following an italicized word is italicized — but
  when italics mark **root-text words**, the following mark is
  italicized only if the root text itself has that (or a
  similar) mark there. When in doubt, query with a Comment.
- Parentheses and brackets italicize with their *surroundings*,
  not with their contents.
- **Footnote markers are never italicized.** Footnote leads (and
  their colon) are.
- Translators' notes are typically italicized; inside a fully
  italicized paragraph, book titles flip to roman.
- Closing quotation mark goes **inside** ending punctuation when
  quoting a word or phrase; **outside** when the quotation is a
  full sentence. Periods and commas sit inside quotation marks;
  semicolons and colons outside; question marks and exclamation
  points by sense.

## Style: Citations & Bibliography

- First citation of a bibliography entry: "See ff. 25a–27b in
  bibliography entry B25 (ACIP digital text S25004)." Thereafter:
  "See ff. 2a–3b (S2, TD03786)."
- **%** marks a correction to the Tibetan OR a provisional
  bibliography number; **%?** a possible correction; **%%** a
  placeholder (mainly bibliography citations in footnotes). If
  provisional markers survive into an editing copy, leave them
  unless asked.
- Source-language page numbers ride in square brackets — "page"/
  "folio" spelled out first, then "p."/"f.".
- Bibliography order is alphabetical **in the source language**,
  by author name (ignoring parenthesized parts), then title
  (ignoring Arya, a, an, the).
- **Sanskrit entry format**: Author (Tib: name), dates. English
  Title (Sanskrit) (Tib: Tibetan title, Tibetan translation at
  ACIP catalog number, folio range of Vol. N [letter] in the …
  Section [Sanskrit, Tibetan] of the collection [edition]).
- **Tibetan entry format**: Author, dates. English Title (Tibetan
  title, ACIP number), folio range.

## Style: Manuscript Workflow

- Edit only in Word with **Track Changes on**; corrections go
  directly in the manuscript; queries go in **Comments** (a
  footnote's query attaches to its marker in the main text).
- Save as `NAME date_YourName date.docx` (e.g., "INTERLUDE 12 31
  22_Bets Greer 1 5 23.docx").
- Before editing, verify the Word settings the guide specifies:
  smart-quotes autocorrect ON, automatic language detection OFF,
  all text marked English (US).
- Ignore pagination and layout (the manuscript predates page
  design) — but flag anything strange in a Comment, and check
  the hand-built table of contents against the actual headings.

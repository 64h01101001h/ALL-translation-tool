Segment 24 — the root-text line and the sutra citation

TIBETAN (WYLIE)

GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Here we will discuss the line of the root text which says, "The Three Jewels, the Buddha and the rest." Let us first consider the section of the middle-length sutra on the Mother which includes the lines: Do not think that this very Knowledge of All Things is something which applies to what you can see, and do not think it is separate from what you can see.∅→period Just so, never view what you can see itself as being real.∅→period

Segment 25 — the syllogism: what the sutra lines are

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
zhes sogs kyi yum bar ma'i mdo tshig chos can, khyod dkon mchog gsum la 'doms pa'i gdams ngag yin te, dkon mchog gsum po de don dam par grol ba don gnyer gyi skyes bu'i skyabs gnas ma yin zhing , tha snyad du grol ba don gnyer gyi skyes bu'i skyabs gnas yin no, , zhes 'doms par byed pa'i yum bar ma'i mdo tshig yin pa'i phyir
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
These are the "Instruction on the Three Jewels," for they are words from the middle-length sutra on the Mother which give us the following advice: These Three Jewels are no place of refuge for persons who seek an ultimate liberation.∅→period They are a place of refuge for persons who seek a liberation only in words.∅→period
SYLLOGISM full debate format: subject (chos can), thesis, reason (yin pa'i phyir)
├─ SUBJECT zhes sogs kyi yum bar ma'i mdo tshig chos can "These" — see note (1)
├─ THESIS khyod…'doms pa'i gdams ngag yin te "are the 'Instruction on the Three Jewels'"
│  ├─ khyod pron. "you" — debate address to the subject ∅ — see note (2)
│  ├─ EMBEDDED · la-frame dkon mchog gsum la 'doms pa'i "which advises on the Three Jewels"
│  └─ te Wilson: semi-final, opens the elaboration ∅ (his colon)
├─ THE QUOTED ADVICE negated copular clause + zhing + affirmed copular clause
│  ├─ NP · parallel frame ×2 grol ba don gnyer gyi skyes bu'i skyabs gnas "place of refuge for persons who seek… liberation"
│  │  double genitive chain: [seeker-of-liberation]-gyi persons-'i refuge-place — Wilson's stacked 'brel sgra
│  ├─ don dam par adv. "ultimate" vs tha snyad du adv. "only in words" — see note (3)
│  └─ zhing Wilson: coordinative gerund ∅ — his sentence break
└─ REASON zhes 'doms par byed pa'i yum bar ma'i mdo tshig yin pa'i phyir "for they are words… which give us the following advice"
AI NOTE — (1) chos can is the debate-format subject tag ("take as the subject…"); GMR renders the whole syllogism as flowing prose, so the tag's exponent is the plain "These…are" opening — the machine links the structure, not a phantom word. (2) khyod "you": the Analysis literally addresses the sutra lines in the second person, debate-style; GMR's "they" absorbs it — ∅ with the deixis shift flagged. (3) tha snyad du is standardly "conventionally / nominally"; GMR renders "only in words" ("only" his emphasis) — register note, linked verbatim, never auto-resolved (single-register rule). (4) His English re-orders the syllogism: thesis first, reason second, quoted advice last as a block quote — clause links cross accordingly; the hover shows c5 leaping to sentence one. (5) yin pa'i phyir "because they are" surfaces as the single word "for" plus the repeated copula — one span, two English touch-points.

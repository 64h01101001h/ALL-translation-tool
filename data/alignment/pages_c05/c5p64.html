AI NOTE — A one-line citation header. Most of the English sentence is Geshe Michael's own frame: The following selection is, the word Book, the hinge by and the dates (1357-1419) have no Tibetan under them and are left unwrapped. The Tibetan is a single genitive noun phrase — [rje] tsong kha pa chen pos brtsoms pa'i lam rim chen mo las, "from the lam rim chen mo composed by Lord Tsongkapa".

The title is split the way the two landed pages carrying this same English split it (c3p8/s23 and c3p40/s118): lam rim as one dictionary unit and chen mo as another, with las the ablative "from". It is split TIGHTER than those pages, which gave lam rim the whole of "on the Steps of the Path" and chen mo the whole of "the Great Book". Book is English-only — chen mo is simply "Great", the feminine form agreeing with the implied noun of the title — and "on the" is the English's own hinge.

The first syllable reads rjes, and it is left unlinked. "Lord" is unmistakably rje, the honorific; rjes is a different word meaning "after, in the wake of". A depth-5 span feeds the dictionary layer, so linking this one to "Lord" would mint rjes = "Lord". The spelling is filed as an erratum instead: C05:207 carries this frame byte-identically through brtsoms pa'i and spells it rje, and C05:365 gives this very title with rje tsong kha pa. The coincidence that rjes can mean "following" and the English opens "The following selection" is a trap: C05:365 carries the same frame over a Tibetan with no such word in it at all.

AI NOTE — A section header from Tsongkapa's Great Book on the Steps of the Path, introduced at C05:190, opening the treatment of the consequences of the ten non-virtues. Twelve syllables of bare outline formula — gsum pa de dag gi 'bras bu la gsum las, rnam smin gyi 'bras bu ni, roughly "third: of the three [topics] concerning their results, the ripened result…" — which Geshe Michael expands into two full English sentences of about thirty words. Most of that expansion is supplied and is deliberately left unwrapped: Here is the, section, a presentation on the, of the various kinds of bad deeds, We proceed in, parts, beginning with what are called the.

Three readings are worth naming. (1) de dag is the plural demonstrative, "those", pointing back at the deeds under discussion; Geshe Michael writes the antecedent out as "the various kinds of bad deeds". Linking the demonstrative to that phrase would bank a restored antecedent as a lexical equivalent, so it is nulled — the same ruling this campaign made at C05:151. The landed bank agrees: of 51 de dag links it is null 21 times and glossed "these/those/them/they/their" in the rest; not once is it a content noun. (2) las here is the ablative/partitive particle governing gsum, "from among the three", and is NOT the noun las, "deed" — even though the English of this very sentence contains "bad deeds". The landed bank separates the two cleanly: of the 87 las → deed/deeds links, 83 sit at d=5 and 4 at d=7 — not one at d=6 — while of the 321 las spans that ARE at d=6, 205 are null and 60 are "from". There is no "from" in this English to take, so the ablative takes the null. Banking las → "deeds" on this row would pass every mechanical check and still be false. (3) Both genitives are nulled. The English's two "of"s both fall inside the supplied noun phrase, and the second genitive's phrase, "the 'ripened' consequences", carries no "of" at all; the measured course policy nulls bare genitives anyway (128 of 140 gi, 303 of 324 gyi).

rnam smin is the standard abbreviation of rnam par smin pa, Sanskrit vipāka, the ripened or fully-ripening result. It is banked whole rather than split into rnam + smin: "ripen" cannot be cut out of "ripened" without breaking a word, and rnam has no separate exponent. The bank has this headword as "ripening" 11 times and "ripen" 10 times; "ripened" is the same equation in the adjectival surface form Geshe Michael used here. The word is claimed bare, without the quotation marks Geshe Michael put around it — the scare-quotes flag a technical term and are his punctuation, not part of the word.

AI NOTE — Reconciled from a Tibetan-first and an English-first proposal. The sentence is las lam bcu po re re yang … gsum gsum mo — "each one of the ten karmic paths too is three and three": each subdivides by the strength (chung / 'bring / che) of the three poisons behind it. Both analysts gave the predicate gsum gsum the "three" of "three kinds" and left the supplied classifier "kinds" unwrapped; the enumerating gsum after che is nulled rather than stretched onto a neighbour. The heaviest crossing on the page: the citation sa'i dngos gzhir gsungs la stands LAST in the Tibetan and opens the English sentence ("The Main Stage of Levels states that…"), so the second sentence is reordered end to end. re re / re res: the word is the distributive "each one", and it is linked only where Geshe Michael actually wrote a distributive — "each one" in the first sentence and "each" in the medium clause. The two re res whose clauses carry only "instances" are nulled: "instances" is a head noun Geshe Michael supplied for the nominalised chen po / chung ngu, and the landed bank contains no re re(s) → "instances" link. One verb, three births: skye bar occurs once in the Tibetan but governs all three rebirth clauses; Geshe Michael renders it three times ("is born", "take birth", "are born") and only the last — where the Tibetan word physically stands — is linked. Unwrapped by design: "Now", "can itself be divided into", "kinds", "the intensity of", "It says that", "you take birth as", and the "on" of "depending on".

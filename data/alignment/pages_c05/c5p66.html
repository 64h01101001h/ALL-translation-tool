Flat shape: the two Abhidharma term names are banked as their members rather than as whole compounds, so that rgyu mthun → "consistent", 'bras bu → "consequence", byed pa → "action" and myong ba → "experience" each land in the dictionary layer on their own. The full terms are byed pa rgyu mthun gyi 'bras bu (the consistent-cause consequence in respect of ACTION) and myong ba rgyu mthun gyi 'bras bu (… in respect of EXPERIENCE) — the standard Abhidharma pair, recorded here rather than spanned.

Two pieces of the English carry no Tibetan and stay unwrapped. AI NOTE — Two Tibetan names for one result, crossed against the English. Geshe Michael writes “the ‘environmental’ or ‘dominant’ consequence”, but the Tibetan gives bdag po'i 'bras bu first and dbang gi 'bras bu second, and Geshe Michael's own glossary (tier glossary, HTG2016) keys them the other way round: bdag po'i 'bras bu → “dominant” consequence (Skt. adhipati-phala; Hopkins “dominant effect”), dbang gi 'bras bu → environmental. So the two are aligned to the whole headwords, crossed, exactly as the dictionary keys them — not to their pieces. bdag po itself glosses “dominant”, so that member is linked; dbang does not gloss “environmental” (it is power / force / lord), so no member is taken from the second name. The head noun “consequence” is written once and is claimed once, inside the first name; the second “the consequence”, in “the consequence of killing”, has no 'bras bu under it at all and is left unwrapped. nus pa and stobs cross for the same reason: over “little nutrition or potency” the glossary puts potency under nus pa (Hopkins' first sense, and independently attested at TCS04Initiation:41, kha dog dri nus → “color, scent and potency”) and nutrition under stobs. snod (the container-world) has no exponent of its own and is left unlinked; “the outer world” rests on phyi and 'jig rten. Everything Geshe Michael supplied — “Next is what we call”, “Here for example”, “expresses itself”, “around you”, “in the fields”, “very”, “always”, “they are / they have”, “reaching”, “a full” and “of this” — carries no Tibetan and is left unwrapped.

AI NOTE — Third of the environmental (bdag po'i) consequences: what stealing does to the land. The Tibetan is a bare list — noun plus stative verb plus dang, eight times over — and Geshe Michael renders it as seven English clauses, so most of the connective and framing English (“Because … then”, the semicolons, “it”, “stay on”, “to remove hunger”) is supplied and is deliberately left unwrapped.

Five 'bras bu, three “crops.” Clauses three and four are pronominalised in the English (“they spoil”, “they never come up”), so those two 'bras bu are nulled rather than keyed to “they”: 'bras bu is one of the most loaded headwords in the lexicon — the landed bank reads it “result” 20 times and “results” 11 — and a pronoun is not an equivalent.

long ba is left unclaimed. The word means “blind,” said of grain that never fills, and Geshe Michael renders the consequence instead: “they never come up.” The landed bank's three long ba links read “blind,” “He is sightless” and the phonetic “Longwa”; none of them is an emergence verb. Banking “never come up” would put a sprouting verb and a temporal negative into the dictionary layer for a privative adjective, which is the same call this course already made at 46 (bshad “explain” was not banked as Geshe Michael's “promised”), so the clause is left with no span at all.

ches pa is given only “too.” ches marks excess; “long” and “much” are the two different things being exceeded and belong to Geshe Michael's English, not to the particle. The landed bank's one ches link is exactly “too.”

The one dang given English is the eighth, where Geshe Michael actually wrote “or” — a reading the bank already carries 21 times; the other seven are carried by semicolons and are nulled. Text verified byte-identical with C09:155, C16:776 and C17:502 in both columns.

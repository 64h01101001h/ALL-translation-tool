Segment 250 — an animal takes the same water as a home

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de nyid la dud 'gro la sogs pas gnas kyi blos gnas par byed pa dang
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
An animal or such, on the other hand, thinks of this same water as a place to stay, and makes his home there.dative/locative on the object of perception; the "of" in "thinks of" is English rection, not an exponentagentive closing la sogs pa; this English has no "by" for it to governgenitive binding gnas to blo; unrenderedterminative of the resultative byed pa frame; unrenderedclause-final connective carrying the list on into C05:251; the mid-sentence "and" of this English joins Geshe Michael's own two clauses
AI NOTE —

Asvabhava's second case in the four-realms argument: the one stream of water that a craving spirit saw as pus and blood (C05:249) is, for an animal, somewhere to live. The Tibetan is a single clause, de nyid la ... gnas kyi blos gnas par byed pa — "with the thought of [it as] a dwelling, makes [it] into a dwelling." Geshe Michael opens that out into two English clauses, "thinks of this same water as a place to stay, and makes his home there," so both occurrences of gnas have their own exponent: the first is the dwelling as conceived ("place to stay"), the second the dwelling as made ("home").

Left unwrapped as supplied English. "An", "as a", "his" and "there" are English syntax with nothing under them. "on the other hand" has no Tibetan either — the contrast is carried by the bare list connective dang running across C05:249–251, not by any word in this row. "water" is likewise supplied: there is no chu anywhere in this wylie, and de nyid says only "that very [thing]," pointing back to chu klung gi dngos po gcig in the previous segment. Both analysts refused to put "this same water" over de nyid, and that refusal is the main thing this page records.

blos → "thinks" is the span to challenge first. blo is a noun — mind, thought — here in the instrumental, "by the thought of"; the finite verb is Geshe Michael's. The bank carries blo → "to think" and "thought", so the semantic field is right, but no banked blos yet reads as a verb, and a reader should see the recasting rather than have it smoothed over. The "of" after it belongs to English rection and stays outside the span.

sogs takes only "such", the closing gesture, and the "or" in front of it is Geshe Michael's connective and stays outside. The agentive pas is nulled: this English has no "by".

Segment 251 — humans perceive it as water

AI NOTE — Third of the four perceptions of one river (craving spirit 249, animal 250, human here, pleasure being 252). Geshe Michael supplies "look at the same thing and" — roughly a fifth of the English — from the frame set at C05:249 (chu klung gi dngos po gcig la … mthong ba, "When they look at a single thing, a stream of water") and C05:250 ("this same water"). This row's wylie has no mthong and no dngos po gcig: there is nothing under that clause and it is left unwrapped, as are all three supplied "they", the hinges "of it" and "with it", the reflexive "themselves", and the "as" of "perceive it as water". chur is chu plus the terminative, banked whole against the bare noun on the bank's own precedent (rgyur→"cause", bde bar→"happiness", myong bar→"experience"); the terminative's "as" sits outside the span. khrus byed is kept as one compound — the noun-plus-byed light-verb shape the bank already carries at sgrub byed, 'phen byed, mngon du byed and some forty others — rather than split with byed nulled. 'jug is lexically "enter, engage"; "swim" is Geshe Michael's rendering for entering a body of water, the same move as the banked 'jug→"dive" at C04:194 and C04:196. The row's wylie stops mid-phrase: the closing go of der 'jug go opens C05:252, and the independent witness ILL:2332 carries der 'jug go entire (filed as an ingest artefact).

AI NOTE — Fourth and last of the four-views-of-water series running from C05:249 (craving spirits see pus and blood, animals a place to live, humans water): here the beings absorbed in the first formless level see it as space. "The water" has no Tibetan under it — the object of mthong is elided in the Tibetan and carried over from the earlier rows, so Geshe Michael supplies it, and it is deliberately left unwrapped. So are "Those" (there is no de in the wylie; rnams is a bare plural), "who are", "at the level we call the", "their ability to" and "has". The opening go is not part of this sentence: it is the final particle of 'jug go, cut across the row boundary — ILL:2332 carries the whole passage in one row reading ... der 'jug go ,nam mkha' ..., and P5:125/126 split it in the right place. nam mkha' mtha' yas skye mched is the name of the first formless absorption (Skt. ākāśānantyāyatana), and Geshe Michael marks it as a term with his own quotation marks, which is why the whole compound is cut against the whole quoted name; its three members mirror it in reverse. Caution for the dictionary layer: the English "realm" elsewhere in this bank belongs to khams, and skye mched (āyatana, the sense-bases) is banked once as "doors of sense" — "realm" here is Geshe Michael's rendering inside this term-name, not a general equivalent. nam mkha' then returns alone as nam mkhar, the thing actually seen: the same word naming the level and then naming the appearance. The members inside snyoms par zhugs pa were dropped rather than banked (see the reconciler's note). Nothing here is binding until a human reviews it.

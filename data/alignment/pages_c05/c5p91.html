AI NOTE — Two parallel examples of a craving spirit (yi dvags, preta) whose karma inverts the feel of moonlight and of sunlight. The Tibetan is one long reason clause closing lta bu yin pa'i phyir; Geshe Michael lifts lta bu to the very front as "Another example would be" and drops the phyir reason marker, so the English is inverted against the Tibetan at both ends — hence the explicit eng_order. Structure. Each half is [X] gis nyer len dang, [Y] kyis lhan cig byed rkyen byas pa la brten nas, "based on X being made the material cause and Y the contributing factor". One byas pa governs both conjuncts while the English says "provides" twice; the span is attached to the second, adjacent occurrence and the first "provides" is left unwrapped rather than claimed twice. This is the shape already landed at C05:270, which carries the identical construction about a ball of red-hot steel. Word boundaries settled by the master. dgun dus is itself a dictionary headword glossed "wintertime", and sos ka alone is only "spring" — so the "-time" of "springtime" is dus, and the span runs sos ka'i dus to match. lus shes is likewise a headword whose gloss list carries this very English, "consciousness of the body", with lus and shes banked as its members. Expansions with no Tibetan under them. "of its rays on the body" in each first clause (the wylie reads only zla ba'i reg bya'i cha shas gcig / nyi ma'i reg bya'i cha shas gcig, "one part of the moon's / the sun's tangible object"); "these two" after "Based on" (there is no de gnyis anywhere in the row); "So too with", "Another", "would be", and the supplied subject "the spirit". The Tibetan says a hot tangible object arises (skye ba); the English says the spirit "gets" a sensation, turning the Tibetan subject into an English object. skye ba is banked on "gets" because that equivalent stands in Geshe Michael's own glossary for the word, but the valency shift is real and is recorded here; de'i is NOT banked on "the spirit", which is a supplied subject in a different clause. reg bya twice per half. AI NOTE — Gendun Drup closes the point about craving spirits' sensations by citing Nagarjuna's Letter to a Friend: one line of commentary ending in phyir ('for/because'), then two verse lines quoted between las and zhes gsungs pa'i. The double shad , , is verse lineation and the capital In mid-sentence is the head of the second English line, not a new sentence. Geshe Michael's English crosses the Tibetan heavily: phyir and gsungs, last in the Tibetan, open the English clause. Nothing is banked for sos ka. The word is the hot season, and Geshe Michael himself renders this identical verse line — quoted again at ILL:2342 — 'Even the light of the moon Is hot, in the summer,' and glosses it in prose at ILL:2343 as 'the touch of moonlight in the summer.' The couplet turns on the inversion summer-moon-burns / winter-sun-freezes. So 'In the spring' is left unwrapped and filed as an erratum rather than banked as an equivalent for sos ka. Also unwrapped, having no Tibetan under them: '[Arya Nagarjuna's]', 'a fact', 'the light of', 'sensation', and the articles.

Segment 273 — why the sun is never cold

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de yang yul dus khyad par can de dag gi dbang gis skye ba yin gyi spyir de ltar skye ba ma yin te, nyi ma'i steng du grang ba'i reg bya yang med, zla ba'i steng du tsha ba'i reg bya yang med pa'i phyir, der thal, nyi ma'i steng du mun pa'i rdzas med pa'i phyir
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
All of this is caused by the extraordinary circumstances of the particular time and place, for generally speaking it never happens this way: there is nothing at all about the sun that can feel cold, and nothing about the moon that can feel hot. AI NOTE — Reconciled from a tibetan-first and an english-first reading; they agreed on the shape and differed on four spans.
The two phyir and the two "for" do NOT pair off. A mechanical pairing is available here and it is false. The FIRST "for" ("for generally speaking") sits on the adversative yin gyi - Geshe Michael's hinge, not a causal word - while the first phyir closes the sun-and-moon reason, which he carries on the COLON after "this way". Only the final phyir is banked, on the "for" that opens the last clause; the first is nulled.
khyad par can is rendered TWICE - "the extraordinary circumstances of the particular time and place" - and only one may carry the Tibetan. "extraordinary" is banked (4 existing links in the layer, 0 on "particular"; both are in Geshe Michael's gloss list for the word). "particular" and "circumstances" are his expansion and stay unwrapped.
mun pa is normally darkness (the layer carries "dusk" once). Here Geshe Michael writes 'that substance we call "covered space"' - his own technical rendering of the Vaibhashika claim that darkness is a real rdzas, space obscured by something. "covered space" is a listed equivalent in his gloss for mun pa, and the same English stands byte-identical at C15:418, so this is the published reading, not a corruption. Read the link as this passage's technical rendering, not as a bare lexical equation for darkness.
reg bya is the object of touch (the layer's four existing links all give "sensation"); Geshe Michael turns it into the ability "that can feel", and "feel" is a listed equivalent in his gloss. Only "feel" is spanned - the modal "can" is his English. steng du carries both "about" and "on" in his gloss list; it is "about the sun" / "about the moon" in the two negative clauses and "on the sun" in the third.
Supplied English left deliberately unwrapped (rule 7): "All of", "the particular", "circumstances", "it", "there is", both "that can", "This too", "any case of", "we call", and every article.

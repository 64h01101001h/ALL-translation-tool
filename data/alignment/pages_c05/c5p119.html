AI NOTE — Vasubandhu's byis pa'i tha snyad brgyad, the eight expressions of a child -- a child here being anyone who has not yet seen emptiness directly. The Tibetan names four things (mthong ba / thos pa / rnam par shes pa / rtogs pa -- seen, heard, known, sensed: the Abhidharma set dṛṣṭa / śruta / vijñāta / mata, the same four the root verse at 354 assigns to eye, ear, mind and the remaining senses) and then four statements made about them, twice over with the polarity reversed: about things you did perceive you say ma mthong, "I didn't see it"; about things you did not perceive you say mthong, "I did see it." Four plus four is the brgyad of the heading. The two sides run in opposite directions. The Tibetan puts the objects before the statements said about them and names the set only at the very end; Geshe Michael announces the name first and puts each set of statements before its objects, so brgyad reaches back across the whole sentence and every one of the four bzhi crosses its neighbour. Read the numbered pairs against the Tibetan groups rather than against the count of "four", which matches four-for-four and would still pair them wrongly. Two asymmetries are worth seeing. First, zhes is glossed "say" in the two statement-groups and left unrendered in ma mthong zhes sogs bzhi la, where it names the four categories rather than quoting anyone -- which is how Geshe Michael read it ("another four kinds of things"). Compare 356, which runs the same machinery for the 'phags pa'i tha snyad brgyad, the eight expressions of a realized being, and there writes the object phrase with no zhes at all (mthong ba la, ma mthong ba la). Second, the first list of four is spelled out word by word in the Tibetan while the matching list in the third group is compressed to sogs; Geshe Michael's "hear, know, or sense" there is that one particle unpacked, and it is deliberately left unwrapped -- sogs means "and so on", not those three verbs. rtogs pa -> "sense" is likewise this segment's rendering of a word that normally means "realise, understand": it is the fourth slot of a fixed list, not a general equivalent. Left unwrapped as Geshe Michael's own supply: "Now", "makes mention of", "types", "kinds of things", "really", "another", "which consist of", "I", "it", and the bracketed gloss "[one who has yet to see selflessness directly]" -- whose "see" is the one of the five occurrences in this segment that no mthong claims.

GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Sutra also mentions the "eight expressions of a realized person"--four where you say "I did see it" about things you really did see and so on; four where you say "I didn't see it" about things you really didn't see and such. Regarding these two sets of eight, one may ask for a more detailed description of the basic four: what it is to be "something seen," "something heard," and so on. That which is experienced by consciousness of the eye, consciousness of the ear, consciousness of the thought, and by the other three kinds of consciousness (those of nose, tongue, and body) represents respectively what is seen, what is heard, what is known, and also what is sensed. The fourfold at the end is the point of the passage, and it is corroborated one row earlier. rim pa bzhin du ("respectively") pairs the four experiencers with the four results in Tibetan order: eye → what is seen, ear → what is heard, yid (thought) → what is known, and the nose/tongue/body triad → what is sensed. Both lists are reordered consistently relative to the classical Sanskrit sequence, so the pairing is internally coherent. C05:354 is the root verse this row comments on, and Geshe Michael renders the same Tibetan there the same way — mthong thos rnam par shes pa dang , ,rtogs pa yin te rim bzhin bshad as "Represents respectively what's seen, What's heard, what's known, and also what is sensed" — and again at C05:355 as "see, hear, know, or sense". So rtogs pa → "what is sensed" is his settled rendering of this Abhidharma list across three consecutive rows, not a reading invented here. It remains a context equivalent: the bank's 47 links on rtogs pa are all realize/perceive, and this row must not be harvested as a general gloss for it. "Represents" is left unwrapped. AI NOTE — Root verse from Vasubandhu's Treasure House of Knowledge IV, naming the last five of the ten non-virtues: divisive speech, harsh speech, meaningless speech, coveting, harmful intent and mistaken view. Geshe Michael Roach sets it as capitalised verse lines, so a capital mid-sentence (Afflicted, Harsh, Meaningless, Others, Flattery, Like, Coveting, Wrong, Mistaken, Nor) is LINEATION, not apparatus, and the English crosses the Tibetan on almost every line — eng_order carries the English sequence. Geshe Michael Roach's own prose commentary on this very verse, at C05:358–362, settles several readings. C05:360: “‘Meaningless speech’ really refers to every case of words spoken in an afflicted state of mind… every afflicted type of the spoken word other than those three already mentioned… flattering someone for the purpose of some gain, or else singing a song out of desire”; C05:361: “a show put on by some performer, or too where he began reciting some wrong commentary written by a non-Buddhist”; C05:362: “mistaken view is the idea that neither virtue nor non-virtue even exists.” That commentary is why kyal pa → “Meaningless”, nyid → “really” and lta ba → “idea” are banked here rather than nulled, and it also shows that “too” in “Like too wrong commentaries” is Geshe Michael Roach's own idiom for also, not a typo. The verse abbreviates. nyon mongs in line five is the metrical short form of nyon mongs can (“afflicted”), which this same row spells out in full twice; the link is a verse-line abbreviation, not a lexical equation of the bare noun nyon mongs (“mental affliction”) with an adjective — the same treatment the campaign gave rigs/ris mthun at c5p56. It is the first span to surrender if this page is ever narrowed. The closing correlative. dge dang mi dge med becomes “neither virtue Nor non-virtue even exists”: the conjunction dang takes “Nor” (the landed layer has dang → “nor” four times, three of them in this course under exactly this … dang … med scope, C05:232 and C05:233) and the negative existential med takes the remaining negative, “neither” (C01:366 med → “Neither”, C03:447 med → “Nor”). “even exists” is left UNWRAPPED rather than banked under med, which would invert its polarity. lta ba occurs twice and its two exponents are inverted in the English: the first, the content of the view, is “idea”; the second is the “view” inside log par lta ba → “Mistaken view”, which Geshe Michael Roach states first. Left unwrapped on purpose: “for gain” after “Flattery” (kha gsag is taken at its tighter boundary — the compound does denote flattery aimed at gain, but there is no second Tibetan word in this line to hang “for gain” on); “mentally”, “type”, “too”, “is that which is”, “even exists”, the “for” of “desire for”, and every article. phyir is nulled: “meant to” is the modal of the whole -ba'i phyir construction, and the 312 banked phyir links give it “because”, “for”, “since” or nothing — never a modal. de las is nulled: “every other” rests on the second gzhan.

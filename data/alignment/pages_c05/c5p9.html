AI NOTE —

A one-line source attribution, not a sentence of the text: the wylie is a single ablative noun phrase, slob dpon dbyig gnyen gyis brtsoms pa'i chos mngon pa mdzod las, “from the Treasure House of Knowledge composed by Master Vasubandhu.” GMR builds a full English sentence around it, and most of that sentence has no Tibetan under it: “The verses throughout the commentary below are” is his editorial frame telling the reader what the ablative las governs, and “(350 AD)” is his dating of the author. Both are left unwrapped, as is the article in “the Master.”

slob dpon (ācārya) is the honorific “Master”; dbyig gnyen is the Tibetan calque of Vasubandhu (dbyig wealth/vasu, gnyen kinsman/bandhu), and GMR gives the Sanskrit name rather than the calque. gyis is the agentive marking Vasubandhu as the composer, and “by” is its exponent. brtsoms pa'i is brtsoms “composed, written” plus the nominalizer-genitive pa'i, which turns the verb into the relative clause modifying the title; English carries the relation in the bare participle, so pa'i is nulled. The title chos mngon pa mdzod is the Abhidharmakośa: chos mngon pa (abhidharma, “higher knowledge”) is GMR's “Knowledge” and mdzod (kośa) is “Treasure House”; the English reverses the Tibetan order, so the head mdzod comes first in English. The final las is the ablative of source, “from,” which in English travels to the head of the citation. The Tibetan is agent-first and the English title-first, so the two sides cross; the reading order is given explicitly.

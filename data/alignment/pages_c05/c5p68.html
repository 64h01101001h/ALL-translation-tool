Geshe Michael breaks that single frame into five English predicates and renders ldan pa twice, once as “is covered with” near the front and once as “a place where there are” at the close; one Tibetan word against two candidate exponents, so it is left unlinked under rule 6. The seventeen dang are unlinked on the same ground — the English carries six “and” and two “or”, and no occurrence can be assigned without guessing. The hinges Geshe Michael supplied — “where you live”, “obstacles like”, “it's”, “with”, “a place where there are” — have no Tibetan under them and stay unwrapped (rule 7).

The chain aligns by position because the counts are exact. Segment 203 — the fruits of meaningless talk

TIBETAN (WYLIE)

AI NOTE —

The ripening of tshig kyal ba, meaningless talk, on the outer world. The Tibetan is a bare list of things that are not many (mi mang ba), strung on dang; Geshe Michael reorders it heavily and supplies a good deal of connective English.

Three occurrences of 'chags pa, two of “grow.” The first two are linked; the third, under “never at the right times,” is elided in the English and is nulled rather than forced onto a “grow” that is already spoken for.

Four negations, three treatments. mi before the first 'chags pa is nulled, because the negation lives inside Geshe Michael's “refuse to,” which is not an exponent of mi; the mi of dus su mi 'chags pa takes “never”; the mi of ring du mi gnas pa takes the fused “n't” of “can't” under the negative-affix convention. mi brtan pa is kept whole on “frail,” because brtan pa alone means firm and a split would invert it.

Where the English outruns the Tibetan. The Tibetan names three places — kun dga' ra ba (pleasure grove), nags tshal (wood), rdzing bu (pond) — and qualifies them with nyams dga' ba, delightful. Geshe Michael gives four: “no places to take your leisure, no parks, no glades, no pools of cool water.” “places to take your leisure” and “of cool water” have no separable Tibetan under them and are left unwrapped. Likewise the single predicate mi mang ba, “are not many,” is distributed across the English as the repeated “no,” and the contrastive “right” of “the right times” is his, not the terminative su's.

rgyu is deliberately left unlinked. 'jigs pa'i rgyu mang po dang ldan pa is “possessed of many causes of fear,” and the English renders rgyu as “things.” rgyu is the word for cause; keying it to “things” would pass every mechanical check and still be false, so the mapping is surrendered and only 'jigs pa → “afraid” and mang po → “many” are kept.

AI NOTE — Both analysts walked this one to nearly the same shape; the settled points are these. The Tibetan names FOUR time-periods — lo re re (each year), dus tshigs (season), zla ba (month), zhag re rer (each day) — and Geshe Michael's English names only THREE. lo re re is therefore left with no exponent: "year" appears nowhere in the sentence. dus tshigs takes seasons, not "four seasons": its binding gloss in the dictionary is literally "four seasons", but the Tibetan does not say four, and the tighter span is the honest one. each one of them is deliberately left UNWRAPPED. The tempting link is re re, which the bank glosses "each one" three times over — but lo re re distributes over YEARS, while "each one of them" resumes the good things, so that link would pass every mechanical check and still be false. 'phel ba → more is the thinnest link here and stands only because "more" is in this word's binding gloss list. Everything Geshe Michael supplied to carry the English — Because, then, what others have, you ever manage to find, starts to, with the passing of, every, and all six ands — has no Tibetan under it and is left bare.

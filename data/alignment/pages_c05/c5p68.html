Segment 203 — meaningless speech and fruit

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
tshig kyal ba'i ni, 'bras bu'i ljon shing la 'bras bu mi 'chags pa dang dus min par yang 'bras bu 'chags pa dang dus su mi 'chags pa dang ma smin pa smin par snang ba dang rtsa ba mi brtan pa dang ring du mi gnas pa dang kun dga' ra ba'am nags tshal lam rdzing bu nyams dga' ba mi mang ba dang 'jigs pa'i rgyu mang po dang ldan pa dang
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Because you have talked meaninglessly, fruits refuse to grow on the trees, or they start to grow at the wrong times, never at the right times, and seem ripe when they're still not ripe, or their roots are frail, or they can't stay long; there are no places to take your leisure, no parks, no glades, no pools of cool water, and many things around to make you afraid.

Segment 204 — coveting and declining prosperity

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
brnab sems kyi ni, phun sum tshogs pa thams cad lo re re dang dus tshigs dang zla ba dang zhag re rer yang nyams shing 'bri la 'phel ba ni min pa dang
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Because you have coveted what others have, then each and every good thing you ever manage to find starts to get worse, and less and less, never more, each one of them, with the passing of each of the four seasons, and in every month, and even day by day.

'phel ba / more and min pa / never retain separate sound lexical links. Supplied participants, what others have, ever manage to find, auxiliaries, and seasonal/monthly framing remain unpaired.

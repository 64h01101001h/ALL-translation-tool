Segment 328 — three kinds of killing and the three roots

AI NOTE — The Tibetan sets out three killings by their root — 'dod chags (desire), zhe sdang (anger), gti mug (ignorance) — each under the same frame las skyes pa, 'arisen from', which Geshe Michael renders three times as "that which comes from". Only comes and from are linked; the relative "that which" and the objects "something" / "of things" are his, supplied so the English reads, and nothing in the Tibetan stands under them.

Four things on this line are deliberately left unlinked. thams cad means all; his "the various paths of action" is a loose quantifier, and the layer has 125 links on thams cad without a single "various" among them, so the word is left unwrapped rather than banked as an equivalent. gsum gsum is a reduplication, 'three apiece': it is banked whole against "three", following the one precedent in the layer, and the distributive it carries is what surfaces separately as "each" in "are each brought to completion" — that "each" is left unwrapped rather than assigned to one of the two gsum. The alternative question yin nam min, literally 'is it, or is it not', survives in the English only as "whether", and no one of its three words owns that word, so all three are nulled. And "of all non-virtue" is Geshe Michael identifying which three roots are meant: there is no mi dge ba anywhere in this line.

The Tibetan here writes srog gcod ba where the standard spelling, and the next two segments of this same commentary, read srog gcod pa. The span is cut at srog gcod so nothing turns on it; the spelling is recorded in the errata register.

AI NOTE —

The answer to the challenge put in 328, plus the qualification that saves the sutra. min te is the bare negative copula with a continuative: Geshe Michael expands a one-word Tibetan answer into a whole English sentence, "The answer is that they are not." Only are not is spanned — The answer is that and they are the frame he supplies, and there is no Tibetan under them.

la dgongs so then says what the sutra did mean: only the sbyor ba, the preparatory or "undertaking" stage of a karmic act, which Geshe Michael flags with quotation marks rather than translating away. The sutra's "they come from the three root afflictions" was said of that stage, not of the completed deed.

Several pieces of English are deliberately left unwrapped because nothing in the wylie stands under them: only (there is no kho na and no tsam in the line), the fact that, stages after the quoted term, deeds (the Tibetan says merely srog gcod pa lta bu'i, "of [things] such as killing" — the head noun is supplied), and was made, which renders the passive of gsungs pa but is separated from statement by from sutra and will not be reached by stretching a span.

Both instances of from are real: the wylie carries las twice, ablative each time — out of the sutra, and out of the three root afflictions. The English crosses the Tibetan order twice, once around mdo las gsungs pa ("statement from sutra") and once around rtsa ba gsum las skyes pa ("come from the root three"), so the English order is given explicitly.

Segment 330 — the undertaking stage and the three roots

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
'o na mi dge ba bcu'i sbyor ba rtsa ba gsum las skyes pa ji lta bu zhe na, srog gcod pa'i sbyor ba 'dod chags las skyes pa ni, sha'i phyir du srog gcod pa lta bu'o, , zhe sngang las skyes pa ni dgra bo gsod pa lta bu'o, , gti mug las skyes pa ni bsod nams kyi blos pham sogs gsod pa lta bu'o
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
One may next ask for a description of the process by which the "undertaking" stages of the ten non-virtues come from the three roots of non-virtue. Let's start with the act of killing. The "undertaking" stage of this type of act comes from desire in a case where, for example, you take the life of another being in order to get its flesh to eat. An instance where this stage comes from dislike would be where you kill your enemy. And a case where the undertaking stage comes from being ignorant of things would be where you kill someone like your father or mother out of some meritorious intent.ji lta bu → ∅ (interrogative 'of what sort'; Geshe Michael dissolves it into the supplied frame 'a description of the process by which', no word of which is its exponent)ni → ∅ (topic marker; unrendered)zhe sngang → ∅ (the wylie and the ACIP misspell zhe sdang, 'dislike'; the link is withheld so a form that is not a Tibetan word cannot enter the dictionary layer as a headword — see erratum)ni → ∅ (topic marker; unrendered)ni → ∅ (topic marker; unrendered)kyi → ∅ (genitive; unrendered)pham → ∅ (the wylie and the ACIP read pham where the sense requires pha ma, 'father or mother'; the link is withheld so a lost syllable break cannot enter the dictionary layer as a headword — see erratum)lta bu → ∅ (the third exemplification; Geshe Michael's noun here is 'case', which occurs twice in this English, so no unambiguous exponent can be banked)
AI NOTE — Gendun Drup answers the question the previous section raised: for each of the ten non-virtues, where does the sbyor ba — the "undertaking", the preparatory stage — come from? He answers once, on killing, in three parallel clauses, one per root poison: 'dod chags (desire) → killing for meat; zhe sdang (dislike) → killing an enemy; gti mug (ignorance) → killing one's parents out of a supposedly meritorious motive. All three are built on the same frame, X las skyes pa ni … lta bu'o, which is why las → "from" and skyes pa → "come(s)" recur four times each in lockstep.

Two words are withheld because the Tibetan is corrupt, not because the English is unclear. zhe sngang is not a Tibetan word; the reading required is zhe sdang, which the very next segment (C05:331) spells correctly in this identical three-root frame. pham stands where the sense requires pha ma, "father or mother" — which is exactly what Geshe Michael's English says, and the classical Abhidharma example of killing under ignorance is killing one's aged parents in the belief that it sends them to a better birth. Both are filed as errata and both are nulled here, so that "dislike" and "your father or mother" carry no link at all rather than key a correct English equivalent to a string the dictionary cannot own. Geshe Michael's English stands verbatim on the page.

lta bu'o closes each of the three clauses and he varies the English every time: "for example", "An instance", "a case". The first two are banked on the exemplification noun; the third is nulled because "case" occurs twice in this English and cannot be resolved. srog gcod pa appears twice and is read the two ways he himself gives — "killing" when naming the deed, "take the life" when describing the act, with srog → "life" inside it; gcod pa is left unlinked, because "take" belongs to the English idiom and not to a verb that means "cut". A good deal of supplied English carries no Tibetan and is deliberately left unwrapped: "a description of the process by which", "of non-virtue", all of "Let's start with the act of killing" beyond "killing" itself, "of this type of act", "of another being", "to eat", "of things", "someone", every "your", "would be where" twice, and the THIRD "undertaking stage", which has no sbyor ba under it at all — the Tibetan's last clause is bare gti mug las skyes pa ni. Two sbyor ba, three "undertaking": that asymmetry is the trap on this row.

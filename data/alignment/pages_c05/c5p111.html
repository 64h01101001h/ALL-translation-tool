TIBETAN (WYLIE)

AI NOTE — The Tibetan is one three-part list: the sbyor ba (“undertaking”) stage of ma byin par len pa (taking what is not given = stealing) as it arises from each of the three poisons — 'dod chags (desire), zhe sdang (dislike), gti mug (ignorance) — each limb closed by lta bu / lta bu'o, “such as, for example”. Geshe Michael front-loads each limb as “An example of…” while the Tibetan closes it, so the English crosses the Tibetan in every limb and eng_order carries it.

Much of the English here is scaffolding Geshe Michael supplied with no Tibetan under it, and it is deliberately left unwrapped: “Next let’s consider”, “the act of”, “for this act”, “A case where this stage”, “would be”, “where you”, “for it”, and “is exemplified by” — a second exponent of the single lta bu'o already claimed as “example”. The third limb’s “the ‘undertaking’ stage for stealing” is a restatement from context: that limb of the Tibetan has neither sbyor ba nor ma byin par len pa, so the segment’s single sbyor ba is spanned at its first occurrence only. The “from” of “from your enemy” likewise renders no las — dgra bo'i nor is a genitive, “the enemy’s valuables” — so only the three ablative las are spanned as “from”.

The two analysts split on the dative la of nor la chags pa (“for” vs null) and on the quotative ces pa (“that” vs null). Both are nulled here on the landed bank’s own precedent, counted at merge time: bare la is null in 885 of 1,005 links and reads “for” in 4; ces/zhes (pa) is null in 265 of 312 links and never reads “that”. The closing lta bu'o is spanned whole, as the bank spans other …pa'o forms, and glossed “example” like its two siblings.

Note the orthography skyes ba in the first limb against skyes pa in the other two; after a sa suffix the nominaliser is pa. Quoted as it stands and recorded as an erratum.

Segment 332 — sexual misconduct from each of the three poisons

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
'dod pas log par g-yem pa 'dod chags las skyes pa ni, chags pa'i sgo nas log par 'jug pa lta bu, zhe sdang las skyes pa ni, gzhan sma dbab pa'i phyir du 'khrig pa spyod pa lta bu, gti mug las skyes pa ni, me tog dang , 'bras bu dang , zas g-yos legs par grub pa dang , chu dogs dang , lam dang 'dra bas thams cad kyis bsten par bya'o zhes pa lta bu'o
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Sexual misconduct that comes from desire would be a case where, for example, one engages in some wrong kind of sexual activity because of lustful feelings. Sexual misconduct born from dislike would be represented by engaging in sexual intercourse in order to harm someone else's reputation. Sexual misconduct that you do from ignorance is typified in the statement that "Sex should be enjoyed by everyone together, like the flowers and the fruits of the earth, like a banquet ready for the feast, like a pool in the river, like a public road."ni → ∅ (topic marker, unrendered)ni → ∅ (topic marker, unrendered)skyes pa → ∅ ('arisen from' recast as 'that you do'; the third clause has no verb of arising)ni → ∅ (topic marker, unrendered)'dra bas → ∅ (one comparative particle; the English carries four 'like's and no one of them is its exponent)
AI NOTE — Three parallel definitions, one per root poison, each on the frame X las skyes pa ni … lta bu — “the one born from X is like …”. Geshe Michael varies the frame every time, and the master attests each variant in his own glossary: lta bu is “for example”, then “represented by”, then (as lta bu'o) “typified”; the supplied copulas “would be” and “is” stay unwrapped. skyes pa is “comes”, then “born”, then nothing at all — the third clause reads “Sexual misconduct that you do from ignorance”, which has no verb of arising, so that skyes pa is nulled. sma dbab pa (to defame) takes “reputation” on the authority of Geshe Michael's own glossary (HTG2016, tier glossary); the verb “harm” is his supplement and is left unwrapped, as is the possessive “'s” after gzhan, whose genitive belongs to pa'i. 'dra bas is nulled because the English carries “like” four times, once per simile, and no single one of them is the particle's exponent. The closing quotation is the non-Buddhist licence-view the Abhidharma is reporting, not a position being taught; it lists five things the Tibetan treats as common property — me tog, 'bras bu, zas g-yos legs par grub pa, chu dogs, lam — and the English keeps all five. “Sex”, “together”, “of the earth”, “for the feast”, “public”, the second and third “Sexual misconduct”, and “in some … kind of sexual activity” are all supplied and are left unwrapped. bsten pa is “rely on, resort to, make use of”; “enjoyed” is Geshe Michael's contextual rendering of it here, not a dictionary sense.

Segment 333 — the three mental misdeeds, and the roots of the ten virtues

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
brnab sems sogs yid kyi gsum ni rtsa ba gsum las skyes te, rtsa ba gsum po de'i mjug thogs las byung ba'i phyir, dge ba bcu sbyor ba dang mjug dang dngos gzhi dang bcas pa rnams chags sdang gti mug med las skyes pa yin no
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Coveting and the rest of the three misdeeds of thought come from these three roots of non-virtue because they occur just subsequent to them--to these three roots. The ten virtues, and here we consider them with all their various stages (undertaking and conclusion, as well as actual commission), come from the states of possessing no desire for something, no dislike for a thing, and no ignorance of things.ni → ∅ (topic marker; unrendered)te → ∅ (clause connective; unrendered)po → ∅ (collective enumerator on gsum; unrendered)de'i → ∅ (adnominal demonstrative on rtsa ba gsum po; "them" occurs twice and "these" is unwrapped — not determinable)las → ∅ (ablative absorbed into "subsequent to"; no separate exponent)dang → ∅ (the dang of dang bcas pa; "with" is carried by bcas pa, as at the root verse C05:327)rnams → ∅ (plural; unrendered)med → ∅ (one negation scoping all three roots against three separate English "no" — which one it is is not determinable)yin → ∅ (copula; unrendered)
AI NOTE — Gendun Drup's prose unpacking the root verse at C05:327, which is quoted almost word for word (sbyor ba rtsa ba gsum las skyes, de yi mjug thogs las byung phyir, brnab sems sogs rtsa gsum las skyes, dge ba sbyor dang mjug bcas rnams, chags sdang gti mug med las skyes). That verse is the witness used to settle three readings here. Geshe Michael renders las skyes there as "come from" twice and mjug thogs las byung as "occur just subsequent to" with no "from", so the first and third las carry "from" and the middle one is nulled; and sbyor dang mjug bcas rnams gives "with undertaking and conclusion", so bcas — not the dang in front of it — owns "with". Several things Geshe Michael SUPPLIES have no Tibetan under them and are left unwrapped: misdeeds (the wylie says only yid kyi gsum, "the three of thought"), of non-virtue (no mi dge ba stands anywhere in rtsa ba gsum las; context supplies it from C05:330–332), these at both "these three roots", just, they, and here we consider them, all their various stages, the states of possessing, and the complements for something, for a thing, of things. med is nulled rather than linked: it occurs once and governs all three negations while the English distributes "no" three times, so which one it means is not determinable. de'i is nulled too: it is adnominal to rtsa ba gsum po, so its natural exponent would be the unwrapped "these" rather than the pronoun "them" — the verse's bare de yi is a different construction and does not transfer. mjug thogs takes "subsequent" alone; mjug later in the sentence is the other sense, "conclusion", the third stage of a deed.

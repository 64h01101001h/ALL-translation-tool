AI NOTE — The commentary's prose definition of divisive speech (phra ma), the third of the four non-virtues of speech. It unpacks the root verse quoted just above at C05:357 — phra ma pha rol dbye ba'i phyir, ,nyon mongs can gyi sems kyi tshig — into four conditions on the words: spoken bsams bzhin du (purposely), ma nor bar (without any mistake), out of a nyon mongs pa can gyi sems (mentally afflicted state), and dbye ba'i phyir (in order to split up others who are friends), closing with the condition that the don, the meaning, is actually taken in by the pha rol po, the other person.

The English reorders heavily. tshig ("words") is the fourteenth Tibetan word but surfaces in the second English phrase, and dbye ba ("split up") precedes its own objects pha rol po and mdza' ba in English while following them in Tibetan, so an explicit English order is supplied.

What is deliberately left unwrapped. "consists of", "which are spoken", "they are meant to", "who are", "their", "should be" and the "by the" of "by the other person" are supplied English frame. The Tibetan has no verb of speaking and no copula here, only the topic particle ni (twice) and the purposive phyir, all nulled. The opening gsum pa ni, "the third," is not carried into the English at all.

sems → "state" is the contested link on this page and is taken, not invented. Geshe Michael renders nyon mongs pa can gyi sems as "a mentally afflicted state" here and the same Tibetan as "a mentally Afflicted state" in the root verse at C05:357, so "state" is the head noun he put where sems stands. The alignment bank already records contextual renderings of sems at the dictionary layer — 48 "mind", 4 "state of mind", 3 "Wish", and single instances of "attitude", "consciousness" and "hearts" — and this is a thinner but attested member of that family. It is flagged here because a reader who wants the fuller equivalent should look to the four "state of mind" links, which is the phrase Geshe Michael writes when the English has room for it.

AI NOTE — The commentary's definition of harsh speech, built on the same template as the divisive-speech definition at C05:358 and answering the root verse at C05:357. The frame is shared — bsams bzhin du purposely, ma nor bar without mistake, tshig gi don pha rol pos go ba'o the other person understands what the words mean — and harsh speech fills the open slot with mi snyan pa'i, unpleasant, where divisive speech carries the afflicted-mind clause instead.

Five stretches of the English are Geshe Michael's supplied syntax and carry no Tibetan under them, so they are deliberately left unwrapped: "consists of" (the Tibetan has no copula; the topic particle ni does that work), "which are spoken" (there is no verb of speaking in the wylie), "their" (the genitive gi is nulled rather than made to own an anaphoric possessive), "too" (there is no yang or 'ang in the Tibetan — it is his connective back to C05:358), and "should be … by the".

Two points of Tibetan mechanics. un: mi takes the negative prefix of "unpleasant" and snyan pa the positive stem, the campaign's negative-affix convention, the shape already landed at C05:340. The agentive. pha rol pos is spanned whole with its fused agentive per rule 9, and the "by" it licenses is left outside: across the landed bank, of 159 fused agentive words standing in a segment where a "by" was available, 11 take it into the span and all eleven are instrumental idioms (dbang gis "by force of", ngo bo nyid kyis "by nature"). Not one banks "by" with a personal agent.

The English crosses the Tibetan twice: mi snyan pa'i tshig stands after bsams bzhin du ma nor bar in the wylie but before "purposely" in the English, and the agent pha rol pos stands before its verb in the wylie but after "understood" in the English.

AI NOTE — Gendun Drup defining ngag kyal pa, meaningless speech, then reporting the wider definition the other schools hold. Both sentences are DEFINITIONS that run backwards in the English. The Tibetan predicates nyon mongs pa can gyi tshig kun ngag kyal pa nyid yin no — ‘all afflicted speech IS precisely meaningless speech’. Geshe Michael turns the definition around and fronts the term being defined, so the English order crosses the Tibetan hard and eng_order carries it. The copula yin is nulled at both places: “refers to” is the inverted frame, not an exponent of yin.

The emphatic nyid → “really” / “actually”. Kept on an in-course witness, not on feel. The root verse this commentary unpacks stands at C05:357: nyon mongs can kun kyal pa nyid is rendered “Meaningless, really every afflicted”. Four Tibetan units, four English units — kyal pa → ‘Meaningless’, kun → ‘every’, nyon mongs can → ‘afflicted’ — and the one residual on each side is nyid → ‘really’. The two sentences here close … nyid yin and both put an adverb immediately before “refers to”, so they are read together: either both stand or both fall.

glu len pa taken as the collocation. len pa on its own is ‘take, take up’ and the layer records nothing else for it; ‘singing’ is the light-verb idiom’s, so the compound carries “singing a song” and len pa is nulled inside it, with glu → ‘song’ beside it.

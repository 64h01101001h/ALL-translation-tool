AI NOTE — Two of the four conditions, named for the arising of eye consciousness: bag chags, the mental seed, is the rgyu'i rkyen (the causal condition), and the mig gi dbang po, the physical eye faculty, is the bdag po'i rkyen (the dominant condition). Note that rkyen is rendered twice in two different ways — factor in the first compound, circumstance in the second: the unit, not the morpheme, is what Geshe Michael is translating. For the second term he does not gloss but explains — the component of the body that provides a contributing circumstance — and there is no lus (body) in the Tibetan, so only the term itself is linked and the component of the body that provides stays unwrapped as his supplied frame. Both occurrences of behind are likewise hinges he supplied over a genitive. mig shes kyi in the second clause has no exponent in the English at all. The identical Tibetan sentence — byte-for-byte — appears at ILL:2244 under a fuller rendering: "the deep seed which acts as the cause for the arisal of the visual consciousness is the causal factor for it; whereas the sense power of the eye is the dominant factor."

Segment 212 — what the direct cause of eye consciousness really means

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
'dir mig dbang mig shes kyi dngos rgyur bstan pa ni, mig shes skye ba'i bag chags smin pa'i skabs la dgongs kyi mig dbang gang yin la min no
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Here when the physical faculty of the eye is presented as the direct cause of the consciousness of the eye, the real intent is to refer to the situation where the mental seed which produces the eye consciousness is ripening. They are not talking here of what we normally think of as the physical faculty of the eye.kyi — genitive linking mig shes to dngos rgyur; nulled. AI NOTE — Gendun Drup is correcting a reading. When the root text calls the eye faculty the direct cause (dngos rgyu) of eye consciousness, what it has in mind is the moment the mental seed ripens — not the eye faculty as ordinarily conceived. The sentence turns on kyi after dgongs, the adversative semi-final particle ('… is what is meant, not …'), which Geshe Michael renders by starting a second English sentence (“They are not talking here…”); the particle itself has no separable exponent and is nulled.

skye ba is deliberately left unlinked. mig shes skye ba'i bag chags is 'the seed FOR THE ARISING of the eye consciousness' — the consciousness is the thing that arises. Geshe Michael's “the mental seed which produces the eye consciousness” makes the seed the agent instead, so “produces” is the converse of skye ba, not its exponent, and is not banked (rule 10). Across 102 banked skye ba links there is no causative reading at all (lives 25, develop 10, birth 9, null 10).

An independent witness. ILL:2245 carries this wylie byte-for-byte under a genuinely different English — “when they describe here the sense power of the eye as the direct cause of the consciousness of the eye, what they have in mind is the point at which the deep seed for the visual consciousness ripens; it is not a reference to the power of the eye itself.” It corroborates dgongs → intent (“have in mind”), skabs → situation (“the point at which”), smin pa → ripening (“ripens”) and min → not — and it renders skye ba'i as a bare “for”, with no producing verb anywhere, which is the reading this page banks.

Rule 7 leavings. “when”, “as”, “where”, “real”, “is to refer to”, “are … talking here of” and “we normally think of as” are Geshe Michael's supplied English frame with no Tibetan under them; the Tibetan says only bstan pa ni … la dgongs kyi … gang yin la min no. They stay unwrapped. mig dbang and mig shes each occur twice in the Tibetan, and mig shes takes two different English strings (“consciousness of the eye”, then “eye consciousness”), so the English order is given explicitly.

Segment 213 — the Middle and Extremes verse, and foundation consciousness

TIBETAN (WYLIE)

GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Regarding this the text called Middle and Extremes states, The combination of the objects and the person, The base consciousness, is a consciousness that appears; In reality this is not the being. Here the word "objects" refers to form and the rest, and "the person" refers to the five faculties; the lines are describing a consciousness that arises and which appears to be them, but which is actually foundation consciousness. Master Stiramati also explains the physical faculties as the object of foundation consciousness.

AI NOTE —

The glass-of-water argument, put in an opponent's mouth: yang kha cig … zer na, "someone else might claim." A pleasure being, a human and a craving spirit look at one glass at the same moment, each sees something different, and the opponent concludes that each of the three appearances is true — bden four times over, once for the principle and once for each of the three beings.

The English inverts the Tibetan noun phrase. brlan zhing gsher ba chu phor pa gang la is "at a glass full of wet and flowing water": the wet-and-flowing sits in front of the water in Tibetan. Geshe Michael moves it to the back as an appositive gloss, "a glass filled with water: the thing that we define as 'wet and flowing,'" so those four content words cross. The second occurrence of the same phrase keeps the Tibetan order and does not.

ngo bor is nulled all four times, and that refusal is the main thing this page records. Geshe Michael renders 'gro ba rang rang gi ngo bor "to the perceptions of each different type of being" and the three X kyi/'i ngo bor "to the eyes of X". Both are good English for the construction; neither is an equivalent for the word. The landed bank carries ngo bo and ngo bor twenty-seven times — essence, nature, basic nature, essential nature, embodiment — and not once as a faculty of perception; "eyes" belongs to mig. Four links are given up rather than put a false headword into the dictionary layer.

Left unwrapped as supplied English: "might make the following", "Suppose", "sit down", "the thing that we define as", "different type of", every "to the eyes of", and "else" in "Someone else" — the bank gives yang even / as well / also / moreover, never "else".

cig car → "together" is the span to challenge first. The word is "all at once, simultaneously", about the single moment rather than about company, and lhan cig is the ordinary word for "together". It is kept because the bank already renders cig car (du) with "come together" twice and "at once" twice, because "sit down" is Geshe Michael's own supply with no Tibetan under it, and because the independent parallel at C18:1121 renders the same wylie "sit down together and look upon a glass". The terminative du is split off and nulled so the banked word is the bare adverb.

chur and bdud rtsir are banked whole against the bare nouns, on the bank's own precedent (chur → "water" at C05:251); the terminative's force sits outside the span. thad ka thad kar is nulled — there is no word in this English for it. Nothing here is binding until a human reviews it.

Segment 257 — one glass, three beings, water and not water

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de'i tshe na 'gro ba gsum po de'i mig shes thams cad tshad ma yin nam, 'ga' zhig tshad ma yin pa dag las gang yin, dang po ltar na brlan zhing gsher ba chu phor pa gang po de rnag khrag la sogs pa'i dngos po gsum ga yin pa dang , 'gal ba la ma 'khrul ba'i tshad ma yod pa dang , phor pa gang po de chu yin pa dang chu ma yin pa gnyis kar tshad mas rtogs par thal, de'i tshe na 'gro ba gsum po de'i mig shes thams cad tshad ma yin pa'i phyir
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
We ask you then a question: in the situation you've just described, is it that the visual consciousness of all three beings are a valid perception, or is it that only one or two of them are a valid perception? Suppose you say all three are valid. Well then, the glass of wet and flowing water must be full of something that is all three different things: pus and blood, and each of the others. And then too it must be possible for there to be multiple and yet still valid perceptions which see one thing in two completely incompatible ways. And finally there must be such a thing as a valid perception which correctly perceives that the glass is filled with something which is simultaneously water and yet not water. Why so? AI NOTE — A consequence (thal) argument inside the three-beings / one-glass debate: if the visual consciousness of the pleasure being, the human and the craving spirit are all valid, then the one full glass is pus and blood and water at once, a valid perception is unmistaken about contradictories, and a valid perception sees the glass as water and not water together.

The same three syllables, two Englishes. de'i tshe na opens the segment as "in the situation you've just described" and closes it as "according to your view". The two analysts split on this — one banked the first and nulled the second as a loose frame. The master settles it: Geshe Michael's own glossary for the headword de'i tshe na carries both strings, along with "in this situation" and "suppose". The phrase is a discourse hinge in debate, not a time expression, and this row shows it doing the job twice.

gnyis kar → "simultaneously", banked after a refusal. One analyst refused this under rule 10 on the ground that gnyis ka means "both" and "simultaneously" is a temporal word placed on a quantifier — having checked only the inflected headword gnyis kar, whose gloss is the bare "at both". The uninflected headword gnyis ka lists "simultaneous", "simultaneously", "both ... at the same time" and "both ... together": the both-at-once rendering is Geshe Michael's own, not this page's invention. The span is kept whole as gnyis kar rather than cut back to gnyis ka, because kar is ka plus the terminative fused into one syllable and rule 8 forbids breaking it.

Tightened against over-capture. 'ga' zhig takes "one or two", not "only one or two of them" — both are glossary entries, but "only" is a supplied restrictive and the "of them" ranges over the beings while the Tibetan partitive dag las ranges over the two alternatives. That referent mismatch is why las is nulled rather than given "of them". gsum is preferred to gsum po and rtogs to rtogs par: the tighter span, each cut at a syllable boundary. "correctly" is left unwrapped — rtogs pa already means correct cognition, so the adverb is Geshe Michael unpacking the word rather than a second exponent.

Nulled rather than banked. ma 'khrul ba ("unmistaken") is dissolved into "which see one thing in two completely incompatible ways" and keeps no exponent; "valid" in "multiple and yet still valid perceptions" belongs to tshad ma, which glosses "valid perceptions" exactly. dang po ("the first [alternative]") is nulled because Geshe Michael writes the alternative out instead of naming it, so "all three are valid" is left unwrapped. thal, gang yin and all seven copulas are nulled or omitted: seven yin against three "are" admits no honest one-to-one.

One displacement, recorded. thams cad modifies mig shes in the Tibetan, but Geshe Michael's first "all" sits on "three beings". The word is still the quantifier's exponent; it has moved one constituent, and the second thams cad ("would all have to be") sits where the Tibetan puts it.

Variant reading, not a defect. C15:402 and P9:45 carry this Tibetan byte-identically with a longer English — "the glass of something wet and flowing, of water" against this row's "the glass of wet and flowing water". C16:833 is byte-identical to this row in both columns and is the same file re-ingested, not an independent witness. Both readings are grammatical and complete, so this is a variant between course editions and nothing is filed. The longer reading separates chu out as its own "of water", which independently corroborates the three-way split banked here: brlan "wet", gsher ba "flowing", chu "water" — each of the three an exact glossary equivalent.

Segment 258 — three substances clash, and water excludes non-water

GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)

AI NOTE — A debate consequence. 'dod na mi 'thad par thal is the standard shape — "if you accept that, it follows that it is untenable" — which Geshe Michael compresses to "And if you try to agree to these absurdities, you are wrong". "you try to" and "to these absurdities" are Geshe Michael's, with nothing under them, and are left unwrapped. Two reasons follow, joined by dang. The first ends in phyir, moved to the front of the clause as "for" (hence the eng_order crossing); the second phyir gets no "because" at all, because Geshe Michael sets that reason out as its own sentence opening "Moreover". The English says "the quality of being" four times and the Tibetan has one copula, so the frame is Geshe Michael's and yin is nulled rather than pinned to the one "being" that happens to sit near it; the paired min keeps only the visible "not", which is the bank's commonest reading for it. phan tshun spangs 'gal — the mutually-exclusive dichotomy that admits no third possibility — is nulled: "in such a way that, if something exists and lacks one of these qualities, it must then possess the other" is the term's definition, and banking a definition as a dictionary equivalent is what rule 10 forbids. gsum po is nulled because there is no "three" in this English: the three substances are pus-and-blood, water and ambrosia (see 256–257) and Geshe Michael counts from the other end, "the other two". The two contradictions are kept apart: 'gal ba is the plain incompatibility asserted of the three substances, and dngos 'gal is the technical direct contradiction, banked as "directly incompatible" with dngos → "directly" and 'gal → "incompatible", exactly as C05:243 already banks it.

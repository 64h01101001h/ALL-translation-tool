Segment 232 — the rope labelled a snake

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
'grel pa der chags sogs rnams thag pa la sbrul du btags pa ltar gsungs pa ni mtshon pa tsam ste, chos gzhan thams cad kyang thag pa la sbrul du btags pa ltar rtog pas bzhag par 'chad pa'o, , de la khra bo'i mdog dang 'khyil lugs sbrul dang 'dra zhing , yul mi gsal bar snang ba na thag pa la 'di sbrul lo snyam pa 'byung ngo , , de'i tshe thag pa la thag pa'i tshogs pa dang cha shas sbrul gyi mtshan gzhir 'jog rgyu chung zad kyang med pas, de'i sbrul ni rtog pas btags pa tsam mo
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
When this commentary states that "desire and such" are like a piece of rope labelled "snake," it is only giving a single example; what it means to say is that each and every other existing object is as well like a rope called a snake: they are all established through the process of conceptualization. Here the colored pattern of the rope and the way it's coiled make it resemble a snake; and if you're in a place where you can't see it very clearly, then you start to think to yourself, "It's a snake!" The fact though is that there is nothing about the rope as a whole, nor anything about its various parts, that you could ever establish as being one kind of a snake; the snake of the rope, then, is nothing but a creation of conceptions.rnams → ∅ (plural; unrendered)ni → ∅ (topic marker; unrendered)dang → ∅ (complement marker of 'dra "resemble"; unrendered)thag pa → ∅ (the rope is not named again in Geshe Michael's "It's a snake!"; the five Tibetan thag pa and the five English "rope" do not pair)'di → ∅ (the exponent is "It" in "It's a snake!", but cutting "It" out of the contraction breaks a word, and rule 8 exempts only the four negative affixes)de'i tshe → ∅ ("at that time"; unrendered)thag pa → ∅ (topic-marked "as for the rope"; this clause's one English "rope" is claimed by thag pa'i tshogs pa)gyi → ∅ (genitive; unrendered)mtshan gzhir → ∅ (the technical mtshan gzhi, "illustration / instance of a definition"; Geshe Michael's "one kind of" is a loose recast and is left unwrapped — all nine landed d=5 mtshan gzhi links in the bank read example / typical example / definitive example, never "kind")rgyu → ∅ (nominaliser, "a thing to be posited"; unrendered)chung zad → ∅ ("the slightest"; folded into "nothing" / "ever")kyang → ∅ (concessive "even"; folded into "nothing")pas → ∅ (causal; unrendered)de'i → ∅ (bare genitive demonstrative, "of that"; Geshe Michael resolves it as "of the rope", which supplies a noun the Tibetan does not have here)ni → ∅ (topic marker; the "then," is a discourse hinge, not its exponent)
AI NOTE — Gendun Drup on the rope-and-snake example. The commentary's chags sogs "desire and such" is only an illustration: every other existing object (chos gzhan thams cad) is equally a rope called a snake. The trap on this segment is a counting trap — five thag pa in the wylie and five "rope" in the English, and they do NOT pair. Geshe Michael supplies "of the rope" at "the colored pattern of the rope" (the Tibetan clause de la khra bo'i mdog dang 'khyil lugs names no rope) and again at "the snake of the rope" (the Tibetan is the bare genitive de'i), and he drops the rope entirely at "It's a snake!" (thag pa la 'di sbrul lo). Three thag pa carry an English "rope"; two are nulled and two English "rope" are left unwrapped. mtshan gzhir is nulled rather than banked as "one kind of": mtshan gzhi is the technical illustration/instance of a definition, and every landed link for it in the alignment bank reads example. Inside khra bo'i mdog the two members cross — the master dictionary carries khra bo = "pattern" and mdog = "colored", which is the reverse of the English word order, so the members are assigned from the dictionary and not from the surface order.

Segment 233 — no basis in the heaps for "me"

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de bzhin du phung po la brten nas nga'o snyam pa 'byung ba na, phung po'i steng nas snga phyi'i rgyun gyi tshogs pa dang , dus gcig pa'i tshogs pa dang de'i cha shas de'i mtshan gzhir 'jog rgyu cung zad kyang med de rgyas par 'og nas 'chad de
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
The same thing happens when you get the idea of "me" about the heaps, the various components to yourself. There is nothing about these components as a whole, when you consider them as a continuum in time, nor as a whole considered in a single moment in time, nor as the various parts to a whole, that you could ever establish as being any kind of "me." We'll go into this in more detail further on.la — dative-locative; Geshe Michael's supplied "about" carries it, and "about" occurs twice against one labrten nas — "in dependence on"; no exponent in the Englishna — temporal/conditional; "when" occurs twice against one na, unresolvablesteng nas — "from atop, on the basis of"; rendered by the supplied "about"gyi — genitive; unrenderedde'i — "of that"; Geshe Michael's "to a whole" paraphrases it, but "whole" is claimed twice already for tshogs pade'i — "of that"; unrenderedmtshan gzhi — "basis of designation, illustration"; dissolved into "as being any kind of", no isolable exponentrgyu — nominaliser, "anything to"; unrenderedcung zad — "even a little"; absorbed into "nothing"kyang — "even"; absorbed into "nothing"de — continuative; unrendered
AI NOTE —

The Tibetan runs de bzhin du … nga'o snyam pa 'byung ba na — “likewise, when the thought ‘I’ arises in dependence on the heaps” — and Geshe Michael turns that subordinate clause into a main one. “happens” is his hinge and is left unwrapped; the verb 'byung ba lands on “get”. “the various components to yourself” is his in-line gloss of phung po and has no second Tibetan behind it, so it too stays bare.

The second sentence lists three candidates for a mtshan gzhi, a basis the label “me” could actually refer to: snga phyi'i rgyun gyi tshogs pa, the collection taken as an earlier-and-later continuum; dus gcig pa'i tshogs pa, the collection taken at a single moment; and de'i cha shas, the parts of that collection. Geshe Michael's English says “whole” three times against two occurrences of tshogs pa, which looks unresolvable until the third item is read: “the various parts to a whole” renders de'i cha shas, where there is no tshogs pa at all. A second published rendering of this same Tibetan puts “the collection” at the first two and “the individual parts themselves” at the third, which is the same division. So the two tshogs pa take the first two “whole”s and the third is left unwrapped.

mtshan gzhir 'jog, “to posit as the basis of designation”, is spread across “establish as being any kind of ‘me’”; only 'jog → “establish” is claimed, and mtshan gzhir is nulled rather than attached to a “me” the Tibetan never writes. cung zad kyang med is “there is not even a little”; the negation is banked on med alone, since the English fuses all three words into “nothing”.

Segment 234 — the "me" is a creation of conception

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de'i phyir dang phung po'i cha dang cha can las ngo bo tha dad pa'i de'i gzhir 'dzin rgyu yang cung zad kyang med pas, nga de ni rtog pas phung po la brten nas bzhag pa tsam yin gyi, rang gi ngo bos grub pa med do
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Because of this fact, and since moreover there is not the slightest thing outside of the parts or whole of the components to yourself that you could ever consider any kind of "me," this "me" is nothing more than a creation of conception, based on the components. AI NOTE — The first half is one long causal clause: phung po'i cha dang cha can las ngo bo tha dad pa'i de'i gzhir 'dzin rgyu yang cung zad kyang med pas — “because there is not even the slightest cause for holding, as the basis of that [me], anything different in essence from the parts or the whole of the components.” Geshe Michael compresses “different in essence from” into the single preposition outside of. Both analysts independently gave that preposition to the ablative las and nulled ngo bo and tha dad pa'i, and the bank agrees: bare las at the particle layer is already linked to “from” 70 times and “than” 20 times, while tha dad pa / tha dad pa'i are banked only as “separate” and “distinct”, so hanging “outside of” on the adjective would have minted a looser new equation. rgyu (cause) is nulled: the English “thing” stands in its slot but is not that word. de'i is nulled too — the first quoted “me,” may be its referent, but that is a judgement about where the demonstrative was parked, not a lexical fact, and neither analyst could point at it. bzhag pa is literally “posited, set in place”; creation is Geshe Michael's wording here and is recorded as his, not as a general equivalent of the verb. Two other published renderings of this identical Tibetan exist: C16:811 reads “a creation of projection”, keeping bzhag pa as “creation” and varying rtog pas; ILL:1114 renders the whole slot “a label applied … by our thoughts”. Both are revisions of the English, not defects in any of the three.

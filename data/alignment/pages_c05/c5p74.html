Segment 221 — long-delayed karma, and the permanence problem

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
las dang de'i 'bras bu'i bar du yun ring por chod pa'i dge mi dge'i las las kyang , bde ba dang sdug bsngal sogs kyi 'bras bu 'byung ba ni rang gi sde pa gong 'og thams cad 'dod la, de la las de 'bras bu 'byin pa'i snga logs bar du gnas na ni rtag par 'gyur la, rtag pas don byed par mi nus pas las las 'bras bu 'byung ba'i 'brel pa mi 'thad do
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
All the Buddhist schools, whether higher schools or lower schools, accept the principle that the consequences of pleasure and pain and so on arise from virtuous and non-virtuous karma or actions, even though the original karma and its eventual consequences may be separated by a very long period of time. The problem though is that, if the karma stays around during the entire period up to the point at which it gives its consequence, it would have to be unchanging. An unchanging thing though is incapable of affecting anything, and so you could never have a relationship where karma gave rise to any consequence.bar du (the interval BETWEEN karma and its result) — Geshe Michael's "separated by" carries it; no separate exponenttopic marker; unrenderedsnga logs (the moment just prior) — absorbed into Geshe Michael's "the point at which"; no separable exponenttopic marker; unrendered'gyur (become) — "would have to be" is the English modal frame, not an exponent of the change-verb (C05:170 ruling)ablative (from karma) — Geshe Michael recast it as "karma gave rise to" and wrote no "from" here'thad (is tenable) — Geshe Michael turns the negated verb into "you could never have"; the negation is linked, the verb has no exponentterminative; unrendered

Segment 222 — the objection: a finished deed and its result

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
las de byas pa'i skad cig gnyis par zhig na ni, mtshams de nas 'bras bu dngos su 'byin pa'i snga logs kyi bar du las de med la, las zhig pa yang dngos por med pas las las 'bras bu ji ltar 'byung bar 'gyur zhes brtsad pa'i lan du
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
From the moment after you complete a karma or deed, that deed is finished and gone. During the entire period from that point up to the point at which the consequence actually occurs, the deed no longer exists. A deed which is already gone is no longer a thing that can have any affect on anything. How then does a deed or karma ever produce any kind of consequence? Here is how we explain this problem:pa'i → ∅ (nominaliser + genitive; Geshe Michael makes the verb finite, 'you complete')gnyis par → ∅ (the ordinal 'second'; the English hinge 'after' is not the ordinal)na → ∅ (temporal/conditional; the apodosis is made a main clause)ni → ∅ (topic marker; unrendered)pa'i → ∅ (nominaliser + genitive; unrendered)snga logs → ∅ (the side just before; Geshe Michael writes 'up to the point at which the consequence actually occurs' and drops the 'just before')kyi → ∅ (genitive; unrendered)du → ∅ (terminative; 'During the entire period' is Geshe Michael's expansion of the same bar du and is left unwrapped)de → ∅ (demonstrative; the English here reads 'the deed', and a bare article is not a span head)la → ∅ (connective; unrendered)yang → ∅ (moreover / even; unrendered)pas → ∅ (causal 'because'; stated flat in the English)las → ∅ (ablative 'from karma'; the deed is recast as the subject and no 'from' is written)'gyur → ∅ (auxiliary 'come to be'; unrendered)zhes → ∅ (quotative closing the objection; unrendered)lan → ∅ (the noun 'answer'; Geshe Michael recasts 'in answer to' as 'Here is how we explain', and 'explain' is not what lan means)
AI NOTE — The objection Vasubandhu's commentary now answers: a deed perishes in the very next instant, so during the whole interval before its result appears there is no deed left to give one — and what has perished is not a functioning thing (dngos po) and so can do nothing. TWO VERBS CHANGE VOICE in Geshe Michael's English, in opposite directions, and each is still the only finite verb of its clause on both sides, so each is linked verb-to-verb and flagged here: 'byin (transitive, to give forth) becomes the intransitive “occurs,” and 'byung (intransitive, to arise) becomes the transitive “produce.” Three words are deliberately left unlinked. gnyis par (second) — the ordinal's force surfaces only in the hinge “after,” and gnyis par does not mean “after.” snga logs (the side just before) — Geshe Michael writes “up to the point at which the consequence actually occurs,” where the Tibetan says up to the point just before it does. lan (answer) — “Here is how we explain this problem” carries the sense of “in answer to,” but lan is a noun and “explain” is not its equivalent. Of snga logs kyi bar du only bar is claimed, for “up to”; Geshe Michael's fuller “During the entire period” renders the same bar du and is left unwrapped rather than double-claimed.

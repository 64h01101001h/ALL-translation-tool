Segment 153 — unshifting deeds and the shifting levels of concentration

TIBETAN (WYLIE)

GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
"Isn't it contradictory," one may object, "for you to describe deeds of the upper realms as unshifting, when those of the third and lower levels of concentration are explained as shifting types?" There is no contradiction. The levels of concentration from the third on down are explained as "shifting" or "affected" only with reference to their being affected by the various problems that prevent one from perfecting single-pointed concentration. The point in calling deeds of the upper realms "unshifting" is in reference, rather, to the fact that they do not shift direction when they ripen.demonstrative; unrenderedcopula; absorbed into GMR's recast questionconditional; unrenderednominalizer + terminative; unrendered (spelled par for bar, E filed)comitative governed by 'gal; unrenderedconnective; unrenderednominalizer + terminative; unrenderedtopic marker; unrenderedgenitive; unrenderedinstrumental; the 'by' belongs to GMR's passive frameobject marker on dgongs; the 'to' belongs to GMR's 'with reference to' idiomobject marker on dgongs; the 'to' belongs to GMR's 'in reference ... to' idiomnominalizer + genitive; unrenderedreason particle; GMR recast the reason clause as a statement

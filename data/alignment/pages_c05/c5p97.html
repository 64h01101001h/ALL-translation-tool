AI NOTE — An a fortiori argument, and its hinge is a clause Geshe Michael renders TWICE. The Tibetan yi dvags la ji ltar snang ba ltar gyi phyi rol don du grub pa'i rnag is one relative clause — ‘pus established as an outer object just as it appears to the craving spirit’ — and he unfolds it as “pus that existed as it appeared to exist to the craving spirit; that is, which existed as an outer object.” Only the second half is spanned: that is the half whose words line up with the Tibetan, and both analysts independently claimed it. The first “that existed” is his gloss of the same clause and stays unwrapped.
The correlative ji ltar … ltar has one English exponent, “as”, and it is banked on ltar, not on ji ltar: the layer carries six ltar→“as”/“As” links (and thirteen →“like”) against forty-five ji ltar links with not one on “as”. ji ltar is nulled.
“by Master Vinitadeva” has no Tibetan under it. The wylie says only lung de'i, ‘that quotation’; the attribution is supplied from the surrounding passage and is left bare. So is the closing frame zhes bstan pa'i phyir (‘because what is shown is that …’), which he does not translate in place — and the “for” in his last sentence introduces the karma clause, a different attachment, so phyir is nulled rather than keyed to it.
med owns the whole negative existential “didn't exist”; the 'ga' yang … med idiom (‘not even one’) splits with 'ga' on “single” and yang nulled. sogs owns only the closing gesture “and so on”, by the standing C04/C05 convention. GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
And this is certainly the case, for later on in the text someone argues that, if the pus and blood did not exist as outer objects, and if they were only a part of the mind itself, then they could never provide the function of being something to eat or drink. And in response, Master Vinitadeva says "Actions and their objects are like an injury in a dream." He is saying that, even though the pus and blood do not exist as outer objects, nonetheless they can perform the function of being something to eat or drink.

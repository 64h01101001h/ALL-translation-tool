Segment 348 — the first four non-virtues defined

AI NOTE — A root verse of the Treasure House of Knowledge (the commentary that follows numbers it [iv.289–96]), defining the first four of the ten non-virtues. Geshe Michael sets the four definitions as verse lines, so the mid-sentence capitals (Purposely, Another’s, Engaging, Are) are lineation, not sentence starts, and his English reorders every line: the Tibetan puts the qualifiers first and the verb last, the English puts the verb first. Roughly two fifths of the English is connective tissue he supplied — is, being, to, by means of, kinds of sex, when the, one's, Are, a wrong, given — and is deliberately left unwrapped. THE COMMENTARY IS THE WITNESS FOR THE HARD LINES. C05:351 unpacks line 5 as bgrod par bya ba min par, 'gro bar 'dod pa'i sgo nas and renders it “engaging in kinds of sex which are improper to perform”, which is why improper is attached to bgrod min and Engaging to 'gro ba; the master's glossary-tier entry bgrod = [journey, travel, engaging] was weighed and set aside, since its third sense is almost certainly read off this very English line. THREE WORDS ARE LEFT UNCLAIMED on rule 10. gzhan in the last line is “other”, not “wrong”: C05:353 reads 'du shes gzhan du bsgyur, an impression shifted into a different one, and “wrong” is Geshe Michael's reading of the situation rather than of the word. sgyur ba is change or turn-into, not “given”. 'dod pa, the wish that drives the act, has no exponent here at all. The spelling gthu in line 4 is quoted verbatim and filed as an erratum: it is not a legal Tibetan syllable, and the commentary on this same verse at C05:351 reads mthu.

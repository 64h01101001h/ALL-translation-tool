AI NOTE — A question-and-answer pair from the commentary: how many projecting deeds ('phen pa — the karma that throws a whole new birth) each realm and each type of being can carry. The Tibetan sets the inventory; a good deal of Geshe Michael's English is his own connective tissue and is deliberately left unwrapped: One may ask (his frame for the interrogative gang … du), every different, types of, produced by, only … that are possible, Here there can be, bring one a result, for any deed, and the closing in the hells.

Two links surrendered under rule 10. (1) 'on kyang is the concessive “however / though”, and the word standing over it in this row is printed through — a misprint for though, registered as an erratum. The bank carries 'on kyang → “though” twice and “through” never. A link to the misprint would pass every mechanical check and still be false, so the span is left with no English. (2) The closing de na is the bare anaphor “there”; Geshe Michael resolves it to in the hells. de is a demonstrative, not the word for hell, so that second in the hells stays unwrapped.

mthong chos is the technical compound for the present, visible life (Skt. dṛṣṭadharma): karma mthong chos la myong 'gyur is karma whose result is felt in this same lifetime. The layer already carries this compound as “this very life” (C05:170). Here Geshe Michael unpacks it rather than naming it, so the compound holds the whole of sees in the very same life, with mthong → “sees” as its member. chos is given no English of its own — the “life” sense is the compound's, not the syllable's, and chos is among the most loaded headwords in the lexicon.

The Tibetan also reads yod cin, where the connective after a d suffix is cing; cin, occurs exactly once in the whole corpus against 453 cing,. The span quotes it as printed.

Segment 173 — the stable child who has overcome desire for a level

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
sa brgyad po gang las 'dod chags dang bral ba brtan pa'i byis pa, 'dod chags dang bral ba'i sa der skyes nas myong 'gyur gyi las mi byed de, de phyi ma la 'dod chags dang bral ba'i sa der mi skye ba'i phyir
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Next let's consider a child [non-Arya] who is stable and has overcome his desire for any one or number of the eight different levels. He does no deeds of the type that will lead to an experience there after being reborn at a level for which he has overcome desire. AI NOTE — Vasubandhu IV on the karma a being free of desire does not do. The Tibetan is three clauses: the subject (a byis pa, an ordinary being who is nevertheless brtan pa, stable in having shed desire for one or more of the sa brgyad po — the eight levels, the four concentrations and the four formless attainments); the thesis (myong 'gyur gyi las mi byed — he performs no karma to be experienced there); and the reason (phyir — because he will not be reborn there next life. 'dod chags dang bral ba occurs three times and is one idiom each time — “free of desire” — but Geshe Michael varies the surface: “has overcome his desire”, “he has overcome desire”, “he’s lost desire”. Only the two content words are claimed ('dod chags=desire, bral ba=overcome / overcome / lost); the supplied “his”, “he’s” and the relative hinge “for which” carry no Tibetan. myong 'gyur gyi las is the technical term, karma to be experienced: myong takes “experience” and 'gyur only the future “will”, while “of the type that”, “lead to an” and “there” are periphrasis and stay unwrapped. Both der are nulled: the locative is what “at a” renders, and the second sa der gets no “there” at all. phyi ma is claimed as “next” only — “life” has no Tibetan under it. Also left unwrapped as Geshe Michael’s supply: “Next let’s consider”, “[non-Arya]”, “who is”, “one or number of”, “different”, “take”, “the fact is that”, and every article.

AI NOTE — Reconciled from a tibetan-first and an english-first proposal. The Tibetan says once what Geshe Michael says twice. ma zad (“not only…”) lets the Tibetan elide the predicate of its first limb — only des skyes nas myong 'gyur du stands there — and the single myong 'gyur gyi las mi byed at the end governs both limbs. Geshe Michael writes “perform any deed which will lead to an experience at such a level” out in full twice. las→“deed” and byed→“perform” are therefore banked on the SECOND occurrence, the clause the Tibetan verb actually sits in, and the first English clause is left unwrapped rather than doubled. myong is the one word that genuinely stands twice and takes both “experience”s.
Deixis left unbanked. des is agentive, yet the slot wants a place: C05:173 has 'dod chags dang bral ba'i sa der skyes nas, C05:175 'dod rtser skyes nas, and the root verse at C05:171 byis pa der skyes. Geshe Michael's “after being reborn there” renders a locative, and 'phags pas already carries the agentive in the same clause. Rather than bank des = “there” and carry a probable misspelling into the lexicon, the particle is nulled and the reading is filed as an erratum. The closing de is nulled too: it is the subject of mi skye, and “This is because” frames the causal clause from outside it.
Left unwrapped (rule 7): “Next let's consider”, “for any one or number of”, “at such a level” ×2, “which will lead to” ×2, “life beyond the next”, “This is”, “again”, “take a” — all Geshe Michael's supplied English or expansions with no Tibetan under them.

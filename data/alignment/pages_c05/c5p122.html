Segment 364 — why the ten are called paths of action

TIBETAN (WYLIE)

GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
One may ask why the expression "path of action" is used in reference to "them"--to the group of ten just listed. The name derives first of all from the fact that the three involved with thought are paths by which the "action" or deed of mental movement is expressed. Moreover, the seven involved with body and speech are action or deeds themselves, as well as paths through which mental movement is expressed. Thus they are all "paths of action." AI NOTE — Vasubandhu asks, through Gendun Drup's commentary, why the group of ten gets the name las kyi lam, “paths of action.” The answer is a split: the three of thought are only paths, along which sems pa (mental movement) travels, while the seven of body and speech are las (deeds) in their own right and such paths — that is the correlative kyang … yang. The root verse this comments on is C05:363, “In them, Three are paths, seven deeds as well.”

Where Geshe Michael's English is his own. “The name derives first of all from the fact that”, “just listed”, “involved with” (twice, resting on the genitives), “or deed”, “action or”, “themselves”, “Moreover” and “Thus they are all” are supplied and carry no Tibetan under them. In particular the quoted “action” in the second sentence has no las under it: the las in yid kyi gsum las is the elative particle, the same construction as de dag las at C05:377, where Geshe Michael likewise renders nothing for it.

Segment 365 — citation header, je tsongkapa's lam rim chen mo

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
rje tsong kha pa mchog gis brtsoms pa'i lam rim chen mo las
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
The following selections are from the Great Book on the Steps of the Path, composed by Je Tsongkapa (1357-1419).honorific raising the name; no exponent in the English, exactly as at the landed C05:242nominaliser plus genitive linking the composing to the title; unrendered
AI NOTE — A one-line citation header opening a reading from the Lam rim chen mo. The Tibetan is one nominalised phrase — rje tsong kha pa mchog gis brtsoms pa'i lam rim chen mo las — and Geshe Michael's English reorders it completely, so an explicit English order is supplied.

Three stretches of the English have no Tibetan under them and are deliberately left unwrapped: the frame The following selections are, the noun Book, and the dates (1357-1419). chen mo is the feminine "great" agreeing with an unstated head noun (bstan bcos or the like); it is spanned as "Great" alone, not "Great Book".

The shape follows two landed siblings rather than taste. C05:242 is the same construction word for word — ... mchog gis brtsoms pa'i ... nas, "composed by Master Kedrup Tenpa Dargye" — and it banks mchog null, gis as "by", brtsoms as "composed", pa'i null and the ablative as "from". C05:190 is this very header with a different honorific (rjes ... chen pos, "Lord Tsongkapa") and it banks the title as lam rim "Steps of the Path" with lam / rim as members and chen mo "Great". The older C03:23 and C03:118 give chen mo "the Great Book" and lam rim "on the Steps of the Path", but there the Tibetan is only lam rim chen mo las with nothing else to carry the English; the tighter C05 reading is preferred here.

rje takes "Je": Geshe Michael transliterates it rather than translating it on this line, the way he transliterates tsong kha pa. The bank's usual reading for rje is "Lord" (27 links), which is what the parallel at C05:190 says, but this row's English says "Je" and the English is quoted as it stands.

Here is the first. One may ask, "How do you define the act of killing?" The Abbreviation says that five parts are involved: the object, the conception, the thinking, the bad thought, and the conclusion.

Segment 342 — the objects of the third and fourth divisions

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
cha gsum pa'i gzhi ni ming dang gzugs yin te, ming gzugs kyis bsdus pa'i dge mi dge med par lta ba las byung ba'i phyir, cha bzhi pa'i gzhi ni ming yin te, rdzun tshig sogs ming la brten nas 'byung ba'i phyir
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
The objects of the third division are names and forms, for the non-virtue in this case arises from a view about the virtues and non-virtues that are all part of names and forms in general. The objects of the last division are names, for lying and so forth all occur--ultimately--based on the names of things. 75 Some Ethical Questionsni — topic marker; unrenderedte — semi-final connective; unrenderedkyis — agentive; there is no "by" in the Englishmed — "there is no"; the negation has no exponent in Geshe Michael's English (erratum filed)par — adverbial/terminative; unrenderedbzhi pa — "fourth"; Geshe Michael writes "last", true of the fourth of four but not the word's equivalentni — topic marker; unrenderedte — semi-final connective; unrenderedla — object marker; the "on" of "based on" is inside the brten nas span
AI NOTE — This row closes the list of four divisions opened at C05:340 and continued at 341. Two readings of gzhi are on the table and the course settles it: the master's binding hgm_gloss for gzhi is the plainly broken ["give it a name"], so the pair rests on Geshe Michael's own English in the immediately surrounding rows — C05:339 "The objects consist of living beings", C05:340 "the objects for each of the four divisions", C05:341 "have enjoyments as their object". cha → "division" is carried by the master ("division", "divisions") and by C05:340/341 ("the first division", "the second division"); it is kept as a bare word rather than folded into cha gsum pa / cha bzhi pa, because those are phrases, not dictionary units.

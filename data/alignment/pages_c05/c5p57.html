AI NOTE — The Exemplists (dpe ston pa, Sanskrit dārṣṭāntika) are named here as a faction within the Sutrist school — mdo sde pa'i sde pa, literally “a group of the Sutrists.” mdo sde pa carries “Sutrist” only; “school” is left unwrapped, because the separate Tibetan word for a faction, sde pa, stands right beside it and carries “group.”

The four mu (“combinations” — the standard tetralemma frame) are built from two variables, ripening (rnam smin) and timing (gnas skabs), each either definite (nges pa) or not (ma nges pa). Geshe Michael renders the same nges pa six times as “definite,” and the negation ma surfaces as “no” in the first combination and “not” in the second.

In the fourth combination “neither” is left unlinked, and so is gnyis ka beside it. “Neither” is one English word carrying both the quantifier (gnyis ka, “both”) and the negation (ma); handing it to either Tibetan word alone would assert something the word does not say. The same construction one segment earlier, at C05:168 (de gnyis ka ma nges pa → “those where neither ripening itself nor its timing is definite”), was banked the same way, with both gnyis ka and ma nges pa unlinked.

Two other things in the English have no Tibetan under them. All four occurrences of “deeds” are supplied: there is no las anywhere in this row. And in the second combination “should ripen” has no Tibetan under it — gnas skabs nges la says only that the time is definite, and Geshe Michael supplies the verb the English sentence needs. The one rnam smin in that clause is linked to “to ripen at all,” the ripening that is not definite. “Possible,” “which hold true in this regard,” “They say there are,” “at all” and “respect/respects” are likewise Geshe Michael's expansion and stay unwrapped.

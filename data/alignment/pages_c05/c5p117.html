Segment 350 — mistaken identity, and taking one's own life

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
ma nor bar zhes pas lha sbyin lta bu gsod pa'i sbyor ba byas nas, gzhan mchod sbyin dang nor nas mchod sbyin bsad kyang las lam gyi dngos gzhi mi 'byung bar bstan, gzhan zhes pas rang bsad pa la las lam gyi dngos gzhi mi 'byung bar bstan to
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
The words "without a mistake" are meant to indicate that no "actual- commission" stage of an act occurs in a case of mistaken identity; for example, in a case where you undertake to kill John but instead kill Joe because you have mistaken him for John. The word "another," finally, is meant to indicate that no "actual-commission" stage occurs either in an instance where one takes his own life.zhes → ∅ (quotative marking the lemma being glossed)pas → ∅ (agentive on the nominalised quote; "The words" is Geshe Michael's frame)pa'i → ∅ (nominaliser + genitive)byas → ∅ (light verb; "undertake" already carries sbyor ba byas)nas → ∅ (converb)gzhan → ∅ (apposed to mchod sbyin; Geshe Michael inverts the confusion and leaves it unrendered)mchod sbyin → ∅ (first of two; the English names Joe only once)dang → ∅ (comitative with nor)nas → ∅ (converb)kyang → ∅ (concessive over the whole clause; Geshe Michael's "but" joins a different pair)las lam → ∅ (the PATH of an act; "an act" is an abbreviation, left unwrapped)gyi → ∅ (genitive)bar → ∅ (terminative under bstan)zhes → ∅ (quotative marking the lemma being glossed)pas → ∅ (agentive on the nominalised quote)bsad pa → ∅ ("takes his own life" is an English idiom with no separable exponent for the killing verb)la → ∅ (locative of the case)las lam → ∅ (not rendered in the second sentence)gyi → ∅ (genitive)bar → ∅ (terminative under bstan)to → ∅ (final particle)
AI NOTE — Gendun Drup closes out the root verse's qualifiers: ma nor bar ("without a mistake") excludes the mistaken-identity case, and gzhan ("another") excludes suicide. Geshe Michael restructures the sentence, stating the conclusion first and then re-telling the Tibetan concretely after "for example", which is why the English says mistaken twice over a single nor. The link is placed on the second one, inside the concrete clause that carries the Tibetan's own participants; the abstract "in a case of mistaken identity" is his summary and is left unwrapped.

The names are a substitution, not a translation. lha sbyin is Devadatta and mchod sbyin is Yajnadatta — the standard Indian pair of example-names — and throughout the ACI courses Geshe Michael substitutes John and Joe for them. C05:369 pairs them the same way in this same reading ("Then you mistake Joe for John, and kill Joe"), and the bank already carries lhas byin → "Devadatta" at C01:222 and C05:92. What is banked here is that rendering, and the direction matters: John is lha sbyin and Joe is mchod sbyin. mchod sbyin is spanned twice and linked once, because the English names Joe only where he is killed.

las lam is nulled at both places. It is the path of an act; the bank glosses it "paths of action" seven times and "path of action" once in ten links, and never "act". "an act" here is Geshe Michael's compression, and "of an act" is left unwrapped rather than banked as an equivalent. kyang is nulled for the same kind of reason: its concession runs from the killing to the non-arising of the actual-commission stage, while the English "but" contrasts killing John with killing Joe — a different joint in the same sentence. bsad pa is nulled in the last clause because "takes his own life" is an English idiom with no separable exponent for the verb; only rang → "own" is claimed there.

The term dngos gzhi appears in the English twice in two spellings, "actual- commission" and "actual-commission", and both are quoted exactly as the corpus carries them. The spaced form is a hyphenated line break that survived digitisation; it is recorded in the errata register and never corrected on the page.

Segment 351 — stealing and sexual misconduct defined

AI NOTE —

Two definitions from Gendun Drup's commentary on the Treasure House: ma byin par len pa, stealing, and log par g-yem pa, sexual misconduct. The root verse they gloss stands three rows back at C05:348, and Geshe Michael glosses three of this formula's own words by name at C05:349 and C05:350 — “The word ‘purposely’” for bsams bzhin du, “The words ‘without a mistake’” for ma nor bar, “The word ‘another’” for gzhan. Those are his own identifications, not ours.

The English stops short of the Tibetan. The tail 'gro bar 'dod pa'i sgo nas 'dod pas (‘by way of wanting to go to her, out of desire’) and rnam pa bzhi yod de (‘and there are four kinds’) have no exponent in this sentence. The count arrives in the next row, C05:352, “This sexual misconduct is of four different types”. Those morphemes are nulled rather than attached to a nearby phrase.

“Kinds” here is Geshe Michael's, not rnam pa. At C05:348 he writes “Sexual misconduct, of four types, Engaging in improper kinds of sex”, where rnam pa bzhi is “of four types” and “kinds” is supplied over bgrod min. The same hand supplies the same word here, so rnam pa is not keyed to it.

“Superior” has no owner and is left unwrapped. The Tibetan is mthu dbang cha. Two independent witnesses carry the same formula with mthu alone and no dbang cha — C05:348 (gthu dang 'jab bus, so spelled there) and TCS02:72 (gathu dang 'jab bus), both rendered “by means of force or stealth”. So mthu is “force” on evidence outside this row, while the cut between mthu dbang and cha, or mthu and dbang cha, is not asserted in either direction and “superior” is keyed to nothing. 'jab bus → “stealth” and bdag gir byed → “take possession” come from the same two witnesses.

min → the “im” of “improper” is the registered negative-affix convention. “Proper to perform” is left unwrapped: bgrod pa is a verb of approach, and at TCS17:841 Geshe Michael renders the same gerundive bgrod par bya ba ma yin pa as “someone with whom it would not normally be proper to engage in such relations” — a phrase-level recast, with no word in it that is bgrod's own English. “Engaging in” is unwrapped for the same reason.

Segment 277 — the numerists on the form in between

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
der thal, chos kyi grags pa'i lung don ni, grangs can gyis rgyang ring thung gi gzugs la bltas pa de'i tshe na, rgyang ring thung gi gzugs dang , mig shes gnyis kyi bar du, las gzugs gsal mi gsal skye ba'i dbang gis, gzugs gsal bar snang mi snang 'jog pa yin gyi, gzugs kyi rnam pa shar ba'i sgo nas gzugs gsal bar snang mi snang 'jog pa ma yin zhes khas blangs pa la, de lta bu'i las gzugs gnyis po des rgyang ring thung gi gzugs gnyis po de bsgribs sam ma bsgribs, bsgribs na mig shes kyis gzugs gnyis po de ma mthong bar thal, des de sgrib pa'i phyir
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
This is a fact, for the actual meaning of Master Dharmakirti's statement is as follows. The Numerist School is describing a situation where you are looking at a physical form either from far away, or from up close. They say that, depending on the distance between you and the object, another physical form which is the result of karma, and which stands between your visual consciousness and the original form, is either clear or not. This then determines whether the original form appears to you distinctly or not. It is not the case though, they say, that what determines whether the original form appears clearly or not is whether or not you have a clear impression of this form. In reply then Master Dharmakirti is asking the Numerists: Let's consider these two cases of some intermediate physical form that comes from karma. Do they, or do they not, function to obscure the two original forms, the one at a distance, and the other close by? AI NOTE — A consequence (der thal … phyir) put to the Numerists (grangs can, Sāṃkhya) in Dharmakirti's name. Two things a reader should know about this page. (1) gzugs stands nine times in the Wylie, and Geshe Michael varies it. Where he writes “physical form” both words are taken (w8, w17, w37); where he writes “the original form” only “form” is taken (w12, w21, w25, w29), because “original” is his own disambiguator — it tells you which form is meant, it does not translate gzugs. The bank agrees: of 40 banked gzugs links, 23 read “form” and 4 “physical form”, and none reads “original form”. (2) Geshe Michael renders the phrase rgyang ring thung gi gzugs dang , mig shes gnyis kyi bar du twice — loosely as “the distance between you and the object”, then literally as “which stands between your visual consciousness and the original form”. Only the literal rendering is spanned. bar du is linked to the SECOND “between” because that is the one whose two relata are the Tibetan's own (gzugs and mig shes); the first “between” joins “you” and “the object”, which are Geshe Michael's supply and stand under no Tibetan here. Left deliberately unwrapped as Geshe Michael's supplied prose: both occurrences of “Master”, the second “Dharmakirti” (the name is repeated with no Tibetan under it), “the distance between you and the object”, the whole frame “In reply then … Let's consider”, “cases”, every “original”, “function to”, “which is the result of”, “that comes from”, and “you are”. The agentive particles follow the standing C05 practice: gyis, kyis and the first des are nulled because no “by” governs the agent they mark, while the final des is linked to the “by” of “obscured by the others”, which does — and “the others” is left unwrapped, since it renders no word in this Tibetan.

Segment 278 — suppose they do not obscure: seeing all four at once

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
ma bsgribs na, mig shes des las gzugs gnyis dang rgyang ring thung gi gzugs gnyis po de cig car du mthong bar thal, des de ma bsgribs pa'i phyir zhes bkag pa'i don yin pa'i phyir
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
And suppose you say that they do not obscure them. Wouldn't your visual consciousness then see both the two intermediate forms created by karma, and the two original forms, the near one and the far one, all at the same time? AI NOTE —

A consequence (thal) thrown at the opponent, in the standard debate shape … mthong bar thal, … pa'i phyir — consequence, then reason. If the karma-made forms standing between the eye and its object do not obscure, the eye consciousness would have to see all four forms at once: the two made by karma and the two ordinary ones, the far and the near.

Geshe Michael splits the consequence into a rhetorical question and its answer, so the question carries mthong and “They would have to” carries thal; “because” renders the first phyir, and the closing yin pa'i phyir, the reason-marker of the whole proof, has no second “because” and is left null.

Much of this English is supplied and is deliberately left unwrapped: intermediate (twice), original (twice), your, according to you, the Master's statement, wherein he, and the whole of that belief of the Numerists have no word under them in this Tibetan. Geshe Michael is naming, from the surrounding argument at segments 275–277, what the bare demonstratives des and de merely point at — which is why all four of them are nulled here rather than mapped to the noun phrases sitting unclaimed beside them.

The Tibetan names the far form before the near one (rgyang ring thung, an ellipsis for rgyang ring / rgyang thung); the English reverses them, which is what eng_order records.

Segment 279 — four things we never asserted, and one we do

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
gnyis pa der thal, phyogs snga mas brlan zhing gsher ba phor pa gang la, 'gro ba gsum gyis cig car du bltas pa de'i tshe na, brlan zhing gsher ba phor pa gang po de, dngos po gsum gyi gzhi mthun du yang khas ma blangs, skyes bu gcig gi gzugs mdzes mi mdzes gnyis ka yin par yang khas ma blangs, yi dvags kyis mig shes dang chu klung gi bar du rnag khrag gi las gzugs skye bar khas ma blangs, yi dvags kyi mig gis rnag khrag gi las gzugs dang chu klung gnyis ka mthong bar yang khas ma blangs, las kyi dbang gis yi dvags kyi chu klung ma mthong bar khas blangs pa'i phyir
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Our second point [that you have failed to comprehend the position we expressed above] is also quite true. Our original position was describing a situation where three different kinds of beings were sitting together and looking at a glass full of something wet and flowing. It is not our position that the glass full of something wet and flowing is one thing that is three different things. And it is not our position that there is such a thing as the physical appearance of a person which is at once both attractive and ugly. It is furthermore not our position that the blood and pus represent some kind of physical form which results from karma and grows up between the visual consciousness of the craving spirit and the stream of water. And it is not our position that the craving spirit's eyes see both this blood and pus as some kind of physical form resulting from karma, and the river of water at the same time. AI NOTE — Five clauses built on one verb, khas len / khas blangs, "to take as one's position." Four of them deny (khas ma blangs) and the fifth affirms (khas blangs). Geshe Michael recasts the verb as a nominal frame — "It is (not) our position that ..." — and the two analysts split over how much of that frame to bank.

What is linked, and what is not. In the four denials the negation ma stands between khas and blangs, so the verb is not contiguous and cannot be quoted whole; only ma is linked, to Geshe Michael's "not," five times against his five "not"s, the fifth being the ma of ma mthong. "our position" is left unwrapped in all four: there is no pronoun in the Tibetan, and "our" is Geshe Michael's supplied subject. In the closing clause khas blangs does stand contiguous, and is linked to the single word "position" — not to "our position."

yang. Three of the four denials carry yang and the third does not — yet it is the third that Geshe Michael marks, with "It is furthermore not our position." Because the emphatic in the English does not sit where the emphatic in the Tibetan sits, all three yang are nulled rather than matched to "furthermore" or to "And."

las gzugs is linked as two words, las → "karma" and gzugs → "physical form," rather than as one compound carrying "physical form which results from karma." The relative clause is Geshe Michael's way of spelling out the relation the Tibetan leaves implicit, and it stays unwrapped.

rnag khrag is pus (rnag) plus blood (khrag), and Geshe Michael writes it the other way round here. The compound is linked whole and the two members are linked inside it in the order his English puts them, which is how the same compound was handled at C05:247, 258, 259 and 269.

chu klung appears three times and is rendered "stream of water" twice and "river of water" once; each is linked to the wording that stands over it.

Not linked. gzhi mthun ("common locus") is nulled: "one thing that is three different things" unpacks the term instead of translating it, and harvesting that kind of unpacking is exactly how the dictionary acquired its present gloss for this headword. der thal and pa'i phyir, the consequence frame, are nulled — "is also quite true" is the concession the marker makes, not an exponent of it. de'i tshe na is nulled because what stands over it is "a situation where." gcig, yin par and every dang / zhing are nulled: their only candidates are supplied articles and an "and" that occurs eleven times in this English.

yi dvags kyis mig shes. The agentive kyis here does the work of a genitive — Geshe Michael reads "the visual consciousness of the craving spirit," and the parallel clause two lines later spells it yi dvags kyi mig gis. C15:424 and P7:28 carry the same passage and both read kyis, so this is the published reading and no erratum is filed.

AI NOTE — The four-realms glass, one segment on from its twin. The opening clause brlan zhing gsher ba phor pa gang la … cig car du bltas pa de'i tshe na is the stock example already landed at C05:297, and this page follows that page's cuts exactly — brlan → “wet”, gsher ba → “flowing”, phor pa → “glass”, gang → “full”, cig car → “together”, bltas pa → “look”, la → “at”. Only the cast changes: 297 had 'gro ba gsum, “three kinds of beings”; here Vasubandhu names them, lha mi yi dvags dud 'gro sogs.

snang ba'i snang ba is two spans, not one. The phrase is “the appearance that appears as X”. Both analysts cut it the same way independently: the genitive-bearing member is the appearing-as, which Geshe Michael writes “looked like”, and the head noun is the one he turns into a verb, “appeared” (“appearing” in the hells sentence, where his English order crosses the Tibetan). The genitive 'i is left outside both spans — his “that” is English relative syntax.

The tsam is deliberately unlinked. “just looked like” occurs FIVE times in this row and only one of the five has a tsam under it. A frame used four times without the particle is the translator's cadence, not the particle's exponent, so the mechanically available link to “just” is refused here under Rule 10.

tshad mar song ba four times, rendered three ways — “genuine” twice, then “valid perception” and “valid perceptions”. All four are banked, because the neighbouring page C05:294, on this same argument, already carries tshad mar song ba → “genuine” and → “genuinely so”.

Left unwrapped. “And consider again”, “According to you”, the “it would have to be” frames, “towards something”, “Finally”, “this person” and the whole question “Why would this all have to be so?” are Geshe Michael's supplied scaffolding; the Tibetan under them is the bare consequence particle thal, the existentials yod, and the debate particle gang zhig, all nulled here. dngos gnas pa is split after C05:294: dngos → “actual”, gnas pa nulled, “objects” left standing on its own.

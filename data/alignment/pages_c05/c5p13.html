Master Purnavardhana presents the eight chapters of the Treasure House as follows: Eight chapters were written to elucidate in detail what is indicated only briefly by the opening words of the texts: "All phenomena, stained or without stain." the first two of the chapters are devoted to a general treatment of stained and unstained phenomena. A detailed presentation of stained phenomena is left to the next three chapters. The third, for example, relates (1) who it is that is so very afflicted, (2) where they live so very afflicted, and (3) how they are so very afflicted. It does so with respective presentations on (1) the five types of beings in the three realms--the world of living beings; (2) the external world--the "vessel" which holds these beings, and (3) the four modes of birth and twelve links of dependent origination. AI NOTE —

The tail of a table of contents for the Treasure House: three chapters are announced (gnas phyi ma gsum) and then taken one at a time. GMR reorders heavily — the Tibetan puts each chapter number LAST in its unit (… ston pa bnas drug pa, … byed pa gnas bdun pa, … bstan pa gnas brgyad pa) while the English fronts it (“Chapter Six covers…”), and the second sutra citation is quoted BEFORE ye shes rten brten pa'i in the Tibetan but after “a base for wisdom to rely on” in the English.

gnas does double duty: the word for a chapter of the text (four times) and, in the Chapter Six line, the ordinary word for a place (gang du rnam par byang ba'i gnas, “where it is, the place, that he is purified”). The three interrogatives gang / gang du / ji ltar are the skeleton of that line and carry only “who” / “where” / “how”; the “it is … that” frames are GMR's scaffolding and stay unwrapped, as do “phenomena”, “the person” in the Chapter Seven sentence (no gang zag there), “concerns”, “that which provides”, “is something” and “again”.

dbang du mdzad nas (“taking X as the focus”) occurs twice and is nulled both times; GMR carries it in the hinge verbs. gang gis (“by what”) is nulled rather than mapped to “what”, which occurs three times in the English. The closing bshad do zhes 'chad do (“so it is explained”) is unrendered.

Segment 319 — desire-realm virtue: the white-and-black deed

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
'dod par gtogs pa'i dge ba ni dkar gnag la rnam par smin pa yang dkar gnag pa'i las yin te, rang gi ngo bo nyon mongs can min pas dkar, rgyud gcig la nyon mongs pa dang 'dres pas gnag, 'bras bu yid du 'ong ba 'byin pas rnam smin dkar, rgyud gcig la sdug bsngal dang 'dres pas rnam smin gnag pa'i phyir
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Virtue taken in by the realm of desire represents a type of deed which is both white and black, and which also ripens into something both white and black. Since its basic nature is free of affliction it is white but--since within a single mental stream it is also mixed with afflicted things--it is black as well. It ripens into something white because it gives forth a pleasant result but--since within the particular mental stream this is also mixed with suffering--we can also say it ripens into something black.ni → ∅ (topic marker; unrendered)la → ∅ (la-don joining the deed to its two qualities; unrendered)yin → ∅ (copula; Geshe Michael's "represents" is his own verb and is left unwrapped)te → ∅ (clause connective; unrendered)can → ∅ (possessive "having"; no separable exponent)min → ∅ (negative copula; "free of" is a positive recast, not banked — rule 10)gcig → ∅ ("one, single"; the second rendering is "the particular", which the landed bank has never yet banked on gcig — withheld under rule 10)phyir → ∅ (closes the whole reason chain; the English has no closing "because")
AI NOTE — This is the third of the four deeds of the Treasure House's karma chapter: the white-and-black deed (dkar gnag pa'i las), identified here with virtue taken in by the desire realm. The Tibetan names the pair twice — once of the deed, once of its ripening — and Geshe Michael keeps both. Four reasons follow, each closed by pas: white because its own essence is unafflicted; black because within one mental stream it is mixed with afflicted things; its ripening white because it gives forth a pleasing result; its ripening black because within that same stream it is mixed with suffering. All four pas are linked whole per rule 9 (Since / since / because / since), as are the two locative la ("within") and the two comitative dang ("with"): each one visibly carries its relation in the English.

dkar gnag is banked as a compound. It is a headword in Geshe Michael's own glossary with the equivalent "white and black", so the "and" is what the juxtaposition means rather than English he supplied; dkar → "white" and gnag → "black" sit inside it as members. Where the two words stand alone later in the sentence they are linked flat. gnag (not nag) is this commentary's own orthography — 26 rows of the corpus use it, and both spellings are glossary headwords — not a spelling defect.

Three things deliberately not banked. yin → ∅: it is the copula, and "represents" is Geshe Michael's own verb. min → ∅: the negation is real, but "free of" is a positive recast, and every one of the 46 landed links on min renders it with a negative word. The second gcig → ∅: the identical rgyud gcig la is "a single mental stream" the first time and "the particular mental stream" the second, and while Geshe Michael's glossary does list "particular" among his equivalents for gcig, none of the 89 links already in the bank render it that way — so the loose second rendering is left unclaimed and the first, "single", carries the word.

Left unwrapped as Geshe Michael's supplied English: "the realm of" (the Tibetan is 'dod pa alone, with no khams — in the bank "realm" belongs to khams), "a type of", "by", "both" ×2, "but" ×2, "into something" ×3, "it is" ×3, "this", "as well", and "we can also say".

AI NOTE — Unstained deeds (zag med kyi las) — the fourth of the four kinds of deed at [iv.237–40], after the black, the white and the mixed of 317–319.
Three negations, one "never". mi dkar mi gnag rnam par mi smin negates three times — "not white, not black, does not ripen". Geshe Michael negates once, in "never ripen into anything either white or black", and folds the two colours into the ripening, the frame he uses at C05:318 and 319 ("ripens into something white"). Only the mi that governs the verb smin is linked to "never"; the other two are nulled rather than made to share one English word. The positive stems dkar and gnag keep "white" and "black", which are true of them at the word level even though the Tibetan predicates non-whiteness of the DEED while the English predicates whiteness of the RIPENING — a clause-level link would not survive that shift, which is one reason this page stays flat. rnam par of rnam par smin pa has no separate exponent.
The second sentence is restructured, and the root verse settles it. "Their nature is to be what brings..." has no Tibetan for "Their nature" — the wylie is simply ... byed pa yin no — so "Their nature" and "to be what" are left unwrapped and only the copula yin takes "is". zad par byed pa is split because its English is discontinuous: zad takes "end", byed pa takes "brings", and the supplied "to an" between them is unwrapped. The root verse this passage is commenting on, at C05:316, reads de zad byed pa zag med yin and Geshe Michael renders it "The unstained is what brings it to an end" — the same distribution, word for word, from his own hand: zag med → unstained, yin → is, byed pa → brings, de → it, zad → end. He renders the same phrase "act to bring ... to an end" at C05:321, 322 and 323.
Left unwrapped as supplied English: "which are", "into anything either ... or", "Their nature", "to be what", "that is, the ... kind of deed", "to an". Segment 321 — the root verse on ending black and white deeds

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
chos bzod 'dod chags bral ba yi, ,bar chad med lam brgyad kyi gang , ,sems pa rnam pa bcu gnyis de, ,nag po zad par byed pa'i las, ,dgu pa'i sems pa gang yin pa, ,de ni dkar nag zad byed yin, ,dkar po bsam gtan chags bral ba'i, ,bar chad med lam skyes tha mas
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Twelve types of mental movement had with Phenomena, mastery, and eight of the Uninterrupted path free of desire Are deeds that act to bring the black to an end. That which is the mental movement in the Ninth acts to end the white and black. White by the final uninterrupted arising, Free of desire for the concentration.yi — genitive tying the first item to what follows; unrenderedkyi — genitive; a second genitive stands in the same sentence, so neither is given the relationgang — relative "whichever"; no exponent in this verse linede — demonstrative closing the verse line; unrenderedpar — terminative on zad; the "to an" of "to an end" is English grammar, left unwrappedni — topic marker; unrenderedyin — copula; Geshe Michael recasts the line as "acts to end", with no separate "is"lam — "path"; elided in this verse line, supplied in the prose at C05:324
AI NOTE — Root verse of the Treasure House of Knowledge (Abhidharmakosha IV) on which deeds bring black and white karma to an end, in double-shad verse lineation. Geshe Michael decodes it himself in the prose that follows, and those three rows settle almost every reading here. chos bzod is dharma-forbearance, and C05:322 names it: “the four instants known as ‘the mastery of phenomena’” — which is why the verse's compressed “Phenomena, mastery” is kept whole as the compound and split inside it into chos = Phenomena and bzod = mastery, rather than flattened into two list items. Twelve = four chos bzod instants plus eight uninterrupted paths, as C05:322 spells out. de at the head of line 6 is the correlative that answers gang yin pa, and C05:323 writes the same construction out in prose: “that type which is the mental movement in the ninth instant” — so “That” belongs to de and gang yin pa takes only “which is”. The last two English lines run in the reverse of the Tibetan order: line 7 supplies “White…Free of desire for the concentration” while line 8 supplies “by the final uninterrupted arising”, and C05:324 gives the same sentence as prose. lam is nulled in line 8 because the verse writes only “uninterrupted arising” there; the prose at C05:324 restores “path”. The instrumental “by” of “White by the final” and the “to an” of “to an end” are left unwrapped.

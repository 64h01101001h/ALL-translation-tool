AI NOTE — “It is not though the case that” is Geshe Michael's fixed cleft, not a word-for-word rendering. He writes the identical string at C04:162, where the Tibetan has neither an opening de nor an opening yang — and the landed page for that segment cuts ma yin → “not” and leaves “It is … though the case” bare. This page follows it: de and yang are nulled and only ma yin → not is claimed, even though the sentence-initial “It” and “though” would both pass every mechanical check.

Three more places where the English carries less Tibetan than it looks. (1) The SECOND cha shas gsum ldan is compressed to “they”: there is no second “three parts” in the English, and the second ldan is a converse besides — the Tibetan has the glass endowed with the parts, the English has the parts staying with the glass. All three words are nulled. (2) “the glass of water” in the second and last clauses is supplied; the Tibetan there has only de'i, “its”. The Tibetan names pus before blood; Geshe Michael reverses them to “blood and pus”. The two spans cross, which is why this pair is worth clicking: rnag is banked as “pus” seven times out of seven elsewhere, and khrag as “blood” six out of six.

One debate move in the standard three-part consequence (thal 'gyur) shape: the concession 'dod na "suppose you agree", the consequence … med par thal, and the reason 'dod pa de'i phyir. Twenty syllables of Tibetan against three English sentences, because Geshe Michael unpacks the debate shorthand into speech.

Most of the first English sentence has no Tibetan under it. 'dod na says only "suppose you agree"; what is agreed — "that it is only a matter of better or worse misperceptions" — is Geshe Michael restating from C05:310 what the opponent has just conceded, and is left unwrapped rather than hung on words that do not say it.

thal stands last in its Tibetan clause and is rendered at the front of the English as "Are you saying then that", the byte-identical rendering already banked for thal six rows earlier in this same argument at C05:305. The single med negates both conjuncts at once, and Geshe Michael distributes it into two identical English phrases. The intensifier "even" is Geshe Michael's, sitting inside the predicate; the same phrase with the same supplied "even" is already banked for med at C05:292 ("not even exist").

AI NOTE — Gendun Drup's prose restating the root verse at C05:321, and the phrase-by-phrase correspondences are fixed by that verse: chos bzod is rendered there as “Phenomena, mastery”, bar chad med lam as “Uninterrupted path”, 'dod chags bral ba as “free of desire”, sems pa as “mental movement”. Geshe Michael breaks the one Tibetan sentence into three English ones, so the resumptive de of rnam pa bcu gnyis po de surfaces as the “These” that opens his third sentence, and the English crosses the Tibetan at nearly every clause. Three things are deliberately not banked. (1) zhi: the wylie reads chos bzod pa zhi and the ACIP CHOS BZOD PA ZHI, where the arithmetic of the sentence (four plus the eight uninterrupted paths is the rnam pa bcu gnyis) and Geshe Michael's “the four instants” both require bzhi. A pair zhi → “four” would pass every mechanical check and still be false, zhi being the unrelated word “peace”; it is filed as an erratum and “four” is left unwrapped. (2) bzod pa is left unbanked inside chos bzod pa: “mastery” is Geshe Michael's rendering of the technical compound, not of the word, which the bank carries as “bear” and “forbearance”. (3) The second zad par byed pa and the final byed pa are nulled as a broken verb and a light verb respectively. Unwrapped because Geshe Michael supplied it: “these deeds” in the first sentence (the wylie there is only zad par byed tshul ni, with no las), “First let's take”, “instants” and “known as” (there is no skad cig ma in the Tibetan), “had with”, the bracketed gloss “[part of the path of seeing, the direct perception of selflessness]”, “all the”, “they function to”, and the second “deeds” of “black deeds”, where the Tibetan has only nag po.

Segment 323 — the ninth instant, and ending the white and black

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
bar chad med pa'i lam dgu pa'i sems pa gang yin pa de ni dkar gnag zad par byed pa'i las yin te, 'dod pa'i dge ba la 'dun pa 'dod chags dang bral ba'i sgo nas spong , 'dod pa'i nyon mongs thob pa chad pa'i tshul gyis spong ba'i phyir
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Next let's take that type which is the mental movement in the ninth instant of the uninterrupted path. This is a deed which acts to end the white and black, first of all because it eliminates desire-realm virtue by means of eliminating the aspiration, or the desire, for it. Secondly, it eliminates all the mental afflictions of the desire realm--the process here being that one stops the hold which retains them.ni→∅ topic marker; unrenderedyin te→∅ copula plus connective; the "is" of "This is a deed" belongs to Geshe Michael's sentence framela→∅ dative on the object of 'dun pa; Geshe Michael's "for it" is left unwrappeddang→∅ complement particle of bral ba (X dang bral ba = free from X), not a conjunction; the "or" joins Geshe Michael's own appositive pairbral ba→∅ separation, "free of"; "eliminating" is Geshe Michael's recast of the whole idiom, and C05:324 renders the identical phrase with no exponent heregyis→∅ instrumental; this clause carries no "by"
AI NOTE — Commentary on the root verse at C05:321 (de ni dkar nag zad byed yin). The subject is sems pa, the mental movement of the ninth instant of the uninterrupted path. The Tibetan is ONE sentence, … gang yin pa de ni … las yin te, and Geshe Michael breaks it into two English sentences, so the relative and its correlative surface separately: gang yin pa is "which is" and de is the resumed "This". His supplied material carries no Tibetan and stays unwrapped — "instant" (there is no skad cig), "type", "all" (no thams cad), "first of all" and "Secondly" (the Tibetan marks its two reasons only with the closing phyir). dkar gnag and zad par byed pa are both master headwords, glossed "white and black" and "brought to an end", so each is banked whole with its members underneath. bral ba is NULLED: it means separation, and "eliminating" is Geshe Michael's recast of the whole … dang bral ba idiom — C05:324 has the identical construction ('dun pa 'dod chags dang bral ba'i tshul gyis spong ba'i phyir) with one "eliminates" and nothing over bral ba at all. thob pa is the Abhidharma possession (prāpti); only the bare "hold" is claimed, and "which retains them" is expansion.

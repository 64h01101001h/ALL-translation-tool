The commentary turns to a discrepancy. On Geshe Michael's reading, The Sutra on the Ten Levels assigns the results of the lesser and the medium grades of a deed in the opposite arrangement to the one just given — the greater grade is left alone.

The English is longer than the Tibetan in three specific places, and all three are deliberately left unwrapped. however: the contrast is carried by the topic particle ni, and the bank nulls that particle 381 times out of 385, including all 51 segments where a contrastive English word was sitting right there. the kinds of instances: Geshe Michael expands the bare numeral gnyis, carrying over the lesser/medium/greater division from the previous segment; there is no Tibetan word under the expansion. of this after "in reverse": the Tibetan has no demonstrative, only the verb ldog.

Note the word order. AI NOTE —

The head sentence attributes the list to two scriptures — bden pa po'i le'u (the Chapter on the True) and sa bcu pa'i mdo (the Sutra on the Ten Levels) — and says each of the ten nonvirtues carries 'bras bu gnyis gnyis, two results apiece.

Eleven Tibetan items, ten English ones, and the English is right. The list is strung on dang and has eleven members. Nine of them state two results each. The ninth (mi phan pa tshol ba'am phan pa mi tshol ba) and the tenth (gzhan la gnod pa byed pa'am gzhan gyis gnod pa byed par 'gyur ba) each state one result with an internal 'am alternative, so the two of them together are the pair belonging to the ninth nonvirtue. That is why Geshe Michael sets both under a single 9).

What is deliberately left unwrapped. Much of this English is clause-level rendering rather than word-level exponent, and those stretches carry no span: "you get sick easily" for nad mang ba, "is dominated by desire" for 'dod pa che, "No one believes what you say" for skur pa mang, "has any particular value" for tshig bzung mi 'os, "don't have enough to live on" for longs spyod chung. tshol ba (to seek) is nulled both times it occurs: Geshe Michael writes "finding yourself without help" — the English idiom find oneself — and then "never find", and seeking is not finding. mi btsun pa is nulled too: btsun pa is venerable or reputable, a property of the words, while "No one respects" is a verb with the opposite argument structure.

A note on ngan. The same root is rendered twice and differently — "undesirable" for ngan pa in item 5, "harmful" for ngan in item 10. Both are banked as Geshe Michael wrote them.

A variant reading, recorded here because this row is the sound witness. This segment reads chog mi shes pa, not knowing satisfaction, and Geshe Michael's "never satisfied" confirms it. The same passage at C09:152 and C17:499 reads cho ga mi shes pa, not knowing the ritual. The corruption is in those rows, not in this one.

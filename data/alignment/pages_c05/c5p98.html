Segment 292 — sutras that must be interpreted

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de ltar ma yin na gzugs la sogs pa'i skye mched rnams med par thal, de yod par ston pa'i mdo drang don dgongs pa can gyi mdo yin pa'i phyir, der thal, nyi shu pa'i rang 'grel las, de bzhin du bcom ldan 'das kyi gzugs la sogs pa'i skye mched yod par gsungs pa yang de ston pas 'dul ba'i skye bo'i ched du ste bka' de ni dgongs pa can no, , zhes gsungs pa'i phyir
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
If this were not the case, then one would have to say that form and other such doorways through which perceptions grow do not even exist at all. Why? Because you would be saying that all the sutras which state that they exist are sutras which do not mean what they say; sutras which you have to interpret to understand their true meaning. And this too is certainly the case, for the autocommentary to the Twenty Verses states that: In the same way, statements by the victorious Buddha where He says that form and other such doors of perception do exist would be examples of His word that must be interpreted to establish their real meaning; statements that are only spoken figuratively, for the benefit of disciples who might require such explanations.rnams → ∅ (plural; unrendered)par → ∅ (nominaliser + terminative; unrendered)gyi → ∅ (genitive; unrendered)las → ∅ (ablative marking the source of the citation; unrendered)yang → ∅ (additive "also"; unrendered)de ston pas → ∅ ("by that Teacher"; the English "He" may be it, but it may equally be a pronoun for bcom ldan 'das — no pointable exponent)ste → ∅ (connective; unrendered)ni → ∅ (topic marker; unrendered)zhes → ∅ (quotation marker; unrendered)
AI NOTE — Gendun Drup runs an absurd consequence (thal) with its reason (phyir), then proves it from scripture: if the doorways of perception were not as stated, every sutra teaching that they exist would be drang don — scripture whose meaning has to be drawn out rather than taken at face value — and the Twenty Verses autocommentary is quoted saying exactly that. Shape. Flat: d=5 words and d=6 particles in wylie order, three d=7 members inside their compounds, no clause layer. Two words rendered twice. skye mched appears twice and Geshe Michael renders it two ways in one segment — “doorways through which perceptions grow” and “doors of perception”; dgongs pa can likewise gets a long rendering each time. Both pairs are his style, not a slip, and each is recorded where it stands. Left unwrapped (rule 7): “Why?”, which Geshe Michael supplies as the debate question and which has no Tibetan under it at all; “you would be saying that all the”; “where He”; “would be examples of”; “at all”; the third “sutras”, which is his appositional repetition of the two mdo; and the closing gloss “statements that are only spoken figuratively”, a second English rendering of a dgongs pa can already claimed a few words earlier, which cannot be claimed twice. Nulled. de ston pas (“by that Teacher”): the English “He” may be it, but it may equally be a pronoun standing in for bcom ldan 'das, and the link is not one that can be pointed at. Witness. Segment 293 — those sutras are figurative, and the objection returns

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
byas pa la kho na re, skyon med de, phyi rol don du grub pa'i gzugs sogs skye mched yod par ston pa'i mdo drang don dgongs pa can yin pa'i don yin pa'i phyir, zer na, lung snga ma'i don yang mtshungs te, phyi rol don du grub pa'i rnag khrag gi thigs pa 'ga' yang med ces pa'i don yin pa'i phyir
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
In response to this someone might claim: There's no problem; the point of this statement is to say that sutras which explain form and similar doors of perception as actually existing as outer objects are only spoken figuratively, and must be interpreted to establish their true meaning. Well then, the meaning of the original statement then is just the same: it is saying that "there does not exist even a drop of pus and blood which exists as an outer object."byas pa — the foregoing statement; Geshe Michael's 'this'. AI NOTE — The objection and its turning. phyi rol don du grub pa'i occurs twice, and the second occurrence settles the first: there Geshe Michael writes "which exists as an outer object", so the existence verb in this phrase is grub pa, not the yod par that follows it in the first clause. The one "existing" in "as actually existing as outer objects" therefore sits on grub pa and yod par is left with no exponent. This also follows the landed C05:288, where the same construction (sngon po phyi rol don du grub na) was cut phyi rol → "external", don → "object", grub → "exist". The whole opening frame is the landed C05:283 span for span: byas pa nulled, la → "to", kho na re → the naming of the speaker with kho and na re as members, skyon med → "no problem" with its two members, zer na nulled. don appears six times in the Wylie doing four jobs — "objects"/"object" (the external referent), "point" and "meaning" (the sense of a statement), the bound member of drang don, and a sixth Geshe Michael flattens away. 'ga' yang is kept whole as the quantifier it is, carrying "even", exactly as the parallel pus-drop passage at C05:287 banked 'ga' yang → "single".

Segment 294 — the dry riverbed and the fruitless tree

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
yang kha cig, yi dvags kyis chu klung la bltas pa de'i tshe, chu klung bskams pa la tshad mar song ba'i de'i mig shes dang , 'bras bu can gyi ljon pa la bltas pa de'i tshe, 'bras bu med pa la tshad mar song ba'i de'i mig shes tshad ma yod par thal, 'gro ba gsum gyis brlan zhing gsher ba phor pa gang la bltas pa de'i tshe, rnag khrag dngos gnas pa la tshad mar song ba'i yi dvags kyi mig shes tshad ma yod pa'i phyir
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Yet again, another argument might be made: Let's take the case of one of those craving spirits that looks at a river of water, and sees it as a dry riverbed, genuinely so. AI NOTE — A fresh objection built on the same glass of liquid that runs through C05:256, 262, 284 and 285, so the vocabulary is the course’s own: brlan → “wet”, gsher ba → “flowing”, phor pa → “glass”, gang → “full”, rnag khrag → “pus and blood” — each already banked many times over on the earlier pages, and bltas pa → “look” with the object-marker la carrying the “at”, exactly as at C05:270, 287 and 288.

Three things about Geshe Michael’s English are worth seeing beside the Tibetan. First, mig shes stands three times in the Tibetan and “visual consciousness” only twice in the English: he merges the river-gazer’s and the tree-gazer’s into one subject, “The visual consciousness of both of these beings”. The phrase is hung on the second, where the Tibetan predicate tshad ma yod par thal actually attaches, and the first is left unlinked rather than doubled onto English it only half owns; the two de’i that own them are nulled for the same reason.

Second, ’bras bu med pa is literally “there being no fruit”, and “nothing but bare limbs” is a recast: “limbs” has no Tibetan at all, and the “nothing” of “nothing but” is the English idiom for “only”, not the negation. Both words are left null rather than keyed to a picture the Tibetan does not draw.

Third, the reason-marker phyir closes the Tibetan sentence and Geshe Michael sets a bare “Why?” in the middle of the English. It is tempting to link them, and both analysts did; it is left unlinked here. Everywhere else in this corpus a standalone interrogative “Why?” sits over a Tibetan interrogative — ci’i phyir at C05:73 and 78, cis at C05:230 and 235, ci slad at C05:255 — while bare phyir is linked to “why” only inside the connective “this is why” (C03:465, C03:466, C04:60, C05:246). There is no interrogative in this Tibetan, so the question mark is Geshe Michael’s hinge and the particle is recorded as unrendered.

tshad mar song ba — “has come to be a valid perception” — is what he renders “genuinely so” twice and “genuine” once; tshad ma itself is “a valid perception” in the two places where the argument names it outright.

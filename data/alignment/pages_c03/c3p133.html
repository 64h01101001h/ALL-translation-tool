tsha dmar is left unspanned on purpose. GMR’s “look on this Word of the Buddha as perfectly accurate” renders tshad mar byed pa, “to treat as valid/authoritative” (tshad ma + terminative -r). The spine carries the syllable break one letter early — tsha dmar, which parses as “hot/salt” + “red” — and the ACIP of this same segment is keyed the same way (TSA DMAR, where the correct form would be TSAD MAR), so the defect is in the source keying and not in the EWTS converter. Banking tsha dmar ↔ “perfectly accurate” would put a nonsense pair in the dictionary layer, so the two words are left bare, “as perfectly accurate” is left unwrapped, and the erratum is filed. Only byed is claimed, against GMR’s verb “look” — the tighter span, without the phrasal “on”.

The two colloquial clauses are aligned whole, at d=3. shi skye ba chad and rgas ma le bog are a matched pair of Bodongwa’s spoken-register concessions, and their parts do not map one-to-one onto “die, then die” / “drop from old age, then drop”: chad is “let it be cut off”, not a second “die”; skye ba (birth) has no exponent at all; and ma le bog is not a form either analyst could gloss with confidence. English “die” and “drop” each occur twice against one Tibetan verb, so a word-level claim would also have to guess which token it owned (Rule 6, unequal counts). The phrase is the honest unit here. “If you” / “if you” is GMR’s supplied conditional — there is no na in the Tibetan — so it stays outside both spans. The cost is that rgas → “old age”, which is unambiguous on its own, is absorbed rather than shown; that trade favours Rule 10 over coverage.

Idiom, not lexicon, at chu bur. chu bur is a water bubble or blister; chu bur gyi mig is “bubble eyes”, i.e. cataracts, which GMR renders “blurry eyes”. The span records what stands where and should not be read as a gloss of chu bur alone.

Under-claimed on purpose. kho bo is the humble first person singular, “I”; GMR’s “People like you and” is expansion, so the span takes only “I”. kho na is the exclusive “alone, nothing but” — trust in the Teacher alone — which GMR turns into the hedge “if nothing else”; the span takes “nothing else” and leaves the hinge “if”. The genitive ’i of ston pa’i is left bare rather than banked as a member of a looser parent “of the Buddha”, because that parent would have swallowed the article GMR supplied; ston pa → “Buddha” and bka’ → “Word” are the tighter, flat pair. byed is spanned twice with different English — “look” in tshad mar byed and “keep” in yid ches byed — both times as the light verb of a Tibetan verb-phrase idiom whose finite English verb is the exponent; the two occurrences are separated by the forward cursor, not by a guess. Both kyang are nulled: the first is additive (“Bodongwa too”) and the English “same” is GMR’s cohesion device, and for the second the two “but”s in the English are discourse hinges GMR supplied, only one of which could be his. Articles, “may have”, “we must”, “from”, “and”, “your” and “in the” are left unwrapped.

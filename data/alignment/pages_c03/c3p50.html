Segment 148 — the fourth reason: others gain faith

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
gzhan gyis spyod lam des 'dug pa mthong na shin tu dang ba skye ba dang
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Whenever someone else sees you sitting in this position, then they start to have deep feelings of faith in you.
AI NOTE — dang ba (clear faith) → "deep feelings of faith" — the same word rendered "and purely" in the C01 prostration verse (C01:31, dang bas). Two very different English treatments of one term: an adverb of manner there, a noun phrase of emotion here. gzhan gyis … mthong na is a conditional ("if others see"); English gives "Whenever", generalising the condition into a habitual.

Segment 149 — the fifth reason: the Buddha's own posture

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
spyod lam de sangs rgyas dang sangs rgyas kyi nyan thos rnams kyis gnang zhing bsten pa ste rgyu mtshan de lnga mthong nas skyil mo krung bcas te gnas par gsungs so,
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
This position, finally, is one that Lord Buddha, and the disciples of Lord Buddha, have granted us, and have used themselves. Understanding these five reasons for doing so, states the text, one should determine to sit in a cross- legged position.
AI NOTE — nyan thos → "the disciples" — note that the SAME word is "Listeners" in the title "the Levels of Listeners" (nyan thos kyi sa, four holdings). So nyan thos has two English treatments in this course: capital-L "Listeners" inside a book title, lowercase "disciples" in running prose. The title form is frozen; the prose form is not. gsungs so → "states the text" — the supplied subject naming the source rather than a person, where C01 usually supplied "our Lama" or "Lord Tsongkapa". ENGLISH QUEUE: "cross- legged" recurs (second occurrence, with 144).

Segment 150 — why sit straight

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
lus drang por bsrang ba ni rmugs pa dang gnyid mi 'byung ba'i don du gsungs so,
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
It is stated as well that the purpose of sitting up very straight is that this prevents mental fogginess and sleepiness."prevents" and "the purpose" both render mi 'byung ba'i don du — the negative purpose clause split across two English phrases
AI NOTE — rmugs pa dang gnyid → "mental fogginess and sleepiness" — and C03:27 gave the same pair, reversed, as "sleepiness and fogginess". Same two words, same course, order swapped and one modifier added ("mental"). A second instance of the paired-term inversion seen at segment 140. mi 'byung ba'i don du ("for the purpose of not arising") → split across "the purpose" and "prevents" — the negative purpose clause distributed over two English phrases.

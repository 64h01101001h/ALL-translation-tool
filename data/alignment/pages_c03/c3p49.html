Segment 145 — the first reason: practiced ease comes quickly

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
lus legs par bsgrims na spyod lam de shin tu sbyangs pa skye ba dang rjes su mthun pas myur ba myur bar shin tu sbyangs pa skye ba dang
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
When you position your body a bit strictly, it is quite conducive to reaching the state of practiced ease; as a result, you will then be able to reach practiced ease very quickly.skye ba (1st, "arising") — carried by "reaching"
AI NOTE — shin tu sbyangs pa → "practiced ease" — twice in the Tibetan, twice in the English. Cross-reference: at C03:196 and 197 the same term (in its shin sbyang contraction) is rendered "Physical meditative pleasure" and "Mental meditative pleasure". So the technical term for prasrabdhi has TWO English treatments in this course — "practiced ease" in prose, "meditative pleasure" in the chart headings — both attested. bsgrims → "position your body a bit strictly"; myur ba myur bar (reduplicated) → "very quickly", the same intensifying doubling as dal bu dal bus last batch.

Segment 146 — the second reason: you can sit longer

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
'dug pas dus yun ring po 'da' bar byed nus shing spyod lam des de'i lus shin tu ngal bar mi 'gyur ba dang
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
When you sit this way, you find yourself able to sit for a long period of time. It also helps to prevent you from feeling tired.de'i lus ("that person's body") — absorbed into "you"
AI NOTE — 'dug pas → "When you sit this way"; ngal ba → "feeling tired". Note de'i lus ("that one's body") folds into plain "you" — the Tibetan's third-person impersonal becomes second person, which is the drift observed last batch operating in the other direction.

Segment 147 — the third reason: unique to Buddhists

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
spyod lam de mu stegs dang phas kyi rgol ba dag dang thun mong ma yin pa dang
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Another advantage is that this position is unique to Buddhists, and cannot be confused with the position of non-Buddhists in general, or of those whom you may be debating.the "cannot be confused with" clause is English scaffolding — thun mong ma yin pa ("not held in common") is a single negated compound; GMR unpacks its implication
AI NOTE — THE NEGATED FORM IS STABLE WHERE THE POSITIVE VARIES. thun mong ma yin pa ("not held in common") → "is unique" — and C01:494 rendered the same negated compound "is truly unique". Two courses, one English sense. But the POSITIVE thun mong has three renderings across the same corpus: "common" (C03:104), "as a whole" (C01:486), "that are shared" (C03:133). So the negation is settled and the positive is not — a genuine asymmetry worth recording for the successor dictionary, and one a single-gloss entry could not express. mu stegs → "non-Buddhists"; phas kyi rgol ba ("opponents") → "those whom you may be debating" — the technical term for a debate adversary rendered as a situation.

Segment 133 — the preparation

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
dang po ni, sngar bshad pa'i sbyor ba'i chos drug dang khyad par du byang chub kyi sems ring du bsgom zhing de'i yan lag tu skyes bu chung 'bring dang thun mong ba'i dmigs pa'i ngo bo rnams kyang sbyang bar bya'o,
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Here is the first of the two. As a preparation, one should begin with the six preliminary practices presented above. One should, most especially, meditate for an extended period of time on the Wish for enlightenment. As an auxiliary to this meditation, one should finally train oneself in the subjects that are shared with people of lesser and medium capacities.
AI NOTE — sbyor ba'i chos drug → "the six preliminary practices" — a SECOND rendering: C01:485 and C03:24 both gave "the six preliminaries". Same compound, two English forms, both GMR's; preserved side by side rather than harmonised. thun mong ba'i → "that are shared" — A THIRD SENSE for thun mong, joining "common" (C03:104) and "as a whole" (C01:486). byang chub kyi sems → "the Wish for enlightenment"; skyes bu chung 'bring → "people of lesser and medium capacities" — the scopes named by capacity rather than by the usual "scope".

Segment 134 — posture and the steps themselves

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
gnyis pa la gnyis, lus kyi spyod lam ji 'dra bas bsgom pa dang , bsgom pa'i rim pa nyid bshad pa'o, , dang po ni
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Meditating itself can be presented in two parts: an explanation of how one should position ones body during meditation, and an explanation of the actual steps of meditation itself.bshad pa'o — the two "an explanation of" frames carry itdang po ni ("the first is") — no English here; it opens the next segment's content
AI NOTE — ENGLISH QUEUE C03-#10: "position ones body" — "ones" for "one's", and it recurs at segment 135. Kept verbatim, flagged never corrected. lus kyi spyod lam ("the body's manner of conduct") → "how one should position ones body" — the technical posture term rendered as a whole instruction. Note the register shift in this stretch: the translation moves to impersonal "one should" from the "you" of the preceding pages, tracking the Tibetan's own shift into prescriptive outline.

Segment 135 — the eight-featured posture

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
sgom rim bar pa dang tha ma las gsungs pa ltar stan shin tu 'jam po bde ba la lus kyi kun spyod chos brgyad dang ldan par bya'o,
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
The middle and the final books of the Steps of Meditation explain how one should position ones body; that is, one should sit on a very soft and comfortable meditation cushion, in a position with eight different features.
AI NOTE — chos brgyad → "eight different features" — and at C03:76 the SAME compound was "the eight worldly thoughts" ('jig rten chos brgyad). Two utterly different senses of chos brgyad, distinguished only by context: worldly concerns there, posture features here. A translator meeting the phrase needs both attested, which is exactly what the layer now holds. stan → "meditation cushion"; sgom rim bar pa dang tha ma → "The middle and the final books of the Steps of Meditation" — the Bhavanakrama volumes numbered in English where the Tibetan names them by position.

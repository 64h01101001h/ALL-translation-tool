AI NOTE — Wylie: `sbyor bas bsten tshul gnyis yod`. English: "How to Serve Him/Her in Your Deeds". Literally "there are two ways of serving [him/her] by deeds." **Band.** This is the outline band — the "Expanded Outline to the Lam-Rim Source of All My Good" run that opens at 213 and continues through 224 and beyond. It is not prose, and it names no mental state, so the nine-states vocabulary of 202–212 (setting/placing the mind, quieting/pacifying) is not in play. Its immediate sibling is 216, `bsam pas bsten tshul` = "How to Serve Him/Her in Your Thoughts": same frame, same verb, contrasting instrumental. **Renderings specific to this band — check before reusing.** C03 350 carries this exact wylie inside running prose: `dang po la'ang bsam pas bsten tshul dang , sbyor bas bsten tshul gnyis yod`, rendered "The instruction in developing faith comes in two stages: how to follow a Lama in one's thoughts, and then how to follow him in one's actions." So the same Tibetan reads `bsten` = "follow" and `sbyor ba` = "actions" in prose, against "Serve" and "Deeds" here. C01's ten occurrences of `bsten tshul` likewise give "proper behavior towards one's lama" and "how one should take himself to a lama." Corpus-wide, `gnyis yod` normally carries a full exponent — "there are two kinds", "may be divided into two", "comes in two stages" (C01 279, C01 451, C02 31/33/34/38/39, C03 350, C05 136).

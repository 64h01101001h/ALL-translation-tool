One clause, hinged on the instrumental: [dran shes shugs can] gyis || lta ba 'tshol ba — "one seeks the view by means of [being] one who has great power of mindfulness-and-watchfulness." Everything before gyis is the equipment; everything after it is the act. dran shes is the lexicalized abbreviation of dran pa (mindfulness) + shes bzhin (watchfulness), and GMR renders it as a two-limbed English phrase, not as two nouns. C03:191 settles the d=7 split rather than leaving it to guesswork: the same compound there yields "to bring your mind to the object and maintain watchfulness". Segment 199 is the same shape with "the" for "your" and an added comma. So dran = the object-holding limb, shes = the watching limb, and the "and" between them is the buried dang of the unabbreviated dran pa dang shes bzhin — which is why the d=5 parent is licensed to span across it. s199-03 takes "watchfulness" only; "maintain" is GMR's verbal support and stays with the parent, since shes bzhin carries no verb of its own. CROSSING — flag for the reader. The English limbs run in REVERSE of the Tibetan. GMR fronts "a great ability" (= shugs can) ahead of the two limbs, while the wylie puts dran shes first. Spans are ordered on the wylie as required, so the English character indices jump backwards between s199-01 (eng at 78) and s199-04 (eng at 61). That is expected, not an ordering fault.

AI NOTE —

The stanza is one Tibetan sentence hung on a single verb of request, byin gyis rlobs. GMR splits it into two English imperatives — “Bless me to see clearly…” and “Grant me then…” — so the one verb has two candidate exponents and only one may be banked. The complement that actually hangs off byin gyis rlobs is brtson pa drag pos slob par, “to train with fierce effort,” which surfaces under “Grant me then A fierce resolve To master the vows”; so the verb is aligned to “Grant me” and “Bless me” stays unwrapped as GMR's supplied second imperative.

sems…bskyed is the standard compound for generating the Wish for enlightenment, but tsam (“merely, itself”) is wedged between its members, so it cannot be spanned whole; the head sems carries “Wish” and the verb bskyed is nulled. The concessive kyang is what GMR turns into the whole predicate “Is not enough”; that is a recasting, not a lexical exponent, so it is nulled and the phrase left unwrapped. med is nulled because “not” occurs twice in the English with no way to resolve which is which. mi is aligned to “cannot”: the Tibetan carries only the negation, and the modality of “can” is GMR's reading of the future negative mi 'grub, “will not be accomplished” — but “cannot” is a single indivisible word that exists only to render that negation.

rgyal sras, literally “son(s) of the Victor,” is a compound whose two members both surface in GMR's “children of the Victors,” in reverse order. legs par here is adverbial “well,” rendered “clearly”; the “well” in “well trained” is GMR's supplement and is not claimed. The trailing zhes pa'i oo ka gcig ste, “that is one verse,” is editorial apparatus with no English.

Segment 184 — C03:184 — Agitation is the first to lose its power

TIBETAN (WYLIE)

GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
[18] Agitation is the first of the two that loses its power to appear in your mind.Standalone la-don particle closing the adverbial sngon la, 'earlier, first off'. Because the wylie markers run regularly 18, 19, 20, 21 across 184-187, the Tibetan is the reliable index here and Geshe Michael's brackets are the irregular ones: C03:185 (wylie 1a9 = 19) carries no bracketed number at all, and C03:187 (wylie 2a1 = 21) is numbered [17], duplicating the [17] already used at C03:183. SCOPE SHIFT ON sngon. In the Tibetan, sngon la modifies skye ba: the construction is "the power [of agitation] to arise first declines". The English lifts "first" a level and predicates it of the losing — "Agitation is the first of the two that loses its power". The lexical link sngon -> "first" is the correct exponent; only the attachment differs. sngon does not license "of the two". UNWRAPPED ENGLISH — deliberate, and normal. "[18]" is editorial apparatus, and the only Tibetan that could pair with it is the corrupted folio marker. "is" is the English copula; the Tibetan clause is verb-final on nyams pa and supplies none. "of the two" has no Tibetan exponent — there is no gnyis in the wylie; the pair, bying ba and rgod pa, is supplied from the chart, where it is established at C03:171 (the elephant's black = dullness) and C03:172 (the monkey's black = agitation). "in your mind" is likewise supplied: skye ba is bare "arise/appear", with no sems, no yid, and no locative naming a mind. "that" is carried by the Tibetan nominalization, not by a separate morpheme. "its" is English possessive grammar, not the genitive 'i — which links skye ba to nus pa rather than marking a possessor. "the" occurs twice and is barred by the repeated-function-word rule. BOUNDARY CALL ON skye ba'i. The two readings split here: one gave skye ba -> "appear" with 'i nulled, the other skye ba'i -> "to appear". The tighter span wins, so this spec owns "appear" only. "to" does occur exactly once in the caption, so linking it would have been legal — but it is the exponent of the nulled genitive relation rather than of any Tibetan word, and in doubt it stays loose. ROOT DECOMPOSITION DECLINED. One reading broke each pa-word into root + nominalizer at d=7 (rgod/pa, skye/ba, nus/pa, nyams/pa). Not carried: it yields three identical bare "pa" spans, and a child sharing its parent's start offset makes the forward-only cursor jump backwards. The one bound morpheme worth listing is the genitive 'i, which follows its parent and reads cleanly at d=7. la in sngon la is space-separated and sits inside no d=5 span, so it is correctly d=6. TRANSITIVITY AND REGISTER. nyams pa is intransitive — the power declines, degenerates, is damaged — while the English recasts it transitively as "loses its power". The span owns only "loses"; "power" is split off to nus pa and the possessive to nobody. "loses" is a contextual rendering new to this chart for nyams pa and worth a translator's eye, but it is GMR's and is preserved as written. rgod pa, by contrast, is steady: "agitation" throughout the chart and the C03 prose (C03:172, 177, 190, 191), so no register warning is needed here.

Two things follow. ANTI-CONFLATION, the link a reader is most likely to misread. s13 `ting 'dzin` → "one-pointed concentration" is NOT an over-capture of a bare "concentration". TIBETAN-FIRST WALK. `shes bzhin gyis` is agentive: watchfulness is what does the not-letting. `sems` is the thing not let, `'phror` its resultant state, the terminative -r subordinating it to `ster` — literally "does not grant the mind unto scattering". `ster` is "give", here permissive, hence "let". s04 owns "distracted" only; "get" is the English light verb of the causative "let ... get", licensed by ster's construction and not by 'phro. C03:185 corroborates the narrow reading — there `sems ... 'phro ba` is "allowing your mind to be distracted", and C03:191 `'phro bas` is "distraction". SPELLING FLAG, not corrected.

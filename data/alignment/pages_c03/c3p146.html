Segment 457 — no more advanced than bugs and birds

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
des na rang res 'dzam gling 'dir lus longs spyod stobs 'byor snyan grags ji tsam 'dzoms par brloms kyang , so so'i 'khos ka la dpags na, dud 'gro 'bu srin dang bya bye'u sogs las khyad par ma thon pa'i rig rtsal stobs 'byor rgya chung chung zhig las med pa
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
You and I might have some delusions about getting it all together in this world; a good body, lots of things, power, fame. Regardless of how far we progress in these things though, let's be honest. AI NOTE — Reconciled from a Tibetan-first and an English-first reading, which agreed on every span but one. The Tibetan is a single concessive sentence — des na rang res … brloms kyang … las med pa, roughly "so, however much we may pride ourselves on having assembled body, goods, power and fame in this world, if we take the measure of our own situation there is nothing but a very small scope of intellect and strength, not standing out from animals, bugs, birds and the like." Geshe Michael breaks it into three English sentences, so the English crosses the Tibetan at both ends: brloms ("delusions") and 'dzoms ("getting it all together") surface in the FIRST English sentence, while ji tsam … kyang ("how far … though") is unpacked into the second, which also adds the free "let's be honest." The idiom khyad par ma thon ("not to stand out") is split by its own negation, so no one span can hold it: khyad par (distinction, superiority) carries "more advanced", ma takes the "n't" of "aren't" under the negative-affix convention, and thon, the light verb, is nulled rather than credited with "advanced". longs spyod (wealth, possessions) is given "things" alone; "lots of" is Geshe Michael's colloquial quantifier and stays unwrapped. dang is the "or" between "bugs" and "birds", the two nouns it coordinates; the other two "or"s belong to rig rtsal stobs 'byor (asyndetic) and to sogs. Two Tibetan negations, ma and med, collapse into one English "aren't"; the affix goes to ma, whose clause is the one actually rendered, and med is nulled. stobs 'byor occurs twice and is rendered twice differently, "power" then "strength". The two English "than"s both come from the single las after sogs; only the first is claimed, and rgya chung chung zhig las med pa has no separate English of its own.

Segment 458 — nothing worth clinging to, and where it ends

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
'di la shi sbar 'tham ste chags sems dang yid brtan byar mi rung ba lta ci, 'khor ba'i bde 'byor gyi mthar thug tshangs pa brgya byin dang 'khor sgyur gyi rgyal srid la longs spyod pa mang po yang mthar ngan 'gro'i gnas ngan mi bzad par 'khyam dgos par bshes spring sogs nas gsungs pa ltar
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
These things are hardly something worth getting attached to, nothing you would want to hang on to until your dying breath, nothing you could trust at all. Much less; for you can even attain the ultimate pleasures and possessions of all cyclic life--you could become the mighty being called Pure One, or Hundred Gifts, or else enjoy all the many riches in the kingdom of a world emperor. In the end though it always turns out the way that the Letter to a Friend and other such works describe it: wandering aimlessly around in a house of horrors, the three lower realms of life.la, dative/topic marking the object of attachment; unrenderedste, gerundive linker; unrendereddang 'and'; GMR coordinates with a comma insteadbya ba + terminative, 'as a thing to be done'; dissolved into three negative predicates, no contiguous exponentgenitive; absorbed by the attributive 'ultimate'mi bzad 'unbearable'; folded into 'house of horrors'par, adverbial terminative; unrendereddgos 'must'; the necessity surfaces only as GMR's 'it always turns out', which is not dgospar, complementizer terminative; unrenderednas, ablative marking the source of the citation; unrendered
AI NOTE — Three moves, each inverted in the English. (1) 'di la shi sbar 'tham ste chags sems dang yid brtan byar mi rung ba lta ci is one predicate: these are not fit (mi rung) to be made (byar) objects of death-grip clinging, of attachment, or of trust. shi sbar 'tham is an idiom, not a clause: sbar is the paw or clenched fist and 'tham is to clench shut; the same idiom at TCS03:288 (dngos la shi sbar 'thams) is rendered "die clawing after the things I possess," and here it is unwound into "hang on to until your dying breath," which is why shi and sbar 'tham cross. Geshe Michael fronts the head ("hardly something worth") and runs the three objects out in the reverse of their Tibetan order. The single negation mi becomes three English negatives; only "hardly" is claimed, and byar has no contiguous exponent left. (2) lta ci is the pivot "much less," and what follows is the concessive: the ceiling of what cyclic life pays out, a right-branching genitive stack the English reads back to front ("ultimate" / "pleasures and possessions" / "cyclic life"), then Brahma (tshangs pa, "Pure One"), Indra (brgya byin, "Hundred Gifts") and the kingdom of a wheel-turning emperor. The one Tibetan coordinator dang sits between brgya byin and 'khor sgyur, so it is the "or" of "or else"; the earlier "or" and the word "else" are the translator's. The clause closes on yang, which Geshe Michael renders twice, as "even" at the head of the sentence and as "though" in "In the end though"; the dictionary sense "even" is claimed and "though" is left as connective tissue. (3) mthar ... 'khyam dgos: in the end one must wander. dgos is the necessity verb; its inevitability comes through as "it always turns out," which is not a rendering of dgos and is left unwrapped. The destination is named twice in Tibetan (ngan 'gro'i gnas ngan mi bzad pa) and twice in English in the opposite order: the epithet "house of horrors" first, the plain "lower realms" second, with ngan doing duty in both. Left unwrapped on purpose: "things", "are", "getting", "nothing you would want to", "until your", "breath", "at all", "for you can ... attain", "you could become the mighty being called", "else", "all", "riches" (the nominal half of longs spyod pa), "though", "always", "around", "three", "of life".

Segment 459 — whatever you get here deceives you in the end

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
srid pa'i phun tshogs ji 'dra zhig thob kyang mthar bslu ba'i chos can las ma 'das pa'i rang mtshangs sam nyes dmigs legs par bsams pas, 'khor ba mtha' dag sdug bsngal gyi rang bzhin can du mthong zhing rig nas
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
It really doesn't matter what kind of so-called "good" thing you can get here in the world of suffering, eventually all it can do is deceive you. You must by yourself expose the lie; you must learn to think clearly about all the problems these things always bring to you. AI NOTE — One long Tibetan causal chain (...bsams pas, "because you have thought well about...", feeding mthong zhing rig nas) that Geshe Michael breaks into three English sentences, supplying the hinges — "here", "so-called", "expose", "by yourself", "you must", "learn to", "these things always bring to you", "In time", "that", "is in its", "inch" — all of which are left unwrapped. Both occurrences of "suffering" are unwrapped: the segment has exactly one sdug bsngal, and it sits in the predicate sdug bsngal gyi rang bzhin can du = "is in its essence pain"; "world of suffering" and "suffering cycle" are his standing glosses on srid pa and 'khor ba, so only "world" and "cycle" are claimed. The concessive kyang in the ji 'dra zhig ... kyang frame ("whatever kind ... even so") carries "doesn't matter", as at C03:438; the framing "It really" is not claimed. The litotes las ma 'das pa'i ("does not pass beyond [being a thing that deceives]") is GMR's "all it can do is": a construction, not a word, so its three morphemes are nulled rather than pinned. rang mtshangs is a lexical compound, "one's own hidden fault", which GMR names "the lie"; it is kept whole, and the parallel at TCS09:271 (srid pa'i mtshang bcar = "expose the lie of this cycle of suffering") shows "expose" translates bcar, a verb this segment lacks.

Segment 127 — the six conditions summarised

'di dag ni sgom rim bar pa'i dgongs pa ltar bshad pa ste, rgyas par ni nyan thos kyi sa las shes par bya'o, , de lta bu'i chos drug ni ting nge 'dzin bzang po gsar du skye ba dang skyes zin pa mi nyams par gnas pa dang gong nas gong du 'phel ba'i rgyu rkyen gyi gnad bsdus pa ste, khyad par du tshul khrims dag pa dang 'dod pa la nyes dmigs su blta ba dang mthun pa'i yul na gnas pa gtso bo yin no
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
I have explained all these following the Stages of Meditation; for a more detailed presentation, you can refer to the Levels of Listeners. Segment 128 — Geshe Tonpa on the gathering

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
dge bshes ston pa'i zhal nas, rang cag man ngag kho nas lan snyam yin man ngag kho na btsal bas ting nge 'dzin skyer mi 'dod pa de tshogs la gnas pa med pas lan pa yin gsung ste, tshogs ni sngar bshad pa drug la sogs pa yin no
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Geshe Tonpa has said, "When we find ourselves unable to develop deep concentration, we blame it on the instructions we got, and we go and look for some different ones. The real reason though is that we fail to do the gathering." Here "gathering" refers to the six conditions and so on that I have explained above.gsung ste ("so he said") — carried by the closing quotation mark

Segment 129 — the first four perfections feed the fifth

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
gzhan yang phar phyin dang po bzhi ni lnga pa bsam gtan gyi tshogs su 'gro ba yin te, sgom rim dang po las, zhi gnas ni rnyed pa la sogs pa 'dod pa la mi blta zhing tshul khrims la legs par gnas la sdug bsngal la sogs pa dang du len pa'i ngang tshul can du gyur la brtson 'grus brtsams na myur du 'grub bo, , de bas na 'phags pa dgongs pa nges par 'grel ba la sogs pa las kyang sbyin pa la sogs pa gong ma gong ma'i rgyu nyid yin par bstan to, , zhes gsungs so
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
We can also say that the first four of the perfections function as conditions for the fifth perfection, of meditation. The first book of the Stages of Meditation says, If you want to reach quietude quickly, then make great effort: don't be concerned with a desire for things; keep your morality well; and be ready to undergo whatever pain or such you must. This is in fact why texts like the exalted Commentary of the True Thought teach that giving and the rest are causes for each of the perfections above them.rnyed pa la sogs pa ("gain and the rest") — folded into "a desire for things"zhes gsungs so — English TRUNCATES at "are caus", so the citation-closing frame has no English here

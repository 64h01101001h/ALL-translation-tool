Segment 136 — the legs: the Vairochana posture

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de la rkang pa ni rje btsun rnam par snang mdzad kyi skyil mo krung lta bu skyil mo krung yongs su rdzogs pa'am yang na skyil krung phyed pa'ang rung ste de ltar bya'o,
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
[1] As for the legs, one should sit in a full lotus, similar to the full-lotus position of the holy being named Vairochana. It is also quite acceptable to sit in a half lotus position.de ltar bya'o ("do it that way") — carried by the prescriptive frame'am yang na ("or alternatively") — absorbed into "It is also"
AI NOTE — A NAMING-POLICY EXCEPTION. rnam par snang mdzad → "Vairochana" — kept in SANSKRIT, where C01 calqued the other holy names: 'jam dbyangs → "Gentle Voice", byams pa → "Loving One", tshangs pa → "Pure One", thub pa → "the Able", rdo rje 'chang → "the One who Holds the Diamond". So Indian TEXT titles are always calqued (four of them so far) and BUDDHA names usually are — but not this one. rje btsun → "the holy being named", which does the work of introducing an untranslated name. skyil mo krung → "full lotus", consistent with C03:27.

Segment 137 — the eyes

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
mig ni ha cang yang mi dbye ha cang yang mi zum par sna'i rtse mor gtad do,
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
[2] Ones eyes should be loosely focussed around the plane of the tip of the nose, and neither opened too wide nor completely closed.
AI NOTE — ENGLISH QUEUE #10, THIRD OCCURRENCE: "Ones eyes" — "Ones" for "One's", now at 134, 135 and 137, and capitalised here at the start of the sentence. Three instances make it a habit rather than a slip; kept verbatim regardless. sna'i rtse mo → "the tip of the nose"; gtad ("directed, fixed") → "loosely focussed" — the English softens a verb that is otherwise firm, and "around the plane of" is supplied to match.

Segment 138 — the torso, and the thoughts within

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
lus ni ha cang yang rgyab tu mi dgye ha cang yang mdun du mi dgu bar drang por bsrangs la dran pa nang du bzhag ste 'dug par bya'o,
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
[3] Avoid having your body lean too far back or too far forward: straighten up your spine, and sit there directing your thoughts within.nang du bzhag is linked twice — "directing" and "within" split around the object in English
AI NOTE — THE REGISTER OSCILLATES INSIDE ONE LIST. Item [1] and item [2] use impersonal "one should" and "Ones eyes"; item [3] switches to "your body", "your spine", "your thoughts". Three consecutive numbered items, two grammatical persons. The Tibetan gives no such signal — lus ni is as impersonal as mig ni. This is the translator's own variation, and it is exactly the kind of thing a register warning should surface rather than smooth away. dran pa (mindfulness) → "your thoughts" — a notably plain render of a technical term. nang du bzhag → split across "directing" and "within", wrapping the English object between them.

His opening frame We must understand this process, and has no Tibetan under it: it is his bridge answering the dang that closes s441, and stays unwrapped. kun 'byung is samudaya, the second truth: kun “all” + 'byung “arising, source.” The morphemes do not contain “pain”; that is the complement the term denotes, and GMR's standing rendering supplies it as part of the term's name — source of all suffering at C05:91 (the same apposition: “which implies deeds and mental afflictions”), source of all pain at TCS14:67. The genitive 'i on pa'i is rendered discontinuously as brought to us by and is left unwrapped. AI NOTE — A prose hinge plus the second root verse of the Three Principal Paths. Three things govern the alignment.

1. The Tibetan stops one syllable short of the English. The wylie ends at snang shas; the verb that completes the line, ldog (“turn back, stop”), heads the wylie of segment 444 (ldogs pa ltar). GMR's English here carries the whole line, so stop has no Tibetan under it in this row and is deliberately left unwrapped. The same one-syllable offset occurs at 440/441, so it is the chunker's habit at a verse citation, not a loss.

2. A whole clause is unrendered. sngar skyes bu chung ngu'i skabs bshad pa bzhin du — “just as was explained earlier, in the section on the person of lesser scope” — has no exponent; GMR replaced the scholastic cross-reference with the reader-facing “we need something else:”. The nulls record it word by word.

3. “Before” is GMR's hinge, not dang por. dang por is “firstly”; “Before” is the subordinator of a clause GMR composed (“Before we can do this”), and the priority it carries reappears as his own “first” at 444 (“we must stop desire for this life first”). A dictionary link dang por = “Before” would teach a gloss the word does not have, so it is nulled and “Before” stays unwrapped. de = “this” is kept: demonstrative for demonstrative, and both point at the same antecedent — stopping desire for future lives, the close of 442.

False friend, refused. Tibetan long is spare time, not length; English “long” is duration. The true statement is at the phrase level — long med (“there is no time to spare”) is what GMR renders “not long” — and only the negation med = “not” is claimed inside it.

One phrase-level span. yid la goms (“accustom the mind to it”) is rendered as the unit “Think it constantly”: no single word gives “Think”, “it” or “constantly” honestly, so the idiom is aligned whole and no word-level exponent is invented.

Unwrapped by design (rule 7): “Before we can do”, “we need something else:”, the copula “are”, “to” in “hard to find”, the “'s” of “life's” (the existential belongs to med, not tshe), “and” inside the dvandva, “stop”, “for”, and the fused footnote “.63”.

Witnesses. The verse stands in these words at C01:154 and C16:60 (C01:28 has the variant log for ldog), and the prose hinge again at P1:85. Nothing here is a variant.

AI NOTE — The English is re-ordered wholesale. The Tibetan is one long sentence that ends in its main clause — tshe 'di'i snag shas ldog pa dgos te, “one must turn back the preoccupation with this life” — and Geshe Michael lifts that clause to the front of the paragraph (“we must stop desire for this life first”), then unpacks the subordinate reasons behind it. Hence the heavy crossing in eng_order: the last four Tibetan words are the second, third, fourth and fifth English spans.

Head of the segment. ldogs pa ltar is the tail of the citation begun in 443: 443's wylie stops at tshe 'di'i snang shas and the verse's verb lands here. 443's English already renders it (“stop desire for this life”), so ldogs pa is nulled and only ltar earns English, and only “As” — “the verse says” is Geshe Michael's supplied frame. The verb-frame is fixed in this course: C03:442 phyi ma'i snang shas ldog pa dgos shing = “We must stop desire for any future lives”, which licenses snag shas = “desire”, ldog pa = “stop”, dgos = “must” here. The wylie reads snag shas; every independent witness in the corpus reads snang shas (see errata).

Left unwrapped on purpose. “first”, “To do so”, “it is”, “and life” after “perfect body” (rten = body only), “still” (the correlative of kyang's “while”), “here”, “way”, “any extra years”, “you may have managed”, “the years you do have”, and “constantly” (the first of two renderings of ma chad pa; only the morpheme-by-morpheme “never pausing” is claimed). snon pa brgyag (“to add on”) is one dictionary verb and takes “tack on” whole. bsams is rendered twice (“think how hard…”, “And think too…”); the span takes the second, which governs the 'chi dgos pa'i tshul clause standing directly before it. tshul and yod are nulled rather than banked against “following” and “have”, which fill their slots without rendering them.

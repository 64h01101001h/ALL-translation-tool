AI NOTE — Caption 32 — the last caption on the elephant-path chart, and the only one in the run that is a full sentence (194-197 are bare noun phrases). That is why the span count runs above the caption norm. STRUCTURE. One clause with a fronted relative: [stong nyid la dmigs pa'i] qualifies [zhi lhag zung 'brel], which the instrumental gyis then makes the means of [srid rtsa gcod pa]. The English keeps every piece but reorders them — subject first, the focussing clause demoted to a trailing participle. Spans therefore run in wylie order while their English exponents jump backwards and forwards across the sentence. That crossing is expected and not a defect; only Tibetan order is constrained. ZHI LHAG — the finding worth a translator's time. This is the tight abbreviated dvandva of zhi gnas + lhag mthong, and TWO things are true of it here. (a) The English REVERSES the Tibetan order: the wylie says quietude-then-insight, GMR writes "Special insight [vipashyana] and quietude [shamata]". The d=7 members are mapped across that reversal deliberately — zhi to quietude, lhag to Special insight — so the pairing stays truthful even though the surface order does not. (b) GMR renders this compound four different ways inside C03, all verified against the spine: - 198 (here): "Special insight [vipashyana] and quietude [shamata]" — square brackets, a Sanskrit gloss attached separately to each term, Tibetan order inverted. - 131: "quietude ("quiet") and special sight" — special SIGHT, not insight, and a scare-quoted English gloss instead of a Sanskrit one. Compare 119, also "special sight". - 162: "quietude and special insight (shamata and vipashyana)" — round parentheses, both Sanskrit terms pooled at the end. - 512: "quietude and insight", unglossed. Tibetan order (zhi first) is preserved at 131, 162 and 512 and inverted ONLY here at 198. Preserved verbatim per project rule; recorded, not normalized. SRID / RTSA — the boundary the two analysts split on, resolved to the tighter reading. A wider parent span "srid rtsa" -> "root of this suffering life" is technically legal (all substrings check out) but over-captures: "of" and especially "this" have no Tibetan exponent. And "this" is not an ad-hoc addition — "this suffering life" is GMR's STANDING C03 rendering of srid pa, confirmed at 478 (srid mtshor -> "the sea / Of this suffering life") and 489 (srid pa ste 'khor ba -> "the sea of the pain of this suffering life"). It belongs to the English idiom, not to any token in this wylie. So srid and rtsa stay two tight d=5 spans and "the", "of", "this" stay unwrapped. Note also that "suffering life" for srid pa is interpretive — it names the quality of cyclic existence rather than the mechanism (becoming), and a reader mining this segment for a srid pa equivalent should see the rendering flagged as such.

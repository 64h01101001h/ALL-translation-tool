Segment 466 — why the wise take joy in the discipline

de phyir legs par gsungs pa'i dam pa'i chos, ,'dul ba'i tshul la mang po bkod pa la, ,bstan pa'i go rim shes pa'i mkhas pa rnams, ,ci yi phyir na dga' ba skye mi 'gyur
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
This explains why So much of the holy Dharma, Spoken so very well, Was set down in the form Of the works On discipline. Could it ever happen then That those wise men Who understand The proper order Of the teachings Would not take joy in these?pa'i — nominalizer + genitive on gsungs; unrenderedla — locative on tshul; GMR's supplied "in" carries itla — continuative hinging the statement to the question; GMR closes the sentence insteadpa'i — nominalizer + genitive on shes; unrenderedna — emphatic closing the interrogative ci yi phyir na; "then" may be its trace but is left unwrapped
AI NOTE — Four verse lines; the mid-sentence capitals are lineation. Only content words are claimed and the English crosses the Tibetan heavily, so eng_order is supplied: GMR fronts mang po ("much") and dam pa'i chos before the relative legs par gsungs pa'i, reverses line 3 word for word (bstan pa'i go rim shes pa'i mkhas pa rnams comes back as "those wise men Who understand The ... order Of the teachings"), and writes the final chain 'gyur mi skye dga' ba as "Would not take joy" — the idiom dga' ba skye, "joy arises," taken as the light verb "take joy." GMR's scaffolding has no Tibetan under it and is left unwrapped: "explains," "So," "so very," "Was," "in," "Of the works On," "happen then That," "proper," "in these." In particular 'dul ba'i is just "discipline" — "the works" is GMR expanding the vinaya into its scriptural corpus. ci yi phyir, literally "why," is recast as the rhetorical frame "Could it ever happen then"; the interrogative modal "Could it ever" is banked under it, but "happen" is not — at C10:294 GMR renders ga la 'gyur as "How could it ever happen," so "happen" may be the trace of 'gyur, which is already claimed as "Would" here. The emphatic na is nulled and "happen then" stays unwrapped. rnams = "those" follows GMR's registered gloss for the plural marker. The same verse is translated independently at C09:31 ("...all those masters who understand the order Of subjects to learn all take delight in the teachings on discipline"), which corroborates rnams = "those," go rim = "order," and dga' ba skye = "take delight/joy."

Segment 467 — insight needs quietude, concentration needs morality

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
lhag mthong shes rab bslab pa skye ba la, ,zhi gnas ting 'dzin dgos pa'i ming tsam snang , ,'di na rnam dag gting 'dzin skye ba la, ,tshul khrims bslab pa dgos pa'i gtam yag nub
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Nowhere does it say Anything else but this: If you hope to develop Insight, the training Of wisdom well, You must find quietude, That of concentration. It says as well That if you wish to develop Pure single-pointed mind, You must have the training Of morality; And this is fine advice.la — dative of purpose ('for insight to arise'); recast by GMR as the conditional 'If you hope to', which is his hinge, not the particle'sming — 'name, mention'; no word-level exponent. GMR recast 'only the mention appears' as 'Nowhere does it say Anything else but this', a phrase-for-phrase flip with no syllable to point at; left unwrappedtsam — delimiting 'merely, only'; dissolved into GMR's negative frame 'Nowhere ... Anything else but'; no honest single exponentsnang — 'appears, is evident'; GMR's 'does it say' is a different verb supplied for the recast frame, so 'say' is not banked as a rendering of snang'di — 'this / here' (in our day); unrendered — 'It says as well' is GMR's connective between the coupletsna — locative on 'di na, 'here'; unrenderedla — dative of purpose; recast by GMR as 'if you wish to'nub — 'has sunk away, set'; no exponent in the English (see AI NOTE on gtam yag / gtam yang)
AI NOTE — Four lines of nine-syllable verse continuing the poem quoted at segments 465-466; the mid-sentence capitals are lineation. Read flat, the Tibetan says: for developing the training of insight-wisdom, only the mere mention appears that quietude — concentration — is needed; here, for developing pure concentration, the talk that the training of morality is needed [has sunk away]. The English reorders heavily: both la particles are purposive datives that Geshe Michael turns into conditionals ('If you hope to', 'if you wish to'), and dgos pa is spanned as bare 'must' — the verbs 'find' and 'have', the articles, 'You', 'Of', and the metrical 'well' are English-side supply and stay unwrapped. ming tsam snang ('only the mention appears') is deliberately left without English: 'Nowhere does it say Anything else but this' is a phrase-for-phrase recasting in which no syllable carries its own word — snang means 'appears', not 'say' — so all three are nulled rather than banked at the word layer or wrapped whole as a phrase. Two readings in this row differ from the independent witnesses. (1) Line 3 has gting 'dzin where C09:32 and C17:473 read ting 'dzin, the ordinary word for concentration that line 2 of this very row spells correctly; the English 'single-pointed mind' translates ting 'dzin. (2) Line 4 has gtam yag nub where those witnesses read gtam yang nub, 'even the talk ... has sunk away' — the answering phrase to ming tsam snang in line 2. The English here follows yag ('good') and gives 'And this is fine advice', leaving nub unrendered; both spans are honest to the row as ingested. C16:473 and P1:93 carry this row's Tibetan and English identically, so these are the published readings of this translation, not ingest slips.

Segment 468 — brave promises, and the way of the holy

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
khas len tam la rab tu dpa' ba yis, ,dam bcas rab tu 'dor ba phal ba'i phal, ,rang gis ci tsam khas len byas pa yi, ,tshul khrims bsrung la brtson pa dam pa'i tshul
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Some brave souls Claim they'll keep A lot of different vows, But it's oh so common To see them smash Whatever pledges they've made. The way of the holy Is to strive To maintain their morality pure, Exactly as They have agreed To do so.tam as printed; the parallel witnesses C09:33 and C17:474 read tsam (see errata); no exponent in the Englishlocative ('as to'); unrenderedrab tu, intensifier ('utterly'); no exponent in the Englishagentive; carried by English word orderrab tu, intensifier; its force is carried inside 'smash', no separable exponentagentive; carried by English word orderbyas pa, past light verb; the perfect belongs to the whole predicate, not to this morpheme alonegenitive linking the clause to tshul khrims; unrenderedallative on the object of striving; unrendered
AI NOTE — Four verse lines, two sentences; the initial capitals inside the English sentences are lineation, not new sentences. Lines 1-2 are one sentence whose agent is rab tu dpa' ba yis ("by those utterly brave") and whose predicate is the reduplicated nominal phal ba'i phal, "the common of the common," which Geshe Michael renders as the whole idiom "oh so common." Lines 3-4 are the second sentence: rang gis ci tsam khas len byas pa yi tshul khrims, "the morality one has oneself promised, however much of it," then bsrung la brtson pa, "striving to guard it," predicated as dam pa'i tshul, "the way of the holy." The English reverses Tibetan lines 3 and 4 wholesale, which is why the English block crosses. khas len occurs twice and is rendered twice, "Claim" and "agreed"; in line 3 it is the light-verb construction khas len byas pa, whose past stem is what licenses the perfect "have agreed." A good deal of the English carries no Tibetan and is left unwrapped: "Some," "they'll keep," "A lot of different vows," "But it's," "To see them," "Whatever," "they've," "Is to," "their," "pure" (there is no dag pa here) and "To do so." The second syllable of line 1 prints tam; the parallel witnesses at C09:33 and C17:474 read tsam (ACIP TZAM), the same tsam that reappears in line 3 as ci tsam — see the errata. It is left as a null here, not corrected.

Segment 415 — no control over which of the two places we go

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de gnyis gang du skye rang la rang dbang med par 'gro ba gnyis kyi rgyu'am, las dkar nag gi rjes su 'gro dgos shing

AI NOTE — Reconciled from a Tibetan-first and an English-first walk that disagreed on one thing: which Tibetan word GMR's go belongs to. The Tibetan-first walk read 'gro ba as the nominalized main verb (rang dbang med par 'gro ba, "we go without self-control") and gave it go, nulling skye. That parse cannot be sustained: kyi is a genitive, so 'gro ba gnyis kyi rgyu has to be a noun phrase — "the causes of the two migrations" — which is exactly what the ACIP punctuation groups ('GRO BA GNYIS KYI RGYU'AM,) and exactly what GMR renders as "the separate causes for each place". So 'gro ba is nominal here and takes place; the only verb the first clause has is skye.

skye is nulled anyway. The English-first walk gave it "we go". The correspondence is real but it is clause-level, not lexical: skye is "be born", there is no "born" in the English, and "we" is a supplied pronoun. Banking skye = "go" in the dictionary layer would assert an equivalence GMR did not make, so the mapping is dropped and go is left unwrapped. Losing it is cheap.

rang la is split rather than spanned whole — rang (reflexive) and la (dative) each null, since "we have ... no control" absorbs both with nothing to point at. rang dbang med par is taken flat, not as a parent with members: rang dbang = "control", med = "no", par null. Tibetan puts the negation last and English first, and the flat shape says that without wrapping the supplied "absolutely". las takes "things we've done" without the article — GMR's plain-English equivalent for karma, and the article is his. dkar nag is a genuine compound and takes "white and black" whole, with dkar/nag as members; the "and" is supplied inside the compound. rjes su 'gro ("follow after") plus dgos is the obligation the sentence lands on, and the two cross in the English.

The order pins rgyu'am to the first occurrence, which is the one under the genitive chain; the second is GMR's restart and stays unwrapped. Both submitted proposals reported an accepted run without supplying eng_order, which is not possible — the shape here was re-derived and re-proved from scratch.

Segment 416 — few causes of a high birth, a multitude of the low; and the karma fed at death

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
rang rgyud la bde 'gro'i rgyu shin tu nyung la, ngan 'gro'i rgyu ni ha cang mang ba yod, da ltar dge sdig gi bya ba dkar nag ji 'dra zhig byas kyang 'chi khar de dang mthun pa'i las shig la gsos btab nas nying mtshams sbyor dgos la
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Our mental streams contain very, very few of the causes that will take us to a higher birth; but we have a vast multitude of the causes that will lead us to one of the births of misery. Right now we are doing both white deeds, and black deeds: the good and the bad. At the moment of death, the power of one or the other will be activated, and force us over to our next birth. AI NOTE — Shape. Flat: 25 depth-5 words and compounds, 11 depth-6 standalone particles, and 4 depth-7 members inside two compounds. No clause layer. An explicit eng_order is required, and the derivation refuses without it: bya ba's "deeds" occurs three times in the English and gsos btab's "activated" twice, so the generator cannot guess which occurrence each span means.

The crossings. Three places invert. The genitive bde 'gro'i rgyu comes out as "few of the causes that will take us to a higher birth", so rgyu precedes bde 'gro in the English and follows it in the Tibetan. yod fronts to "we have" ahead of ha cang mang ba. And byas fronts to "we are doing" ahead of the whole dge sdig gi bya ba dkar nag string, which GMR also reverses — white/black first, then "the good and the bad" as a gloss — so dge sdig, first in the Tibetan, lands last.

What is deliberately left unwrapped. "contain" is GMR's supplied verb for the locative la of the existential frame rang rgyud la ... yod; only the second half of that frame keeps a verb, so yod takes "have" and the first half is carried by a word with no Tibetan under it. "the power of one or the other" and "force us over to" are likewise GMR's connective tissue. And the entire fourth English sentence — "The seeds of the deeds which are more plentiful are the kind that are likely to be activated." — has no Tibetan in this segment: C03:417 opens a new thought (de nas kyang phyi mar...), so this is oral expansion in the teaching, not a segmentation slip.

What was refused, under Rule 10. las is nulled rather than pinned to the third "deeds" in that fourth sentence. The reading is tempting — las is karma, and "the deeds which are more plentiful" is about karma — but the sentence it sits in is expansion, and "more plentiful" is GMR's gloss of what the karma accords with, not a rendering of any word here. de dang mthun pa is nulled for the same reason: mthun pa is 'concordant', not 'more plentiful'. ji 'dra and kyang have no exponent — GMR flattened "whatever we may have done" into the plain present — and dgos ('must') is nulled because sentence 3 reads "will be". bde was not split off for "higher" (bde is 'happy', not 'higher'), and ngan was not split off for "misery", so both 'gro compounds stay whole.

Over-capture control. Boundaries are cut inside GMR's supplied articles and prepositions: "a higher birth" gives bde 'gro only "higher birth"; "At the moment of death" gives 'chi khar only "moment of death"; "one of the births of misery" gives ngan 'gro only "births of misery". The two dvandvas are split at the word rather than swallowing the conjunction — dge/sdig take "good"/"bad" and dkar/nag take "white"/"black", with bya ba taking "deeds" on its own — so no span owns a comma or an "and" with nothing behind it. Two compounds do carry members: rang rgyud = "Our mental streams" with rang → "Our" and rgyud → "streams" ("mental" is GMR's, and stays inside the parent but outside both members), and 'chi khar = "moment of death" with 'chi → "death" and khar → "moment", which cross inside their own parent. nying mtshams sbyor is left unanalysed at "next birth": splitting it would have had to award "birth" to nying mtshams or to sbyor, and neither is honestly that morpheme.

Segment 417 — fair or foul, the consequences follow like a shadow

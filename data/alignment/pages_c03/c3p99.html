Segment 317 — No thought of returning their kindness

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de'i phyir de dag la drin gzo'i bsam pa med na, klu'i rgyal po rnga sgra'i tshigs su bcad pa las
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Suppose then I were to forget their kindness, and give no thought to returning it. This would truly be an evil way to act, the way of a person without a conscience, of one who had no shame. For the Verses of Drumsong, King of the Serpentines, say as well:dative — 'toward them'; English carries it inside the possessive 'their', so there is no separable exponentablative marking the source of the quotation; GMR's causal frame 'For … say as well' is supplied English, so no exponent is claimed
AI NOTE — Reconciled from a Tibetan-first and an English-first walk. The two agreed on 13 of 15 spans; the two disagreements and their resolutions are recorded below. Short segment (96 characters of Wylie), so 15 spans rather than the 20–45 of a full prose block — what remains is bound morphology with no separable exponent. The argument. de'i phyir ('for that reason') is the hinge back to C03:316, which has just established that every being has already been our parent and showered us with kindness. GMR compresses the whole idiom into the single word 'then', so it is spanned whole rather than broken into de'i + phyir. The clause core is drin gzo'i bsam pa med na, literally 'if there is no thought of requiting kindness'. gzo is the verb 'to requite'; its genitive 'i subordinates it to bsam pa. Crossing order. In English 'returning' lands after 'no thought', while in Wylie gzo'i precedes bsam pa med. Spans are listed in Wylie order and the English side is derived, so s05 legitimately precedes s06 and the crossing is safe. The same crossing recurs at the citation: Wylie puts klu'i rgyal po before rnga sgra'i, English reads 'Drumsong, King of the Serpentines'. Wylie order governs the cursor in both places. Disagreement 1 — the scope of bsam pa med. One walk gave it 'give no thought', the other 'no thought'. Nesting was then legal and necessary: bsam pa → 'thought' and med → 'no' are each unique inside the parent 'no thought'. 'For' and 'say as well' are the supplied citation frame; attributing 'For' to the ablative las would be a guess, so s15's eng is null. AI NOTE — Reconciled from a Tibetan-first (A) and an English-first (B) walk. Verified against the spine: wylie `rgya mtsho dang ni ri rab dang , ,sa rnams bdag gi khur min gyi, ,byas pa mi gzo gang yin pa, ,de ni bdag gi khur chen yin, ,zhes dang`. STRUCTURE. A quoted verse closed by the citation formula `zhes dang`. Three subjects in a dang-list (rgya mtsho / ri rab / sa rnams) share one predicate `bdag gi khur min`; the adversative `gyi` pivots to the counter-claim `byas pa mi gzo gang yin pa de ni bdag gi khur chen yin` — literally "whatever fails to repay kindness done, THAT is my great burden." ENGLISH ORDER IS NOT MONOTONIC, correctly. GMR fronts "My calling's rather to attend" (wylie position 4) ahead of "never fail Repaying kindness granted me" (wylie position 3). Spans are in wylie order per rule 2; their English jumps backwards and forwards, which is expected here and is not a cursor error. WHAT A TRANSLATOR NEEDS — the khur problem. `khur` (burden/load) occurs twice in the wylie and is rendered FOUR ways in the English: "problem", "task", "job", and — for `khur chen` — "calling". This is a live register split, evidenced at span level inside this one segment, and it is the single fact to carry forward: GMR varies the English lexeme for rhythm across parallel clauses while the Tibetan repeats one word. Do not let a concordance conclude that khur means "job". (None of the three registered register splits — gus pa, skyabs su 'gro, mchog 'dzin — occur in this segment.) DISTRIBUTED PREDICATE, deliberately under-covered. `bdag gi khur min` is predicated once but surfaces three times: "is not my problem", "My task is not", "My job is not". One Tibetan phrase against three English exponents — unequal counts, which do not resolve (rule 6). Keyed to the first and nearest exponent only; "My task is not" and "My job is not" are left UNWRAPPED rather than double-keying or fabricating a discontiguous span (rule 7). The subject nouns still carry their own spans, so "the mountains" and "the earth" remain attributed even though their predicates are not. DISAGREEMENTS RESOLVED - `bdag gi khur min` — took B's constituent predicate over A's four separate spans (A nulled bdag gi and min on a GLOBAL ambiguity count; rule 4 measures uniqueness INSIDE THE PARENT, and "my", "problem", "not" are each unique within "is not my problem"). B's member was `bdag` → "my"; widened by one syllable to `bdag gi`, because the genitive is what supplies the possessive. Still tight, still unique in the parent. - `mi gzo` — took A's TIGHTER "fail Repaying" over B's "never fail Repaying", and A's mi → "fail" over B's mi → "never". "fail Repaying" IS `mi gzo` exactly; "never" belongs to GMR's polarity inversion of the whole clause ("my calling is that I should never fail...") and has no Tibetan exponent, so it stays outside the span. This is the boundary-disagreement rule and the semantics agreeing. - `gyi` → null, taking B. A keyed it to "rather", which is verbatim English but sits four clauses downstream in a different sentence; the conservative reading holds. - `byas pa` → "kindness granted", taking B's tighter boundary. A included "me", which `byas pa` does not license (the beneficiary is implied, not stated). - `bdag gi khur chen yin` → "My calling's", taking B on rule 8. A split "My calling" out of "My calling's", breaking a word at the apostrophe; the "'s" is the copula `yin`, but a non-negative affix may not be broken, so `yin` gets no member and the parent carries the whole phrase. - d=7 spans with null English (B's `rgya`, `rab`) dropped per rule 3. `rnams` and the nominaliser in `byas pa` likewise have no exponent and are omitted. - Final `dang` kept (A had it, B dropped it); it is in the wylie and completes `zhes dang`. NOT ERRATA, checked and dismissed - `ri rab` is Mount Meru, rendered "the mountains". Translational generalisation in a verse register, not an error; the d=7 member ri → "mountains" is sound (ri rab = supreme mountain), `rab` has no exponent. - ACIP "MTSO" for `mtsho` is correct ACIP orthography (tsh → TS, ts → TZ). Verified in the spine: 1219 segments contain MTSO, 0 contain MTSHO. - The " , ," shad spacing is normal ACIP-derived punctuation. - Tibetan spelling audit clean throughout: rgya mtsho, ri rab, sa rnams, khur min gyi (correct connective after final -na), byas pa mi gzo (cf. gzo ba, "requite"), gang yin pa, khur chen yin. Segment 319 — A kindness returned is goodness

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
gzhan yang , , drin la lan yod bzang po ste, ,drin gzo med pa ngan pa yin
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
So too say the lines, A kindness returned is goodness, And evil is kindness unreturned.13member of the idiom gzhan yang ('moreover'); the English 'So too' carries no separate exponent for it
AI NOTE — Two lines of quoted verse with GMR's framing clause. 15 spans on 72 characters of wylie -- short for a segment, but the citation is short; nothing is left unaccounted for except the items listed below. UNWRAPPED BY DESIGN (rule 7). Three pieces of English are deliberately not keyed to Tibetan: - "say the lines," -- GMR's supplied attribution formula. The wylie has only 'gzhan yang' introducing the citation: no verb of speaking, no noun for "lines". Its Tibetan counterpart is 'zhes pa ltar', which opens C03:320, not this segment. Folding it into s01 would be over-capture. - the article "A" in "A kindness returned" -- no Tibetan exponent. - the footnote digits "13" -- see errata. STRUCTURE: THE PHRASE NODES ARE LOAD-BEARING, NOT COSMETIC. 'drin la lan' and 'drin gzo med pa' are spanned as d=5 phrases with d=7 members rather than as flat top-level words. In the English, "kindness" occurs twice and "returned" occurs twice (once free, once inside "unreturned"); flat top-level spans would leave both as unequal-count ambiguities under rule 6 and the page would refuse. Nested, each member is unique inside its parent -- verified: "kindness" and "returned" each occur exactly once in "kindness returned" and exactly once in "kindness unreturned" -- so rule 4 is satisfied and the ambiguity never arises. The same nesting is what makes 'drin' resolvable on the Tibetan side: it occurs twice in the full wylie but exactly once inside each parent. 'gzhan' IS NULLED, NOT DROPPED (s02). It has no exponent in "So too", but it is a pronoun, not a bound particle, so rule 3's leave-it-out clause does not reach it; rule 5 governs and it takes eng: null. The reader should see that the idiom has two members and that only one surfaces in English. NEGATIVE-AFFIX CONVENTION (s12, s13). 'med' owns the "un" of "unreturned" and 'gzo' ("to repay") owns the stem "returned"; both are marked subword, the only breaking of an English word rule 8 permits. 'med' is preferred over 'med pa' as the tighter span -- the negation lives in 'med', and 'pa' is a nominaliser with no exponent, so 'pa' is left out under rule 3. Member order follows the wylie (drin, gzo, med) while the English order inverts; rule 2 permits this, since only the Tibetan cursor is forward-only. PARTICLES DROPPED. 'la' (dative on drin) and 'pa' (nominaliser on med) are bound particles with no English and are left out per rule 3. 'ste' is standalone and does surface, so it is kept at d=6 owning "And" -- it is the semi-final connective joining the two verse lines, and "And" is exactly that coordination surfacing at the head of line two. Sole occurrence of "And", no ambiguity. "is" RESOLVES BY EQUAL COUNT. "is" occurs exactly twice in the English and exactly two spans claim it: 'yod' (existential, first clause -- the only verb in 'drin la lan yod bzang po ste', so the "is" of "is goodness" rests on it) and 'yin' (copula, second clause). Equal counts, so it resolves in order under rule 6. 'bzang po' IS NOT SPLIT. "good" inside "goodness" would be a broken word outside the negative-affix exception (rule 8), so 'bzang po' takes the whole of "goodness". Same for 'ngan pa' / "evil". No agentive -s occurs in this segment, so rule 9 does not bite. FOR THE TRANSLATOR. The line is a proverb built on a chiasmus: drin la lan yod / bzang po :: drin gzo med pa / ngan pa. 'lan' (a return) and 'gzo' (to repay) are the noun and verb sides of one idea, which is why both surface as "returned" in the English; they should not be read as two different concepts. 'drin gzo' is well attested across the spine -- 28 segments contain it by wylie substring match (C01:280, 299, 301; C03:317, 319, 320, 322; C04:111; C07:34, 53, 55; C11:480; C16 x6; C17:112, 114; TCS02 x2; TCS03:52; TCS14:107; P1:12, 13, 15; TITLS:614) -- and the immediate context sets it up: C03:317 "give no thought to returning it", C03:318 "Repaying kindness granted me", C03:320 'drin gzo ba dgos' / "now that we must repay them". Treat 28 as a lower bound on real attestations: it counts only the exact string 'drin gzo' and misses 'drin lan' phrasings such as C03:320's 'drin lan bsab pa'. REGISTER. None of the three recorded register splits (gus pa, skyabs su 'gro, mchog 'dzin) occurs in this segment, so no register warning attaches here. Worth flagging separately for a human: 'bzang po' / 'ngan pa' are rendered here as the nominals "goodness" / "evil" in a verse register, where the same pair reads "good" / "bad" in prose (compare C03:317's "an evil way to act" for 'lugs ngan pa'). That is a verse-vs-prose register difference in GMR's own English, not a defect, and not auto-resolvable. ORTHOGRAPHY. The wylie is sound throughout; no spelling defect found. The ' , , ' and ', ,' sequences are ordinary verse-line shad punctuation, not ingest damage -- P1:12 carries the same line with lighter shad spacing and identical syllables. RECONCILIATION RECORD. Proposal A and Proposal B agreed on the title, on the errata, on 'ste' -> "And" at d=6, on the negative-affix treatment of "unreturned", and on leaving "say the lines," unwrapped. Three disagreements were decided: (1) structure -- B's nested phrase nodes taken over A's flat 'drin' / 'la' / 'lan', because A's layout leaves "kindness" claimed once against two occurrences, an unequal-count failure under rule 6; (2) 'gzhan' -- B's explicit null taken over A's omission, since rule 5 covers a word with no exponent and rule 3's leave-it-out clause covers only bound particles; (3) 'med pa' vs 'med' -- B's 'med' taken as the tighter span on a boundary disagreement. A's richer cls glosses were carried into B's skeleton.

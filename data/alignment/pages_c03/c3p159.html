Segment 496 — the wish alone is not enough

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de ltar sngar bshad pa bzhin sems can gyi don du sangs rgyas kyi go 'phang thob par byed snyam pa'i sems tsam zhig bskyed par byas kyang , sems bskyed nas rgyal sras kyi sdom pa bzung ste spyin sogs phar phyin drug la ma bslab par 'tshang rgya bar mi 'gyur la
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Suppose you are able, as described above, to reach the Wish for enlightenment, where you truly hope to achieve the state of a Buddha in order to help every living being. This itself is not enough. And then you must train yourself in giving and the other five perfections. AI NOTE —

One long Tibetan conditional — even though you generate a mere mind hoping for buddhahood for the sake of beings, without training in the six perfections you will not awaken — which GMR breaks into five English sentences, so several hinges have no single word under them: kyang becomes “This itself is not enough,” and the negation ma … mi becomes “Otherwise there is no way.” “The Wish (for enlightenment)” is GMR's term for bodhicitta; the wylie has no byang chub, so bare sems (mind) is not given “Wish” — only the idiom sems bskyed, taken whole, is matched to “reach the Wish.” drug (six) is left unwrapped because GMR counts the other five beside giving. AI NOTE —

The Tibetan is one consequential sentence, des na ... ste. des na means only 'therefore'; GMR recasts it as a full clause, This fact explains why, whose subject and verb are his own supply, so the hinge is left unwrapped rather than banked on the particle. He also fronts the evaluation (ha cang gnad che, 'so very important') that the Tibetan holds to the end.

gsungs is the honorific of 'say'; that honorific is what licenses GMR's holy beings, but no agent noun stands in the wylie, so only said is wrapped. 'dir ('here', in this context) is unrendered. phar phyin drug and rnam pa gsum both put the numeral after the noun and the English reverses both. all, the, of, it is, when, that, are, have are GMR's supplied English.

Segment 498 — morality common to robed and lay

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
spyir ser skya gang yin yang nges par bsrung dgos pa'i bsrung bya thun mong ba mi dge bcu spong gi tshul khrims legs par bsrung ba'i
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
The first type is called the "morality of keeping oneself from committing wrong." Here you begin by being extremely careful to keep the morality of avoiding the ten bad deeds.78 This type of good behavior is common to everyone, whether they wear the robes or not, and must absolutely be maintained.'in general'; no exponent in the Englishnominalizer + genitive; unrendered'that which is to be kept'; GMR's 'This type of good behavior' is a supplied summary, not a renderingnominalizer + genitive; unrendered
AI NOTE — The wylie is a fragment of a running definition (C07:82 carries the same passage, continuing with thog), and GMR's English re-orders it almost completely. Tibetan order: spyir ser skya gang yin yang nges par bsrung dgos pa'i ('in general, whether robed or lay, must definitely be kept'), bsrung bya thun mong ba ('the common object of keeping'), mi dge bcu spong gi tshul khrims legs par bsrung ba'i ('of keeping well the morality of abandoning the ten non-virtues'). GMR states the object first and the scope last. ser skya is literally 'saffron and white', the ordained and the lay; GMR renders the pair idiomatically as 'whether they wear the robes or not', of which only 'robes' is claimed here, with gang yin yang supplying 'whether' and 'wear ... or not' left as GMR's periphrasis. legs par ('well') is taken as 'careful'; 'extremely' is GMR's intensifier with no Tibetan under it. The opening sentence, 'The first type is called the "morality of keeping oneself from committing wrong,"' has no Tibetan under it in this row and is left unwrapped.

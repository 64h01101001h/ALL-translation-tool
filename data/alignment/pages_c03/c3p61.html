So the caption depicts the recognition-and-return function operating across states 3-4, not a stage of its own. Segment 182 — C03:182 — nye bar 'jog pa: the fourth mental state

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
1a6/nye bar 'jog pa
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
[16] 4) Maintaining the mind tightly on the object.Terminative particle bound to nye, making it adverbial; no separate English exponent — the whole adverbial force is carried by the single word 'tightly', already owned by s1/s2.Nominalizer bound to 'jog; English uses the bare -ing form ('Maintaining'), so the nominalizer has no distinct exponent to link.
AI NOTE — Wylie: "1a6/nye bar 'jog pa" | English: "[16] 4) Maintaining the mind tightly on the object." Chart caption for the fourth of the nine stages of concentration on Trijang Rinpoche's elephant-path diagram. Six spans, two lexical words with their bound morphemes; nothing padded. FOLIO MARKER. "1a6/" is left unspanned. It is the damaged form of "16/" — a folio letter spliced into the digits, the same collision that produced "19a67" for 1967 at C03:166 (verified) and that runs through this whole caption block, where the markers climb 1a0/ … 2a9/ across 176–195 (verified). Here the damage is independently confirmed by Geshe Michael's own bracketed caption number, which is also [16]. Damage in the input, not text: not spanned, not linked, not corrected. CAPTION NUMBERING. This caption behaves: [16] sits correctly between 181's [15] and 183's [17]. Worth recording because that is not universal in this run — C03:187 ("dul bar byed pa" / "[17] 5) Controlling the mind.") is numbered [17] where [21] is what the sequence predicts (…[16] 182, [17] 183, [18] 184, unnumbered 185, [20] 186, then [17] again at 187, [22] at 188 — all verified against the spine). That is his text and stays verbatim; nothing is renumbered here or there. Both "[16]" and "4)" are his editorial apparatus with no Tibetan exponent — the wylie carries no numeral at all — so both stay unwrapped. MATCH TO THE PROSE LIST AT C03:157. Exact. C03:157 gives the nine states as "sems 'jog pa, rgyun du 'jog pa, glan de 'jog pa, nye bar 'jog pa, dul bar byed pa …" and renders the fourth as "maintaining the mind tightly on the object". Same Tibetan, same English word-for-word, differing only in the initial capital and the final period. Caption and prose list agree — worth recording, since neighbouring captions do drift: C03:179's "[13] 3) Keeping the mind on the object, with patches where you lose the object." adds a comma the prose list does not have (verified). CROSSING — THE SAME TIBETAN, A DIFFERENT RENDERING. Geshe Michael translates nye bar 'jog pa differently elsewhere in the same course: C03:201 (and its twin at C16:378) lists it as "(4) placing the mind on the object closely" (verified). So "tightly" and "closely" are two of his own renderings of nye bar, and "Maintaining" and "placing" two of his own renderings of 'jog pa. Both are his English and both are recorded; neither pairing is binding, and any dictionary use needs human review before promotion. TIBETAN WALK. Two words, and the whole caption hangs off them. "nye bar" is nye ("close, near") plus the terminative bar, the standard adverbializing frame — literally "closely, in proximity". He reads it as pressure rather than distance: the mind held near the object without slack, hence "tightly". The lexical weight sits on nye, so nye carries the English and bar is null. "'jog pa" is the verb 'jog, "to set / place / lay down", nominalized by pa; it is the same verb that heads the first three states (sems 'jog pa, rgyun du 'jog pa, glan de 'jog pa), which is why the fourth state is a placing and not, like the fifth and sixth, a byed pa "making". DELIBERATELY UNWRAPPED. "[16]", "4)", "the mind", "on the object", and the final period. The wylie has no sems and no dmigs pa — he supplies both the object of the verb and the prepositional phrase from the context of the chart, as he does throughout this list. "the" occurs twice in the segment and is unlinkable in any case; "on" belongs to the English verb frame, not to any Tibetan here. Left-over English here is the correct outcome, not a gap. RECONCILIATION. The two proposals agreed on every boundary except one: the English-first reading opened a d=5 term-level span over the whole "nye bar 'jog pa" owning "Maintaining the mind tightly on the object". That is dropped — the tighter reading wins, and the two noun phrases it would have swept in have no Tibetan exponent at all. The idiom is still visible as the pair s1+s4. Depths audited: nothing here is a standalone space-separated particle, so there is no d=6 span; bar and pa are inside the d=5 spans above them and are d=7, as are the roots nye and 'jog. All six tib strings are unique contiguous substrings of the wylie and all eng strings unique whole-word substrings of the English (checked); no span cuts an English word, so subword is false throughout.

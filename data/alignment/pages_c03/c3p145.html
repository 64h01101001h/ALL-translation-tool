Segment 454 — drom's exhortation, and giving up this life

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
'brom rgyal ba'i 'byung gnas kyi rang rgyud bskul 'debs las gsungs pa sogs ltar yang nas yang du bsams te tshe 'di'i snang shas ldog thabs la 'bad cing
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Contemplate on these quotations, and on works like the verses by Drom Gyalway Jungne, where he urges himself to perfect his practice.66 Think them over constantly, and try your hardest to follow the instructions on how to give up living for this life.ablative marking the source text (said 'in' the Exhortation); GMR's relative 'where' is his own hinge on 'the verses', not this particlenominalizer; unrendered'in accordance with', governing the whole gsungs pa sogs phrase; absorbed into 'Contemplate on ... and on' and unrendered, as at C01:142 and C03:358gerundive connective; unrenderedgenitive linking tshe 'di to snang shas; its nearest trace is GMR's 'for', which is left unwrapped as a hingedative on the object of 'bad; unrenderedcontinuative; 'and' occurs twice in the English and cannot be assigned
AI NOTE — One Tibetan sentence, two English ones. Literally: in accordance with (ltar) what is said (gsungs pa) in (las) Drom Gyalway Jungne's Exhortation to His Own Mind-stream (rang rgyud bskul 'debs) and other such things (sogs), having thought (bsams te) again and again (yang nas yang du), strive ('bad) at the means (thabs) of turning back (ldog) the preoccupation (snang shas) with this life (tshe 'di'i). Four consequences. (1) The title rang rgyud bskul 'debs is unpacked as the relative clause "where he urges himself": bskul 'debs (exhortation) is "urges" and rang rgyud (one's own mind-stream) is "himself"; "the verses" and "to perfect his practice" are Geshe Michael's framing of the title and carry no Tibetan. (2) sogs ("and so forth") is rendered "works like": this is Geshe Michael's fixed idiom for sogs ltar, which he gives as "works such as" at C01:142 and "and other such lines" at C03:358; the such-as sense lives in sogs, while ltar ("in accordance with") is absorbed into "Contemplate on ... and on" and is left silent. (3) The single verb bsams is rendered twice, as "Contemplate" and again as "Think"; only the second is claimed, because it stands beside its own adverb yang nas yang du = "constantly" (compare C01:154, yang yang bsams pas = "Think it constantly"). (4) tshe 'di'i snang shas ldog is a fixed phrase of this text, rendered "stop desire for this life" at C01:153-156 and C16:60-62; here it comes out as "give up living for this life", so "living" sits in the slot "desire" occupies elsewhere and is the residue snang shas owns once ldog = "give up", 'di = "this" and tshe = "life" are taken. thabs ("method") surfaces as "how to", as ldog tshul does at C01:153; "to follow the instructions on" and "for" are supplied hinges. The whole tail runs backwards in English, so eng_order reverses six spans. The footnote number in "practice.66" is an ingest artefact of a registered class and lies outside every span.

AI NOTE — The prose hinge des kyang mi chog pa (“even that is not enough”) introduces four verse lines by rgyal mchog bskal bzang rgya mtsho, the Seventh Dalai Lama; the initial capitals inside the English sentence (This thing they call, Is a house, Wherever you go, In such) are lineation of the citation, not sentence starts. The verse is one long Tibetan noun phrase that GMR breaks into English sentences, so the crossings are heavy: lcags sreg gi khang pa (“house of burning iron”) surfaces as “a house of red-hot steel” with the genitive gi landing on “of” after the head noun. GMR renders phyogs bcu gang du phyin kyang twice: literally as “In any of the ten directions” (du → In, gang → any) and idiomatically as “Wherever you go”; the second pass is left unwrapped and the concessive kyang is nulled. The third line, snying la gzan kyang — literally “though it gnaws at the heart” — is recast as “You wish so badly That it weren't happening”, which has no word-level correspondence, so snying, la and gzan are left with no exponent and only kyang survives, as “But”. snang tshul (“way of appearing”) occurs twice: the first, rang gi snang tshul du 'dug pa, is banked as “nature” (“its very nature”); the second stands where GMR writes “our life”, but “life” is not a gloss of snang tshul and is left unbanked. The two occurrences of “house” are the two different Tibetan words khang pa and gnas; gnas ngan (“bad place”) is GMR's “house of horrors”. Also unwrapped by design: “in itself”, “This thing they”, “Wherever”, “will … you”, “very”, “How … our life”, “To”, “aimlessly”, the fused terminative “In” of 'drar, and every article. The trailing “67” is a footnote number fused to the final period.

Segment 456 — nothing but suffering; seek the way out

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
zhes gsungs pa ltar khams gsum gyi 'khor ba 'dir mtho dman gang 'dra zhig tu skyes kyang sdug bsngal gyi rang bzhin kho na las med tshul bsams nas 'khor ba las thar thabs dang , dgra nyon mongs pa 'joms thabs la 'bad dgos
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
This cycle of life, with its three different realms, is absolutely nothing but suffering, by its very nature. It doesn't matter at all whether you take a higher birth within it, or a lower--it's all the same. Consider carefully the truth of these words, then go and seek some way of finding freedom from the cycle, and a way to smash your enemy: the afflictions of the mind.quotative 'thus'; folded into GMR's "these words", no separable exponent'in accordance with'; the comparative frame is unrenderedgenitive; GMR inverts the possession as "with its", which is his own hingeindefinite particle; unrenderedterminative on the birth-type; unrenderedgenitive linking sdug bsngal to rang bzhin; GMR recasts it as "by its"dative governed by 'bad; unrendered'must'; the necessity is carried by GMR's imperative "go and ...", no separable exponent
AI NOTE — Reconciled from a Tibetan-first and an English-first proposal. The Tibetan is one instruction, ... tshul bsams nas ... 'bad dgos: "having contemplated the fact that [in this cycle of the three realms, whatever birth one takes, there is nothing but the nature of suffering], one must strive at a means of freedom and a means of destroying the enemy, the afflictions." Geshe Michael front-loads the content of the contemplation as two English sentences and puts the instruction last, so nearly every span crosses. gang 'dra ... kyang is the concessive-indefinite frame "no matter what sort"; GMR discharges it twice. The frame is decomposed the conventional way: gang 'dra (the interrogative "what sort") owns "whether", and kyang (the concessive that turns the question into "no matter") owns "It doesn't matter". The second discharge, "it's all the same", is a fresh clause with its own subject and copula and no Tibetan under it, so it is left unwrapped, as is the intensifier "at all". kho na las med = "there is nothing other than": las is the ablative behind "but", med is "nothing", and kho na (only, exclusively) surfaces as the intensifier "absolutely"; the same translator renders sdug bsngal kho na las snying po med par as "it's really nothing but suffering" at C01:251, and sdug bsngal kho na las ma 'das as "nothing but suffering anyway" at C01:234. "very" in "by its very nature" is English idiom and is left unwrapped. 'dir is 'di + terminative and GMR draws on it twice, as the opening "This" and as "within it"; the span takes "within it", which renders both morphemes and sits in the Tibetan word's structural slot (locative on skyes), leaving "This" unwrapped. The first gyi is nulled because "with its" inverts the possession (Tibetan: the cycle of the three realms). dang takes the second "and" (between the two thabs); the first "and" in "go and seek" is GMR's.

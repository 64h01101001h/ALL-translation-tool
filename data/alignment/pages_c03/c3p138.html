Segment 433 — what the effort buys, for now

AI NOTE — Reconciled from two independent analyses. The segment is one Tibetan concessive sentence — de ltar ... 'bad pas (by striving like that) ... 'thob pa srid mod (granted, one might obtain) — which Geshe Michael unrolls into three English sentences and re-orders: mod surfaces as "Admittedly" at the head of the third sentence, and gnas skabs, which opens the Tibetan result clause, closes the English as "for the time being". Both analysts claimed blang dor for "observing the laws"; the reconciler tightened it to "observing". blang dor is what to take up and what to put down, and GMR's fixed rendering of the same frame las 'bras kyi blang dor elsewhere is "our careful observation of the laws of karma and its consequences" (TCS07:523; "observance" at TCS13:705), where "the laws" travels with las 'bras, not with blang dor; bare las 'bras is likewise "the principles of action and consequence" at C01:220. So "the laws" is his noun for karma, not the Tibetan's, and is left unwrapped rather than banked on either word. This is the opposite remedy from C03:427, where the English could not be partitioned and the phrase had to widen; here it partitions exactly. ngang tshul (the way something is) is rendered as the discontiguous frame "how ... works" around "death", so it is nulled: one analyst took "how", the other "works", and neither fragment is the word. bsam is single in Tibetan but doubled in English ("contemplate" ... "think"); it takes "think", the verb of its adjacent object, and by the same principle 'thob pa takes "acquire" (the verb of its adjacent object dpal 'byor) and leaves "to reach" unwrapped. mngon mtho lha mi'i rten bzang recurs verbatim at C03:398 as "the higher forms of life--among humans or the beings of pleasure", which fixes mngon mtho as GMR's whole rebirth-noun ("better forms of life" here) with lha = pleasure being, mi = human, and leaves rten bzang without a separate exponent. The three dang face four "and" and the two genitives face five "of": unequal counts do not resolve, so all are nulled. Left unwrapped as GMR's supply: "Suppose we are able to follow all the instructions", "Then we make all", "manage", "one of the", "as a", "also", "some", "amount", "at least", and the articles.

AI NOTE — GMR's prose gloss on the root verse quoted at C03:432 (spyad pas mi ngoms sdug bsngal kun gyi sgo); the closing "door to every pain I have" deliberately reuses that verse's published wording. Three structural points. (1) The sentence is a ni ... yin topic-comment frame: ni marks "pleasant things in the circle of life" as topic and the segment-final copula yin surfaces at the FRONT of the English as the "is" of "...in the circle of life is that," — a 300-character crossing, which is why the page carries an explicit eng_order. (2) GMR doubles freely: ji tsam ... yang ("how much ... no matter") is stated once in the Tibetan and written twice in the English; only the first "no matter how much" is spanned. Likewise chags pa lhag par 'phel is said twice ("makes us want more" / "increases our desire"); only more, increases and desire are claimed. (3) The negative adjective mi bzad pa is aligned member by member against "unbearable": mi owns "un" (negative-affix convention), bzad owns "bearable", the nominalizer pa is unrendered. The lexical verb 'dren carries "delivers"; the light verb byed and its terminative par are unrendered. Deliberately unwrapped as GMR's supply (rule 7): "But", "the nature of all", "in the", "that", "we"/"us"/"it", "what we get", "feel as though we've", "It only ... it only", "makes us want", "And this then", "to us", "The pleasant things turn around, and", "the" before "door", and "I have" — sdug bsngal kun is simply "all suffering", with no first person in it.

Segment 435 — the great fifth on the affliction that never leaves

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de yang rgyal mchog lnga pa chen pos, ,sngon thog med dus nas rang sems dang , ,dus skad cig 'bral ba med pa yi, ,grogs nyon mongs 'di ko bsten bsten pas, ,mtha' mi mngon 'khor ba'i rgya mtshor bskyur, ,las mi 'dod rlung gis rab bskyed nas, ,yongs sdug bsngal gsum gyi rba klong du, ,dus gtan du mgal mi ltar 'khor ba, ,'di bsams kyin nyon mongs dvangs ma'i dgra, ,tshe 'di snang dgra zhig bsad pa la, ,rang go chod dpag bor rlom byed na, ,dus gtan gyi dgra po nyon mongs 'di, ,da gdul pa'i dus la nges par bab
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
That highest of Victors, the Great Fifth of the Dalai Lamas, has said as well, What happened before? Someone has been in my mind for time with no beginning. When was that? There's never been a moment when they were gone. Who are they? I live, and live again, the mental afflictions. And in the end? They will leave me to rot in the ocean of suffering life, without an end in sight. And the karma? It comes like the wind, with all the things I never wanted. How far? It whips around me everywhere, and stirs great waves, the three forms of suffering. How long? I could wander around this sea forever; the torch would spin, and the circle of light would blaze. What should I see? Think on this, and see that the afflictions of the mind are the one true enemy. What must be done? The enemy of living for this life must die. Who shall do it? You will have to pretend that you are warrior enough to be the one. When will it come? Your foes, the afflictions in your mind, have always been there waiting, ready for the battle. Now then? AI NOTE — Thirteen verse lines from the Great Fifth Dalai Lama, and one Tibetan sentence: a relative construction whose head noun is grogs, "companion" — the companion who has never once left you, who turns out to be the mental afflictions. Geshe Michael dissolves that architecture. He breaks the verse into thirteen answers and prefixes each with a rhetorical question of his own (What happened before? When was that? Who are they? …). Those questions have no Tibetan under them and are left unwrapped, with one exception: And the karma? carries the only English exponent of las anywhere in the segment, so las is spanned there. The "before" of What happened before? was not claimed for sngon; the two analysts split on it and the null was taken.

Three places where Tibetan and English do not sit in the same line. bskyed ("generated, stirred up") ends line six in the Tibetan but surfaces as "stirs" in line seven's English: Geshe Michael moved the verb down to the waves it produces. bsten bsten is a reduplication — "rely and rely" — rendered as the doubled "live, and live again", so the two syllables are spanned separately. And 'khor ba in line eight is a pun on the wheel of samsara and the wheel a whirled firebrand draws in the air (mgal me'i 'khor lo, the alatacakra); Geshe Michael renders it twice, as "wander around this sea" and "the torch would spin", and only the second is spanned, since a span may claim one contiguous stretch of English.

Deliberately left unwrapped: "of the mind" and "in your mind" around the second and third nyon mongs (the first is "mental afflictions"; "your" has no Tibetan), "great" before "waves", "been" after "never", the articles, and "to be the one". Two spellings worth knowing about: mgal mi stands for mgal me, "firebrand", and dpag bor for dpa' bor, "as a warrior" — which is exactly what "warrior enough" reports. Both readings are carried identically by the parallel witnesses at C16:464 and P1:79, so they are the published text, not an ingest slip.

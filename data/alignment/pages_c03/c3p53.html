Segment 157 — the nine mental states, named

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
sems gnas dgu ni, sems 'jog pa, rgyun du 'jog pa, glan de 'jog pa, nye bar 'jog pa, dul bar byed pa, zhi bar byed pa, rnam par zhi bar byed pa, rtse gcig tu byed pa, mnyam par 'jog pa rnams yin la
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
The nine mental states are as follows: setting the mind on the object; keeping the mind on the object with brief continuity; keeping the mind on the object with patches where you lose the object; maintaining the mind tightly on the object; controlling the mind; quieting the mind; completely quieting the mind; attaining single-pointedness; and reaching deep meditation.
AI NOTE — A CLEAN NINE-TO-NINE LIST, in order, no crossings — rare in this corpus and valuable for exactly that. The English distinguishes the three 'jog pa states by what happens to the object: rgyun du → "with brief continuity", glan de → "with patches where you lose the object", nye bar → "tightly". zhi bar / rnam par zhi bar → "quieting" / "completely quieting" — the rnam par prefix carried by one adverb. CROSS-REFERENCE: states 8 and 9 match the chart headings at C03:192 and C03:194 word for word — prose list and chart agree. WYLIE QUEUE: glan de 'jog pa — the third state is normally written slan te 'jog pa; kept verbatim.

Segment 158 — the six powers

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de dag stobs drug gis 'grub lugs ni, bla ma'i gdams ngag thos pa'i stobs kyis sems gnas dang po 'grub, bsam pa'i stobs kyis gnyis pa 'grub, dran pa'i stobs kyis gsum pa dang bzhi pa 'grub, shes bzhin gyi stobs kyis lnga pa dang drug pa 'grub, brtson 'grus kyi stobs kyis bdun pa dang brgyad pa'i grub, 'dris pa'i stobs kyis dgu pa 'grub po
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Here is how you use the six powers to attain these nine states. The first of the states is attained through the power of learning the instructions for meditation from your Lama. The second is reached through the power of contemplating these instructions. You achieve the third and fourth mental states through the power of bringing your mind back to the object. The fifth and sixth are attained by the power of watchfulness. The seventh and eighth you bring about through the power of effort, and the ninth with the power of complete habituation.shes bzhin gyi stobs kyis — English TRUNCATES at "attained by th"; the fifth and sixth states onward have no English here
AI NOTE — dran pa'i stobs → "the power of bringing your mind back to the object" — a FOURTH rendering of dran pa, matching 154's phrasing almost exactly. Across this course dran pa runs: "your thoughts" (138), "to bring the mind back to the object" (154), "bringing The mind back to your object" (156), and this. GMR treats the term as an instruction, not a noun. thos pa / bsam pa → "learning" / "contemplating" — the first two of hearing-thinking-meditating as ordinary verbs. WYLIE QUEUE: brgyad pa'i grub — a stray genitive where the parallel clauses all read pa 'grub; kept verbatim. ENGLISH QUEUE: truncates at "attained by th".

Segment 159 — the four modes of focus

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de dag yid byed bzhir sdud tshul ni, dang po dang gnyis pa'i skabs bsgrims te 'jug pa'i yid byed dang , bar pa lnga'i skabs chad cing 'jug pa'i yid byed dang , brgyad pa ni chad pa med par 'jug pa'i yid byed dang , dgu pa ni lhun gyis 'grub pa'i yid byed du 'jog pa yin no
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Here is how these nine are grouped into four different modes of focus. During the first and second mental states, you focus by forcefully concentrating the mind. Over the course of the middle five states, you focus in a stream, but with interruptions. In the eighth mental state you focus in an uninterrupted stream, and during the ninth you focus on the object completely effortlessly.
AI NOTE — A CLEAN FOUR-TO-FOUR MAPPING. yid byed → "modes of focus"; the four are distinguished by their relation to interruption: bsgrims te → "forcefully concentrating", chad cing → "in a stream, but with interruptions", chad pa med par → "in an uninterrupted stream", lhun gyis 'grub pa → "completely effortlessly". chad here means "interrupted" — the same syllable that at C01:417 carries the annihilation extreme ("thinking things have stopped"). Two unrelated senses of one syllable, and a concrete reason the dictionary keys on spans rather than syllables.

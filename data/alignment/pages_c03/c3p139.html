AI NOTE — One Tibetan clause chain (it does not close until la) that GMR renders as two English sentences, so the crossing is heavy: ltar ("As") ends the Tibetan opening clause but opens the English, and the predicate nyon mongs pa'i dgra po 'di nyid yin is fronted as "This is none other than the enemy of the mental afflictions". nyid is the emphatic identity particle ('di nyid, "this very one"), and "none other than" is exactly the English idiom that carries that emphasis — remove nyid from the Tibetan and the idiom loses its licence — so it is banked as its exponent. yin takes the copula of "This is"; the other "is" ("there is one thing") belongs to GMR's existential frame. gces par byas is one lexical verb ("to hold dear") and is kept whole as "dearly cherished": both English words draw on gces, and byas is the light verb. Three verbs go unrendered: 'byung ba ("arise"), collapsed into "root of"; bcug pa ("placed") and with it dbus su ("in the middle of"), collapsed into "so close to". "the lines", "there is one thing which acts as", "the very", both "so"s, "close to", both "our"s and the articles are GMR's scaffolding and stay unwrapped.

Segment 437 — the afflictions turn the wheel

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
'dis rang cag rnams sngon chad thob med nas da bar sdug bsngal mi bzad pa la sbyar zhing , da dung nyon mongs 'di gtan brlag tu gtong thub pa zhig ma byung na, de'i dbang gis las bsags, las kyi dbang gis 'khor ba'i gnas su nying ma tshams sbyar, der slar yang nyon mongs pas las bsags te de'i dbang gis nyer len gyi phung por nying mtshams sbyar nas 'gro ba rigs drug tu yang dang yang du skye ba len pa thag chod
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
From time with no beginning up to the present moment, this enemy has led us by the hand to all kinds of unbearable pain. And if still we find ourselves unable to discard these bad thoughts once and for all, they will force us to collect karma. Then the karma will force us to take yet another birth in this house, in the circle of suffering life. And there once again the afflictions will start, and then we'll collect the karma anew. And so it is decided: this karma again will force us into the impure parts of a suffering being, in one of the six forms of life. AI NOTE — Word by word from the Tibetan, flat shape. Two spellings in this row are corrupt and are spanned verbatim anyway (rule 1): thob med stands for thog med, 'beginningless' — which is what GMR's "no beginning" translates — and nying ma tshams stands for nying mtshams, the rebirth-link, spelled correctly later in this same row as nying mtshams sbyar. Both are filed in the errata.
GMR expands freely here and several English phrases have no Tibetan under them, so they stay unwrapped: enemy (the wylie has only 'dis, "by this"), time and moment, by the hand (GMR's idiom for sbyar, 'join'; only the verb "led" is claimed), all kinds of, we find ourselves (byung, 'arise', is nulled rather than given "find"), yet another, this of "this house", the second in, will start, anew, the fourth karma (the wylie there is bare de'i), of a suffering being, and the echo wandering through these six. The negation ma takes the "un" of "unable" and thub pa the "able", the campaign's negative-affix convention; mi bzad pa is spanned whole as "unbearable" with mi and bzad pa as its members. 'khor ba'i gnas su is fully reversed in the English: su→"in", gnas→"house", 'khor ba'i→"circle of suffering life" (GMR's standing gloss of samsara). nyer len gyi phung po ('the appropriated heaps') is GMR's standing "the impure parts of a suffering being"; only "impure" and "parts" are claimed and gyi is nulled, because its relation (heaps OF appropriation) is not the one GMR's "of" marks. The two rebirth-verbs collapse: the second nying mtshams sbyar is nulled and "born" goes to skye ba. thag chod, last in the Tibetan, opens its English sentence as "decided", which is why this page carries an explicit eng_order.

Segment 438 — whatever birth we take, only suffering

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de blangs na 'khor ba 'dir mtho dma' gang 'dra zhig tu skye ba blangs kyang sdug bsngal gsum gyis go mtshams med par mngar ba las, bde ba'i skabs cung zad kyang mi 'byung bas gnas gang du phyin kyang sdug bsngal gyi gnas, grogs su dang 'grogs kyang sdug bsngal gyi grogs, longs spyod gang la longs spyad kyang sdug bsngal gyi longs spyod kho na las ma 'das
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Once we have taken a birth in the cycle, we'll find ourselves tormented by the three different kinds of suffering, without the slightest break.62 It doesn't matter at all whether we take a higher birth or a lower one; there exists no such thing as a pleasant moment here. Whatever place we go is a place that brings us pain. Whatever friend we go with is a friend who brings us pain. Whatever possession we have is a possession which brings us pain. They cannot and will not ever be anything else.de → ∅ (demonstrative ‘that’, the rebirth of s437; GMR's ‘a birth’ anticipates skye ba below)gang 'dra → ∅ (‘what sort of’; absorbed into ‘whether … or’)zhig → ∅ (indefinite marker; unrendered)tu → ∅ (terminative on the birth taken; unrendered)par → ∅ (adverbialiser on med; carried by ‘without’)las → ∅ (ablative ‘apart from’; GMR starts a new sentence instead)'i → ∅ (genitive inside bde ba'i; unrendered)cung zad → ∅ (‘even a little’; carried by the emphatic ‘no such thing as’)kyang → ∅ (scalar ‘even’, with cung zad; unrendered)bas → ∅ (causal ‘since’; rendered as a sentence break)du → ∅ (terminative of destination; unrendered)kyang → ∅ (concessive; folded into ‘Whatever’)gyi → ∅ (genitive; recast as the relative ‘that brings us’)kyang → ∅ (concessive; folded into ‘Whatever’)gyi → ∅ (genitive; recast as the relative ‘who brings us’)la → ∅ (dative on the thing enjoyed; unrendered)kyang → ∅ (concessive; folded into ‘Whatever’)gyi → ∅ (genitive; recast as the relative ‘which brings us’)kho na → ∅ (restrictive ‘only’; polarity flipped into ‘anything else’)las → ∅ (ablative ‘beyond’, governed by 'das)'das → ∅ (‘pass beyond’; recast as ‘be anything else’)
AI NOTE — One long concessive chain. The protasis de blangs na … skye ba blangs kyang is unpacked by Geshe Michael into two English sentences in the opposite order: sdug bsngal gsum gyis go mtshams med par mngar ba, the tail of the Tibetan, heads his first sentence (“tormented by the three … suffering, without … break”), and mtho dma' gang 'dra zhig tu skye ba blangs kyang becomes the sentence after it (“It doesn't matter … whether we take a higher birth or a lower one”). The concessive kyang is what generates “doesn't matter”; “It”, “at all” and “whether … or” are his framing and are left unwrapped, as is the opening “a birth” — skye ba sits under “a higher birth”, and de is a bare demonstrative pointing back to the rebirth of the previous segment.

Then three parallel clauses, each closed by the same bare genitive sdug bsngal gyi X: gnas gang du phyin kyang, grogs su dang 'grogs kyang, longs spyod gang la longs spyad kyang. The indefinite is gang (“whichever”) for the place and the possession and su (“whoever”) for the friend — the animate pronoun, not the terminative after final -s: it is the complement dang 'grogs requires, and the same idiom stands in the Eight Verses, gang du su dang 'grogs pa'i tshe. So each of the three “Whatever”s has its own Tibetan. The genitive gyi is nulled all three times: Geshe Michael renders it as a relative clause (“a place that brings us pain”), and “brings us” is his, not the particle's.

mngar ba is read as mnar ba, “to be tormented” — see the erratum. mngar ba is “sweet”, which neither the instrumental agent sdug bsngal gsum gyis nor the translation allows; sdug bsngal gyis mnar ba is the standard collocation, attested more than a dozen times in this corpus. cung zad is nulled: “the slightest” sits one clause earlier in the English, on go mtshams med par, and claiming it would be a guess. kho na las ma 'das, “has not passed beyond only this”, surfaces as “cannot and will not ever be anything else”: only the negation ma has a word of its own; kho na and 'das are dissolved in the inversion.

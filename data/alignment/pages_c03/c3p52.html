Segment 154 — the corrections

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
gnyis pa'i gnyen por dran pa, gsum pa'i gnyen por shes bzhin, bzhi pa'i gnyen por 'du byed pa, lnga pa'i gnyen por btang snyoms te gnyen po 'du byed brgyad bsten nas sgrub dgos la
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
The correction for the second obstacle is to bring the mind back to the object. The correction for the third problem is watchfulness, and the correction for the fourth is taking the necessary action. The correction for the fifth problem is to leave well enough alone. Thus one must attain quietude through undertaking eight different corrective actions.

GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
The text called The Essence of the Middle Way includes a verse that says, Master the elephant of the mind Step by step, as follows: He wants to go the wrong way, So be sure to tie him to the stake Of the object of your meditation With a strong rope made of bringing The mind back to your object; Then finally use the iron hook Of your wisdom to take control. Here the process of learning to meditate is being compared to the way in which you tame a wild elephant, and this is the point of the illustration above. Here I will give you just a rough explanation of each of the steps you see here.ri mo bkod pa ("the drawings set out") — English TRUNCATES at "and th"; the reference to the chart's pictures has no English here

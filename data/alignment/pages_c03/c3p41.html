Note bde ba'i → "with ease" is separated from yo byad ldan → "needs" in the English line order.

Segment 122 — the second condition: few wants

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
2a/, 'dod pa chung ba ni, chos gos la sogs pa bzang po'am mang po la lhag par chags pa med pa'o
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
[2] The second condition is that you keep your wants few; you have no great attachment to things like fine robes, or a lot of things, or the like.
AI NOTE — 'dod pa chung ba → "you keep your wants few"; chos gos ("dharma-robes") → "robes"; lhag par chags pa med pa → "you have no great attachment". The Tibetan's 'am ("or") surfaces as English "or", one of the few particles that survives as its own word rather than being absorbed.

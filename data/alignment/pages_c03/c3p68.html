GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Here is the first state of meditation, called "placing the mind on the object." You achieve this state by means of receiving instructions from your lama about the object you should use for your meditation. The mind at this point stays on the object only from time to time, and for the most part is not fixed on the object at all: you are unable to keep your mind on the object for any continuous length of time. The two mental functions of noting and examining are present, and due to this you often lose yourself to scattering and agitation. You become aware of this condition and so you get the impression that you have even a greater problem of too many thoughts coming up to your mind than you ever had before. AI NOTE — BAND. This is the PROSE band, state 2 of the nine mental states (prose band = C03:202-211). Its quoted name, "placing the mind on the object with some continuity," is prose-band wording. The other band calls the same state "keeping the mind on the object with brief continuity" — verified verbatim at C03:157 (the list) and C03:175 (the chart caption, "[10] 2) Keeping the mind on the object with brief continuity"). Both stand as written; nothing here is normalised toward the other band. BAND CLAIM CORRECTED. Both source proposals asserted that the two English bands sit on ONE Tibetan verb form, bzhag. They do not, and a translator should not be told they do. Checked against the spine: the list and caption band render a different Tibetan phrase outright — C03:157 and C03:175 both read "rgyun du 'jog pa", while the prose at 203 reads "dmigs pa la bzhag ... rgyun cung zad mthud thub pa". bzhag and 'jog pa are perfect and present stems of one verb, so the root is shared, but the written forms and the surrounding words differ. Note in particular that "rgyun du 'jog pa" contains NO cung zad at all: the caption band's "brief" is Geshe Michael's supplement there, not a rendering of cung zad. The same pattern holds one state up — state 1 is "sems 'jog pa" at C03:157 and C03:167, rendered "setting the mind on the object", against the prose "placing the mind on the object" at C03:202. ngal bso ba — SECOND CORRECTION. Both proposals flagged GM's "making a periodic resurgence" as reversing the Tibetan figure (Tibetan says the thoughts REST, English says they SURGE). Three witnesses say otherwise: C03:203 "making a periodic resurgence"; C03:212 ("rnam rtog res ngal bso ba'i nyams") "you have a feeling that they are making a resurgence"; EM:1079, the parallel passage, "our random thoughts have somehow revived, because there are times when they are quiet, followed by times when they come back up." GM is consistently reading ngal bso ba in its recuperative sense — the thoughts recover their strength and so come back — not as their ceasing. It is a settled rendering, not a divergence to flag. The span stays null because no single Tibetan member licenses the English phrase, which is an over-capture refusal, not an error report. DUPLICATE SEGMENT.

Segment 124 — the fourth condition: give up too many activities

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
4a/, bya ba mang po yongs su spangs pa ni, nyo tshong la sogs pa'i bya ba ngan pa dang khyim pa dang rab byung rnams dang ha cang 'dris bshes byed pa dang sman byed pa dang skar ma brtsi ba sogs yongs su spangs pa'o
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
[4] The fourth condition is that you give up trying to do too many things. You give up undesirable actions like business; you avoid being too familiar with laymen or monks; you stop practicing the minor arts of medicine, astrology, or the like.
AI NOTE — nyo tshong ("buying and selling") → "business"; sman byed pa → "medicine"; skar ma brtsi ba ("star-reckoning") → "astrology"; khyim pa / rab byung → "laymen" / "monks", the householder-and-ordained pair. English adds "the minor arts of" as a frame for the last three.

Segment 125 — the fifth condition: pure morality

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
5a/, tshul khrims rnam par dag pa ni, so thar dang byang sems kyi sdom pa gnyis ka la rang bzhin dang bcas pa'i kha na ma tho ba'i bslab bya'i gzhi mi 'dral ba dang bag med pas ral na'ang skyen par 'gyod pas chos bzhin du phyir 'chos par byed pa'o
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
[5] The fifth condition is that your morality is perfectly pure. With regard to your vows of individual freedom, and your bodhisattva vows, you never break down the foundation of your training, the things you have been taught that are naturally wrong to do, and those that the Buddha has prohibited. If out of a lack of attention you do commit such deeds, your are quick to regret them, and you try to make up in the proper way.

The technical pair rang bzhin dang bcas pa / kha na ma tho ba → "naturally wrong to do" / "those that the Buddha has prohibited" — the natural-versus-proscribed distinction, rendered by explanation rather than by term.

Segment 126 — the sixth condition: thoughts of desire

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
6a/, 'dod pa la sogs pa'i rnam rtog yongs su spangs pa ni, 'dod pa rnams la gsod pa dang 'ching ba la sogs pa tshe 'di'i nyes dmigs dang ngan 'gror 'gro ba sogs phyi ma'i nyes dmigs bsgoms nas sam, yang na 'khor ba'i dngos po yid du 'ong mi 'ong thams cad ni 'jig pa'i chos can mi rtag pa ste nges par de dag thams cad dang bdag ring por mi thogs par 'bral bar 'gyur na bdag de la ci zhig lhag par chags pa la sogs par 'gyur snyam du bsgoms la 'dod pa'i rnam rtog thams cad spang bar bya'othe Tibetan continues past the English — mi rtag pa … spang bar bya'o (impermanence, parting, and the instruction to abandon every thought of desire) has no English in this segment
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
[6] The sixth condition is that you totally rid yourself of the stupid thoughts of desire and the rest. You meditate on the problems of desire in this life--how it can bring you to death, and to bondage; and you meditate on the problems it brings you in the life after--a birth in the realms of misery, or the like. Or else you can meditate on how each and every one of these pretty things in the suffering cycle of life is going to end; how quickly it disappears; how surely it will be torn from me before very long. Try then to get accustomed to thoughts like this: So why do I find myself caring at all about these things? What use are they for me? Use these to rid yourself of every stupid thought of desire.bsgoms nas — the two "You meditate" frames carry it

AI NOTE — Caption 5 of the elephant-path chart. 5a/ is a folio marker fused to the head syllable of glang po, and [5] is the editor's caption number; neither is text, so neither is linked, and the first span begins at glang, not at the start of the string.

One Tibetan verb, two English verbs. The wylie is a single clause on the pattern A B dang, C D mtshon — one final verb governing both pairs (elephant→mind, black color→dullness). The English needs a verb in each half, so Geshe Michael renders that one mtshon twice: represents in the first clause, symbolizes in the second. Only one can own the link, and it goes to symbolizes, the finite verb standing where mtshon actually stands. represents is the distributed second realization of the same verb and stays unwrapped — correct and expected, not an omission. The neighbouring caption confirms the pattern rather than the word choice: segment 172 has the identical frame (sprel 'phro ba dang , kha dog nag pa rgod pa mtshon) and renders it "The monkey stands for distraction, and his black color represents agitation." The English verbs are free variants supplied per clause; mtshon is the only one on the page.

Two crossings, handled the same way. Tibetan kha dog nag pa is head-then-modifier ("color, black"), English is modifier-then-head ("black color"); Tibetan puts the object before the verb (bying ba mtshon) where English puts it after ("symbolizes dullness"). Both invert. Each is quarantined under a d=5 parent that spans the whole phrase against the whole English phrase — exactly licensed, nothing extra — with the members riding at d=7 inside it. The d=5 layer therefore runs forward in both languages, and the inversion never reaches the top row. This is why mtshon and bying ba are not two flat d=5 spans: listed flat they would cross the cursor.

Not linked, deliberately. your in "your mind" and his in "his black color" have no Tibetan licensor — there is no genitive or possessive anywhere in the wylie, only bare sems and kha dog; these are the English supplying the second-person address the chart implies. So sems takes "mind", not "your mind", and the noun phrase takes "black color", not "his black color". The is an article belonging to nobody. The comma and shad are punctuation. po is carried as a null d=7 member of glang po. The nominalizer ba inside bying ba is real but is not listed: bying ba is itself a d=7 member here, and there is no depth below 7 to hang it from — "dullness" already carries it in the -ness counted on its parent.

Cross-references. glang po for the mind is not new here: it comes from the Bhavaviveka verse quoted at segment 156, "Master the elephant of the mind" (yid kyi glang po), which the chart illustrates. The caption's bying ba / rgod pa pair, however, is not drawn from the nine-state list — segments 157–159 name the nine mental states, the six powers and the four modes of focus, and dullness and agitation appear in none of them. Their prose treatment comes later: 204 (both abundant in states one and two), 207 (subtle dullness in state five), 208–209, and 190. The rendering of bying ba as "dullness" is stable across all of them, and the black itself is resolved at 191, where glang po'i nag cha zad — "the elephant has lost all the black."

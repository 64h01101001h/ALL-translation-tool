AI NOTE — The Tibetan is one run-on nominal ending in ma yin par: "the extraordinary attitude of renunciation that wants to attain the bliss of the non-abiding nirvana, the liberation freed from ... , not the vague kind that ...". Geshe Michael cuts it into two English sentences, which is why the exponents cross almost end to end. Time frames. gnas skabs ... dang mthar thug ... pairs "short run" (the lower births) with "ultimately" (all of the cycle); they share one head noun sdug bsngal, which GMR renders twice, as "sufferings" and as "pain". Only "pain" is claimed, where sdug bsngal actually stands; "sufferings" and "three" (nothing under it: ngan song says only "bad migrations") stay unwrapped. The nirvana. mi gnas pa'i myang 'das is the non-abiding nirvana (Skt. apratiṣṭhita-nirvāṇa), abiding in neither the cycle nor mere personal peace; "beyond both extremes" is GMR's fixed rendering of it (so also in C06:70, C17:29). grol ba'i thar pa, "the liberation that is freed from" all that suffering, stands in apposition to it; the two liberation words collapse into GMR's one "freedom", which the head noun thar pa takes, grol ba going unclaimed. Renunciation twice. nges 'byung occurs once; "renunciation" twice, because GMR states it and then resumes it when he begins the second sentence. The occurrence inside the phrase's own English noun phrase, "the extraordinary form of renunciation" = nges 'byung gi bsam pa khyad par can, is claimed. bsam pa ("attitude, way of thinking") sits under GMR's "form" positionally, but "form" is a classifier he supplies, not a gloss of the word, so it is left unclaimed. The negation. One ma yin par governs the whole tail; GMR distributes it over four "not"s, two of which ("not the heart", "not in truth") have no Tibetan at all. Only the first, the one that directly negates yam me ba lta bu ("the rough kind"), is claimed.

AI NOTE — One flat Tibetan sentence closed by a nominalized verb, … gsol 'debs byed pa yin gsungs, “[we] are making a request [that …], he said.” Everything before gsol 'debs is the content of the request, which GMR unpacks into an English main clause: “is that our Lama grant us the strength to develop ….” The words our Lama grant us the strength to have no Tibetan under them — gsungs is a bare honorific “said” with no stated subject, there is no verb of granting, and “the strength” is the noun of GMR's request frame, the same template as C03:493 “we are asking our Lama for the ability to find fierce feelings,” where the frame noun does come from a Tibetan word (nus pa) that this sentence lacks.

don gnyer chen po is literally “great desire,” and chen po takes only the intensifier very. shugs drag tu is one adverbial compound, “intensely, forcefully,” and it is spanned as one unit: across the corpus GMR renders shugs drag as “fierce” far more than any other way (TCS09:269 and C01:150 shugs drag po “fierce”; C03:322 and C03:423 shugs drag tu “fiercely”), and the closest parallel to this very construction, P6:50 'gyod gdung shugs drag tu skyed pa “feel a very fierce kind of regret,” hangs “fierce” on the noun exactly as here. So fierce is claimed for shugs drag, and the second amplification, a strong and, is left unwrapped rather than double-claimed.

bcos ma ma yin pa is a negated phrase, “not fabricated,” rendered positively as genuine (compare C04:107 “a totally genuine state of mind” and TCS09:269 “an uncontrived wish”), so the negation ma yin gets no exponent of its own. skye bar is “to arise, to be born,” causativized by GMR to develop. The trailing VI. Finding the Right Path to Freedom is the next section's heading spliced onto this row by ingest and carries no Tibetan.

AI NOTE — The Tibetan is a bare eleven-syllable outline heading, gnyis pa thar par bgrod pa'i lam gyi rang bzhin gtan la dbab pa ni: ‘second, defining the nature of the path by which one travels to freedom.’ It carries 279 characters of English, and roughly three quarters of that is apparatus Geshe Michael supplies with no Tibetan under it, left unwrapped on purpose. ‘This brings us to the … division of how to train one's mind in the Steps of the path which are shared with persons of medium spiritual capacity’ restates the parent outline heading, which is a different segment (C03:246, skyes bu 'bring dang thun mong ba'i lam gyi rim par blo sbyang ba); none of skyes bu 'bring, thun mong, blo sbyang or rim pa is in this row, and only gnyis pa = ‘second’ is. So the first ‘path’, in ‘Steps of the path’, stays unwrapped, and lam owns the second one. The hinge ‘; that is,’ and the whole closing sentence ‘This point is covered in a single verse from the root text:’ are likewise editorial, pointing forward to the root-text verse quoted at segment 463. The heading's own content is the tail, and its English arrives in reverse: Tibetan goal → verb → path → nature → determine, English determine → nature → path → verb → goal, hence the crossing eng_order. lam gyi rang bzhin, literally ‘the nature of the path’, is rendered interrogatively as ‘what kind of path’; the genitive gyi surfaces as that ‘of’ (kind-of-path mirrors path's-nature), while the intensifier ‘just’ is supplied. bgrod pa is intransitive ‘to travel, to traverse’; Geshe Michael turns it causative, ‘can take us to’, and only the verb head ‘take’ is claimed — the modal ‘can’, the object ‘us’ and the deictic ‘this’ have no Tibetan. thar par is thar pa (liberation) with the terminative -r, whose force is the ‘to’ of ‘to this freedom’; since ‘to’ is not contiguous with ‘freedom’, only the noun is claimed. gtan la dbab pa (lit. ‘to bring down onto the firm’) is the standard idiom for settling or determining a point, here ‘defining’. Parallel witness. The identical Tibetan minus gnyis pa stands as outline item b. at C03:248, rendered ‘Establishing What Path Leads to Nirvana’ — independent confirmation of every content mapping here: gtan la dbab pa = establish/define, rang bzhin = ‘what kind’, bgrod pa = leads/takes, thar pa = nirvana/freedom.

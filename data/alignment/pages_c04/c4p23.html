Segment 69 — a correct perception of a changing object cannot be unchanging

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
de yin na, tshad ma rtag pa nyid du yod pa min te, yul mi rtag pa rtogs pa'i phyir, khyab ste, yul shes bya mi rtag pa nyid yin na, , de 'jal gyi tshad ma de mi brtan pa nyid yin dgos pa'i phyir
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Suppose he did have such a perception. This correct perception--which exists as something unchanging--could never be, because it perceives an object of the mind which is changing. This is always the case, for if the thing it knows--the object of the mind--is something which is changing, then it too (the correct perception which engages in this object) can only be something which has no stability.emphatic nyid + terminative du; unrenderedconnective; unrenderedconnective; unrenderedemphatic particle; unrenderedgenitive; unrenderedresumptive demonstrative; carried by the parenthetical noun phraseemphatic particle; unrendered
AI NOTE —

Debate frame: the thesis (a correct perception cannot exist as something unchanging), its reason (phyir: because it perceives a changing object), then the pervasion khyab, which GMR renders with his fixed formula "This is always the case", and its own reason (the second phyir, rendered as the hinge "for"). GMR's fixed renderings: tshad ma = "correct perception", yul = "object of the mind"; the negated mi rtag pa is given positively as "changing" while the unnegated rtag pa becomes "unchanging".

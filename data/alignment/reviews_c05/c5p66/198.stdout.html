Segment 198 — environmental consequences of stealing

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
ma byin par len pa'i ni, 'bras bu nyung ba dang 'bras bu mi grung pa dang 'bras bu 'gyur ba dang 'bras bu long ba dang than pa ches pa dang char ches pa dang 'bras bu skams pa dang med par 'gyur ba dang
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Because you have stolen, then the crops are few and far between; the crops have no power to remove hunger; they spoil; they never come up; dry spells stay on too long; it rains too much; the crops dry up, or die off.
AI NOTE — PROVISIONAL machine alignment reconciled by Codex. The first, second and fifth Tibetan 'bras bu occurrences map to all three English crops tokens, with the intervening predicates and than pa/char fixing the last occurrence. The third and fourth noun occurrences are pronominalized as they; they are omitted from the lexical bank rather than declared untranslated. nyung ba/few and far between and grung pa/power are exact HGM glossary entries. mi owns no; to remove hunger is explanatory and stays unwrapped. The first 'gyur ba owns spoil. long ba is omitted, not nulled: blind/needy glossary senses and the exact local crop context do not securely establish the metaphorical never come up reading. The rejected 'bras bu long ba/never come up pairing also drops its crop subject; adding they repairs that subject boundary but does not establish the uncertain predicate. ches pa expresses excess duration then excess quantity. med par 'gyur ba/die off is a complete productive resultative predicate at d3, despite its glossary attestation.

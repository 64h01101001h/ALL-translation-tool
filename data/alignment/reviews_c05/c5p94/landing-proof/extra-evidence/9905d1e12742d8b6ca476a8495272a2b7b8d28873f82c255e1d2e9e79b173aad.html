AI NOTE — SOURCE, VERIFIED. wylie: "1a2/dran pa'i ltobs so, 'dis sems gnas gsum pa dang bzhi pa 'grub" — English: "[12] The power of bringing the mind back to the object. This allows you to attain the third and fourth mental states." Two clauses: a label ("[it] is the power of dran pa") and a consequence ("by this, the third and fourth states are attained"). THE TERM. dran pa is the load-bearing word and the one that most tempts a wrong link. Lexically it is recollection/mindfulness, but Geshe Michael never uses those words for it here: across C03 he renders the single term as a whole clause. C03:154 gives "gnyis pa'i gnyen por dran pa" = "The correction for the second obstacle is to bring the mind back to the object"; C03:158 gives "dran pa'i stobs kyis gsum pa dang bzhi pa 'grub" = "You achieve the third and fourth mental states through the power of bringing your mind back to the object"; C03:173 has dran (shes) inside "effort needed to bring your mind back to the object". So the caption's clause is his standing equivalent for the one term, and s01 takes all of it. It is not a paraphrase to be trimmed back to "mindfulness". WHAT IS LEFT UNWRAPPED, AND WHY. "of" is linkable only because it happens to occur exactly once in this caption; in most segments rule 5 would forbid it. "to" occurs twice — "back to the object" and "to attain" — so no span touches it: 'grub is spanned bare as "attain" and the infinitive marker stays loose. "allows you to" has no Tibetan exponent at all; the causative reading comes from the instrumental in 'dis, but that force is smeared across an English idiom rather than sitting on a word, so s06 is nulled. Articles belong to nobody. The folio head "1a2/" is unspanned, and the bracketed "[12]" is therefore unwrapped too — there is nothing on the Tibetan side to honestly link it to. CROSS-SEGMENT CONFIRMATION. The caption is near-verbatim the six-powers prose at C03:158, differing only in that the prose uses kyis after the noun where the caption uses demonstrative-plus-instrumental 'dis, and the caption supplies sems gnas explicitly. The English equivalents there agree word for word on dran pa, stobs, sems gnas and 'grub. That parallel is the strongest evidence for every link above. Note also that this caption names a POWER, not one of the nine states, so it is not one of the nine items at C03:157; the states it points at carry the C03:157 wordings in their own segments — C03:179 [13] "Keeping the mind on the object, with patches where you lose the object" (157 has the same words, caption adds a comma) and C03:182 [16] "Maintaining the mind tightly on the object" (exact but for capitalisation). A RENDERING THAT SHIFTS INSIDE THE CHART. 'grub is "attain" here but "reach" in the structurally identical twin captions C03:183 ("This allows you to reach the fifth and sixth mental states") and C03:189 ("...the seventh and eighth"). Same Tibetan frame, three captions, two English verbs. A consumer keying 'grub should carry both. WYLIE ODDITIES, LEFT VERBATIM. (1) "ltobs" is a keying error for stobs: every parallel power-caption spells it correctly — C03:158, 170, 174, 183, 189. The span keeps "ltobs" exactly as the source has it and maps it to "power"; do not silently normalise. A dictionary consumer should key it as a variant of stobs, never as a headword. (2) The head "1a2/" is the corrupted form of "12/", a folio letter spliced into the caption number — the same collision that produced "19a67" for 1967. The pattern holds across the whole chart (9a/=9, 1a0/=10, 1a3/=13, 1a6/=16, 2a3/=23), and 1a2/ agreeing with the bracketed [12] confirms the reading. It is input damage, not content. (3) Geshe Michael's bracketed numbers are his text and stay byte-for-byte even where they drift: C03:187 is numbered [17] although its marker 2a1/ says 21 and [17] is already spent on C03:183, and C03:185 (marker 1a9/) carries no bracket at all. Segment 179 — C03:179 — glan de 'jog pa, the third mental state (elephant-path caption [13])

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
1a3/glan de 'jog pa
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
[13] 3) Keeping the mind on the object, with patches where you lose the object.Semi-final connective particle (ste/te/de series), linking glan to 'jog pa. It has no exponent of its own in the English; GMR's 'with' is a supplied preposition, and pinning a preposition to a converb is over-capture. Left unwrapped.Nominalizer bound inside the d=5 span s3; the English gerund 'Keeping' carries no separate exponent for it.
AI NOTE — Four spans over a three-syllable caption, and roughly two thirds of the English deliberately left unwrapped. For a caption this bare that is the correct shape, not a gap in the spec. WALKING THE WYLIE. `glan` is the stem of glan pa, to patch or mend — to close a hole in something. "patches" is its exact exponent; the verb/noun mismatch is English's doing, not a paraphrase, and GMR glosses the image himself in the prose at C03:205: the place you put a normal patch is a piece of cloth, here the place you put it is the continuation of your focus — you catch the mind after it wanders and patch the hole. `de` is the semi-final connective particle, and its spelling here is REGULAR, not an oddity: `de` is the form taken after a final -n, so glan + de is exactly what the grammar predicts. `'jog pa` is the verbal noun of 'jog pa, to place or set — "Keeping". `pa` is its nominalizer, at d=7 because it sits inside the d=5 span above it, and nulled because the gerund absorbs it. WHAT IS DELIBERATELY LEFT UNWRAPPED. "with" is not linked to `de`. A converb subordinates a clause; it does not license an English preposition, and pinning one to it is the over-capture the audits keep flagging. "the mind on the object" has no exponent either — there is no sems and no dmigs pa anywhere in this wylie. Contrast C03:167, `sems 'jog pa`, where sems IS present; here `'jog pa` licenses the bare verbal noun and nothing of its object. The translator is completing the idiom, exactly as he does at 175 and 182 where the Tibetan is equally bare. "where you lose the object" is likewise unlicensed: it explains what the patch is patching, and no morpheme here says losing, or object, or introduces a relative clause. And "the object" occurs twice in the line, so it could not be linked cleanly even if something did license it. THE TWO NUMBERS, AND THE CORRUPTED MARKER. "[13]" is GMR's caption index and "3)" the ordinal of the mental state; neither is linked, since nothing in the wylie counts — there is no gsum. The wylie's own head reads "1a3/", which is that same number 13/ with a folio letter spliced into it. It is input damage and stays unspanned. Note the trap: the marker's digits and the bracket's digits agree here, which makes the link look free. It is not — the marker is a corrupted folio artefact, not a Tibetan numeral, and linking it is precisely the mistake it invites. The same collision runs through the whole caption block (1a/, 9a/, 1a3/, 1a6/, 1a9/, 2a1/ at 167, 175, 179, 182, 185, 187) and produced "19a67" for 1967 at C03:166. NUMBERING DRIFT — elsewhere, and not to be repaired. This caption is in sequence: [13] follows [12], and it is genuinely the third of the nine states. The drift is at C03:187, numbered [17] where its own marker reads 2a1/ (21), and at C03:185, which carries no bracket at all. Recorded at block level only; verbatim either way. THE CROSSING WORTH FLAGGING. Against the prose list at C03:157 this is an exact match on both sides: 157 gives the third of the nine as `glan de 'jog pa` — same de spelling — rendered "keeping the mind on the object with patches where you lose the object". The caption differs only in the capital K, an inserted comma after "object", and the final period. But the SAME course renders the same state differently later: C03:201 and C03:205 read `glan te 'jog pa` and translate "placing the mind on the object and patching the gaps". So both the Tibetan (de/te) and the English (keeping...with patches / placing...and patching) vary within one course, and both variants are GMR's. Neither should be normalized to the other; the dictionary should carry both. Worth setting beside its neighbours in the series too: 175 `rgyun du 'jog pa` is keeping with brief continuity, 182 `nye bar 'jog pa` is maintaining the mind tightly — the same 'jog pa verb throughout, differentiated entirely by what precedes it. Here the differentiator is glan: the attention lapses and is repaired, over and over.

Both cross into English. s1 is Tibetan head-first (bying ba + modifier phra mo) against English modifier-first "Subtle dullness"; s6 inverts the pair order, GMR writing "obvious and subtle" for phra rags. The d=7 children carry both crossings, which is why s8 and s9 are given individually inside the parent rather than as one link — and it leaves the "and" inside the parent, so no bare conjunction is ever linked (rule 5: "and" occurs twice in this segment). READING NEW TO WATCH: rags → "obvious". Corroborated from GMR's own prose in this same course rather than assumed — C03:212 "bying ba rags pa" = "whether obvious dullness can arise or not", and C03:207/212 both give "bying ba phra mo" = "subtle dullness". That is what licenses s9 as "obvious" instead of the more usual "coarse/gross".

AI NOTE — Fourth of the five parallel verses at C03:449–453, and the refrain is what makes it checkable. Lines 3 and 4 are template: X mi rtag pa'i sdug bsngal thur gyis dran is rendered “The suffering called impermanence Drags our X Downhill” five times running (tshe life 449, bde skyid happiness 450, snyan grags fame 451, grogs friends here, zas nor food and money 453), and Y kyi/gi kho thag de nas chod is “Dispense with Y” in all five. So “our”, “called” (GMR's gloss of the appositional genitive 'i) and the articles belong to the English template and are left unwrapped, and “hoping” has no Tibetan behind it — contrast 451, where “The hope for” does render 'dod. The mid-sentence capitals are verse lineation, not apparatus. Line 1 is a single locative noun phrase, yul dbus kyi tshong dus mgron khang du, “at the guest-house of the market of the central land”: no finite verb at all, so “They travel to” and “And stop in some” are GMR's. yul dbus is the central province, the capital region — his “big city”, banked whole with no members because dbus does not mean “big”. tshong dus is the trade-gathering, the market fair; GMR turns the destination into the errand, “To sell their wares”, and only tshong (trade, sell) can be pointed at, as “sell”. dus (occasion) is left with no exponent and “their wares” stays bare: banking the compound against the whole phrase would carry in a possessive with no Tibetan under it. mgron khang is guest-house, mgron guest + khang house, and line 2 picks the first member back up as mgron po, the guest himself — a pun the English cannot show. GMR names “The guest” twice for the one mgron po; only the first is wrapped. sleb pa arrive / thegs set off and snga dro morning / phyi dro late-day are two matched pairs bracketing the line, and the English crosses at both. Line 3: in mi rtag pa the negation mi lands exactly on the “im-” of “impermanence” and rtag pa on “permanence”, the campaign's negative-affix convention and the one place here where the two morphologies line up one to one. thur gyis is “by way of the downslope”; dran is literally to recall, bring to mind, and GMR reads it as 'dren, to pull — “Drags”. The same thur gyis dran stands in all five verses and in the independent C16:468–472 witness, so it is the published reading, not a slip. Line 4: gnyen is kin, and “your friends And family” is GMR's doublet for it; only “family” is banked, because the segment's one Tibetan word for friends, grogs, is up in line 3 and already spanned. mthun 'jug, to enter into accord, is “fit in”, with mthun (accord, suit) owning “fit”; the “with” that governs the object is GMR's, since the Tibetan marks that relation with the genitive gi. kho thag … chod, split here around de nas, is one idiom — cut the matter through, settle it for good — and the whole of it surfaces as “Dispense with”, tagged on chod.

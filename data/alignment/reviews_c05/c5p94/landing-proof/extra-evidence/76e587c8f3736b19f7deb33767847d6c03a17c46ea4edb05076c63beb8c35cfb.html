AI NOTE — One Tibetan sentence that GMR breaks into three English ones, so the English crosses the Tibetan heavily. Question frame: de lta'i sdug bsngal dang 'bral ba'i thabs is “the method of parting from suffering of that sort”; thabs is the head and is the FIRST “way” in the English — the second “a way” (in “find a way to stop”) is GMR's own scaffolding with no Tibetan under it. ji ltar bya dgos (“how must one act”) surfaces as “What ... must I follow”: ji ltar = “What”, bya = “follow”, dgos = the first “must”; the “I” is supplied. snyam na (“if one thinks”) is the standard objection-marker; only snyam has a hard exponent (“ask”), and na is left null rather than pinned to “you may”.
Main clause: las dang nyon mongs pa'i rang bzhin can gyi 'khor ba'i skye rgyun unpacks right-to-left in English: skye rgyun (“stream of births”, members crossing — rgyun is “stream” but stands second) is the head noun, 'khor ba its genitive owner, and can the possessive suffix that builds the relative clause. can is spanned only on “has”; the “that ... as its” frame is English grammar GMR supplied, and “very” in “its very nature” is an intensifier left unwrapped rather than charged to rang. The bound genitives 'i on pa'i and ba'i here are not cut.
Negated-until clause: gcod thub pa zhig ma byung bar du is “until some ability to cut it has arisen”. GMR renders it TWICE — positively as the whole sentence “You must find a way to stop the stream of births”, and again as “Until you manage to do so”. Only the second copy is spanned: gcod = “stop”, thub pa = “manage”, bar du = “Until”. The negation ma and the verb byung have no separable exponent (“Until” already carries the negative; “do so” is anaphora), so both are null.
Closing clause: sdug bsngal dang bral ba'i gnas med is “there is no place parted from suffering”; the existential negation med shows up as “never”, with “you will ... find” supplied around it, and bral lands on “free” (the “of” is English government, not the genitive). Note the two stems of one verb in one sentence: 'bral ba (present/future) in the first clause, bral ba (past) in the last — both are standard and both stand in the ACIP.

Points to watch.

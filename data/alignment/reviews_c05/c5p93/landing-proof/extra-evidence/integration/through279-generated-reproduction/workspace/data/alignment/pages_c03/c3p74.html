AI NOTE — NAMING BAND. This is a heading, not prose. Segment 212 closes the nine-states discussion (the impressions passage), and 213 opens the Lam-Rim outline whose entries run 214, 215, 216 onward. It names no mental state, so neither nine-state vocabulary — the list-and-caption band or the prose band of 202-211 — is in play, and nothing here needs checking against them. WALKING THE WYLIE. **yon tan** is the ordinary word for good quality or virtue; the spine's binding gloss list runs "good qualities / good quality / fine spiritual qualities", and GMR compresses it here to the bare noun "Good" as title register. **gzhir** is gzhi, "basis, ground", with the terminative -r, "as/into"; the -r is what makes gzhir gyur a change-of-state phrase, "became the basis". English collapses that whole phrase into one noun, so gzhir carries "Source" and **gyur** is nulled rather than given invented English. Lookup warning: the spine has no entry for the inflected gzhir, and the entry for bare gzhi carries the anomalous hgm_gloss "give it a name" — a dictionary hit on this word will mislead. **ma'i** is the Tibetan habit of naming a text by its incipit and tagging it with ma. **sa bcad** is the technical term for a topical outline of a text; its sole binding gloss is "outline", matching exactly, and sa and bcad are not split out since neither "ground" nor "cut" surfaces separately. **rgyas pa**, "extensive, elaborated", is "Expanded" — note this is a rendering new to the register: the binding gloss list for rgyas pa runs "extensive / more detail / longer / enlarging upon / spread", and "Expanded" appears here as the title-form of that sense. THE TITLE, VERIFIED. yon tan gzhir gyur ma is Tsongkapa's short Lam-Rim prayer, and "Source of All My Good" is its settled English across the spine — confirmed on bare one-line witnesses where the whole Tibetan is just the title (TITLS 759, TITLS 2407, SUBJ 93), and on 30 segments in all across C03, P1, TITLS, SUBJ and TCS08. So the English of this heading is a received title, not a word-for-word rendering, and only the words the Tibetan actually supplies are spanned. DELIBERATELY UNWRAPPED, per the no-over-capture rule. - "Lam-Rim" — supplied by GMR from context. There is no lam rim, and no byang chub lam gyi rim pa, anywhere in this wylie. It sits directly between "to the" and "Source", so a greedy reading of ma'i or 'gred pa'i would swallow it. No span may claim it. - "All My" — supplied here, though worth knowing WHY: "all" is genuinely kun, which appears in the root verse itself (C03/349, yon tan kun gzhir gyur drin can rje / "The source of all my good Is my kind Lama"). The title in this line abbreviates the incipit and drops kun, so in THIS segment nothing licenses "All", and nothing at all licenses the possessive "My". - "to the" and the internal "of" — connective tissue. Two genitives are available and only one relational preposition appears; assigning "to the" to either is a coin-flip, so both genitives are nulled. "of" in "Source of All My Good" is carried in Tibetan by bare juxtaposition and the terminative, not by any particle, so giving gzhir the string "Source of" would be exactly the preposition-grab that has cost pages before. - The leading and trailing shads are punctuation, not words. WYLIE ODDITY: 'gred pa. Both readings of this were checked against the spine rather than assumed. 'gred pa IS a real headword in the dictionary (id 72530, entered from the Lokesh Chandra supplement-1992 layer, status awaiting-hgm-equivalent, no hgm_gloss), so it is not self-evidently a keying error. But it is also a corpus hapax: 'gred occurs in exactly one of the 42,199 segments — this one — against 929 segments carrying 'grel, whose glosses are "commentary, commented, explain". A commentary word is what the syntax wants here. The honest position is that this is very likely 'grel pa mis-keyed but is an attested word in its own right with no binding equivalent, so it is flagged and preserved byte-for-byte, never normalised. DEPTH. No d=7 spans survive. Proposal A offered the genitive 'i inside 'gred pa'i carrying "to"; it is dropped on two counts — its parent is nulled, so it could carry no substring of a parent English that does not exist; and "to" occurs exactly once in the English, which one span cannot share with another. Inside rgyas pa the nominalizer pa likewise has no exponent. ma'i stays at d=6 as a standalone space-separated particle cluster; gyur stays at d=5 as a full verb, nulled rather than dropped because a reader walking the Tibetan will look for it. Segment 348 of this same course carries the identical wylie lam gyi rtsa ba bshes gnyen bsten pa'i tshul verbatim inside running prose, where Geshe Michael renders it "the very root of the path, which is how to take a Lama and serve him or her properly". That single witness fixes every pairing in this heading at once: lam = path, rtsa ba = root, bshes gnyen = Lama, bsten pa = take, tshul = how. Segment 216 confirms the verb-plus-head pairing separately: bsam pas bsten tshul there is "How to Serve Him/Her in Your Thoughts". Two renderings a translator must not promote. The binding hgm_gloss for bshes gnyen is "spiritual guide / spiritual friend / guide" — it does not contain "Lama". "Lama" is bla ma's gloss, and in segment 348 the very same sentence renders bla mas as "a Lama" too, so both words land on the one English here. Likewise bsten pa glosses "keep a high level of / spending time"; "Take" is what he wrote in this heading, and 216–217 use "Serve" for the same verb. Span s5 and span s6 link the English Geshe Michael actually wrote in this segment; neither is a new dictionary equivalent for the Tibetan, and neither may be read back into the glossary. Wylie oddity: the tail is a segmentation bleed. The wylie stops at tshul, but the English continues Root text verse: "The source..." — the head of the root-text citation that follows, pulled in by the segmentation. This is systematic, not a one-off: twelve C03 outline segments carry a "Root text verse" trailer (214, 220, 225, 247, 248, 273, 278, 285, 289, 291, 292, 293), eighteen across the corpus. Everything from the second "Root" onward is left unwrapped, since no Tibetan here licenses it. That bleed is the sole reason rtsa ba is nulled: it supplies the second "Root", and one span claiming a twice-occurring string is undeterminable. Where the bleed is absent the link goes through cleanly — segment 215, a. rtsa ba dad pa sbyang pa, is "The Root Practice: Developing Faith for Him/Her". Structure. The Tibetan is head-final and reads literally "the manner of relying on a spiritual friend, the root of the path"; it mirrors into head-initial English, so bshes gnyen / bsten pa'i / tshul emerge as How / Take / Lama in reverse. gyi is the genitive in its gyi allomorph, selected because lam ends in -m; it is space-separated in the wylie and stays at d=6. bsten pa'i is written whole so the span does not split the syllable pa'i; the genitive inside it has no English exponent of its own — the relation surfaces as the "How to" frame — so it is omitted rather than given a null d=7 member. No d=7 members are proposed anywhere: bshes gnyen is a two-member compound (friend + kinsman) but "Lama" is atomic English with no internal exponents to hand them, and rtsa ba is nulled at the parent. Left unwrapped as connective tissue: the articles "the" and "a", the infinitival "to" (English syntax attaching to "How"), the colon, and the entire bled tail.

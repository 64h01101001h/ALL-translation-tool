AI NOTE —

Three guards, in the order the Tibetan sets them out. dran pa (recollection) comes dang por, first, and its job is negative: ma brjed par 'dzin, to hold on ('dzin, GMR's first “keeps”) without forgetting blang dor gyi gnas, the points of what to take up and what to put down. GMR unpacks the blang dor pair into two full clauses, so blang and dor are members owning one clause each.

shes bzhin (watchfulness) works bar bar du, intermittently — it audits rather than guards. Its object is sgo gsum gyi spyod pa, the activity of the three doors; the Tibetan puts the doors first and the activity second, the English reverses them. legs nyes gang dang gang du 'gro mi 'gro is a full alternation, “whether they go toward whichever of the two, good or bad, or do not go”; the English keeps “whether or not” and “right or wrong” but leaves gang dang gang du and the second 'gro unrendered.

bag yod (care, heedfulness) is the one that runs skabs thams cad du, at all times. GMR expands it to “One's ability to take great care in his life”; only take great care is claimed, the rest is English scaffolding. It has two faces: it blocks ('gog) everything mi rung ba, im-proper (the negative-affix convention), and it does not stray (mi 'da' bar, rendered positively as “within the bounds”) from relying on what is rung ba, appropriate; byas is the causative that GMR renders as the second “keeps”.

Segments 35–36 — ultimate and apparent refuge: twin definitions

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
lam gyi 'grod pa mthar phyin pa'i skyabs, don dam pa'i skyabs kyi mtshan nyid
lam gyi bgrod pa mthar ma phyin skyabs, kun rdzob pa'i skyabs kyi mtshan nyid
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
The definition of an ultimate refuge is "Any refuge where the journey along the path has reached its final goal."
The definition of an apparent refuge is "Any refuge where the journey along the path has not reached its final goal."
AI NOTE — two flags on this minimal pair. (1) INPUT VARIANT: seg 35 spells 'grod pa, seg 36 spells bgrod pa — both attested spellings of the verb "traverse"; the corpus carries both and the machine links both, correcting neither. (2) ELISION: 36 drops the nominalizer chain (mthar ma phyin skyabs, not mthar ma phyin pa'i skyabs) — terse debate-manual style; GMR's English restores the full relative clause both times, so the two English lines are perfectly parallel where the Tibetan is not. "Any" and "where" are his formula glue, ∅ exponents.

Segment 37 — taking refuge: the master definition

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
yul gzhan la rang stobs kyis dpung gnyen du re ba 'cha' ba'i sems pa, skyabs 'gro'i mtshan nyid
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
The definition of taking refuge is "Any movement of the mind that acts of its own accord, and consists of hoping that some object outside of one's self will be able to render one assistance."

AI NOTE — sems pa is the Abhidharma mental factor (Skt. cetanā, standardly "intention"); GMR's "movement of the mind" is his signature rendering and repeats through every refuge definition below — six attestations on two folios make this one of the strongest machine-matched pairs in the whole reading. "Any", "some", "will be able to" are his; the Tibetan states the bare formula. The whole skeleton rang stobs kyis dpung gnyen du re ba 'cha' ba'i sems pa → "movement of the mind which acts of its own accord, and consists of hoping… will render… assistance" is the template segs 39–45 build on by swapping the la-frame target.

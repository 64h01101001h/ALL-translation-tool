AI NOTE — Caption 6 of the elephant-path chart (the nine states of shamatha). Structure: two clauses sharing one clause-final verb — "sprel 'phro ba dang , kha dog nag pa rgod pa mtshon" = "[the] monkey — distraction, and [its] black color — agitation, [these it] represents." Geshe Michael renders the one Tibetan verb as two finite English verbs, "stands for" and "represents". Only one can be licensed by mtshon, so mtshon is linked to "represents", which sits in mtshon's own clause and in the same final position. "stands for" is deliberately left UNWRAPPED — the first clause has no overt Tibetan verb of its own, and double-linking one Tibetan word to two English verbs would manufacture evidence. DIRECT PARALLEL, segment 171: "5a/glang po sems dang , kha dog nag pa bying ba mtshon" = "[5] The elephant represents your mind, and his black color symbolizes dullness." Identical frame, identical kha dog nag pa, identical single final mtshon — but the English distributes the two verbs the other way round (171 puts "represents" in the FIRST clause and "symbolizes" in the second; 172 puts "stands for" first and "represents" second). Anyone aligning 171 should therefore expect mtshon to bind to a different slot than it does here; the two captions are not parallel in their English even though they are parallel in Tibetan. 171/172 are a matched pair: elephant + black = mind + dullness; monkey + black = distraction + agitation. CAPTION VS PROSE. The prose treatment earlier in the course does NOT prepare this vocabulary. Segments 157-159 name the nine mental states and the six powers and four modes of focus, but rgod pa and bying ba appear nowhere in 155-162. The agitation/dullness pair enters the course only with this caption series (171, 172, then 177, 180, 184, 191) and only later returns to prose at 204-212. So the caption is introducing terms, not echoing them. TERM NOTES. rgod pa = "agitation" is stable across the course (177 rgod pa'i yul "the various objects that agitation focuses upon"; 184; 208; 191). "distraction", however, is shared by two different Tibetan words: 'phro ba here and at 185 (sems ... la 'phro ba, rendered verbally as "distracted"), against g-yeng ba at 163 ("give up all distraction") and 181 ("your mind is wandering"). A reverse lookup on "distraction" will pull both; they are not interchangeable — 'phro ba is the mind spilling outward onto another object, g-yeng ba the mind straying. WYLIE ODDITY. The monkey is spelled sprel here; at 191 the same animal in the same chart is spre'u ("left behind the monkey as well"). Both are attested for "monkey" but a string search on either spelling alone will miss half the chart. BOUNDARY DECISIONS. kha dog nag pa is kept as ONE d=5 span rather than split into kha dog = "color" and nag pa = "black": the members would cross, since English puts "black" before "color", which would place a child out of order inside its own phrase. The compound maps onto "black color" exactly, with nothing left over. Unwrapped English, all correct: "[6]" (caption number, matching the folio marker's apparatus status), "The", "stands", "for" (part of the unlinked English phrasal verb, not any Tibetan particle), "and" (see s5), and "his" — there is no genitive or possessive anywhere in the wylie, kha dog nag pa is bare, so "his" has no Tibetan licensor and must not be absorbed into the "black color" span. The shad and comma between dang and kha dog are punctuation and are not linked.

AI NOTE — Caption [7] of the numbered legend to the elephant-path (shamatha) chart described at §156-§166. Not linked as apparatus: the wylie's "7a/" and the English's "[7]". Wylie oddity worth recording: these markers are not folio sides — §176 has "1a0/" for [10] and §178 "1a2/" for [12], so the "a" is a slot filler sitting between digits. The marker simply restates the caption number. THE SUBSTANTIVE CORRECTION TO BOTH PROPOSALS. A and B independently agreed that "to bring your mind back to the object" is an unlicensed expansion — B called it "Geshe Michael's gloss of what the effort is for." It is not. In this very caption series, §168 renders the bare wylie "dran pa" as "Bringing the mind back to the object", §169 renders "shes bzhin" as "Watchfulness", and §158 gives "dran pa'i stobs" as "the power of bringing your mind back to the object" against "shes bzhin gyi stobs" as "the power of watchfulness". dran shes here is the standing abbreviation of that pair, and the English distributes it: "...effort needed to bring your mind back to the object, and the degree of watchfulness needed." So dran and shes each have a real exponent and are linked at d=7 (s25, s26), and the parent dran shes is null — a contiguous English span covering both members would have to swallow ", and the degree of", which is exactly the over-capture the audits flag. Both proposals' dran shes = "watchfulness" gave the whole compound only its second member's English. Term check against the prose, since the caption is meant to gloss it. sems gnas bdun pa matches §157-§158 word for word (same sems gnas + ordinal frame; §158 uses bdun pa for the seventh). But the caption's word for effort is 'bad rtsol, whereas §158 assigns the seventh and eighth states to brtson 'grus kyi stobs, "the power of effort". These are different Tibetan words sharing one English word, and they are not making the same claim: §158's brtson 'grus is the power that produces states 7-8, while the caption's 'bad rtsol is the exertion of mindfulness-and-watchfulness whose magnitude the flames depict from state 1 up to state 7. A translator should not merge them, and a concordance on "effort" will pull both. Note also that §158 assigns dran pa to states 3-4 and shes bzhin to 5-6, while the caption runs their joint effort across the whole span up to 7. Boundary rulings taken from the two proposals. (1) 'di nas is split (A) rather than kept whole as "from this point" (B): "point" has no Tibetan licensor and stays unwrapped; nas = "from" is a single occurrence and is the ablative's proper exponent. (2) che chung gi khyad par is split (A) rather than kept whole as "relative size" / "relative amount" (B): "size" and "amount" are each single occurrences and are exactly what che chung licenses, while "relative" occurs twice and is therefore unlinkable, so khyad par is null in both places. Splitting does not force "relative" onto khyad par — nothing goes onto khyad par. This also settles the two proposals' gi depth disagreement: with che chung and khyad par listed separately, gi is a space-separated syllable between two listed d=5 spans, so it is d=6, not B's d=7. A's ro at d=5 "copula" is demoted to d=6 and nulled — it is a particle, and per the reconciliation rule the doubtful link goes unwrapped. Compounds are decomposed at d=7 only where the members carry distinct sense the fused English hides (sems gnas, me lce, yod med, che chung, dran shes). 'bad rtsol and stobs shugs are left whole: they are synonym-doublets ('bad pa / rtsol ba both "exertion"; stobs / shugs both "force"), and splitting them would yield nulls recording nothing. AI NOTE — Caption 8 of the nine-stage elephant-path chart (dbu ma snying po's taming-the-elephant simile, introduced at §156). It names the second of the six powers and the mental state that power accomplishes. CROSS-REFERENCE — the caption against the prose. §158 treats the same stage in running prose: "bsam pa'i stobs kyis gnyis pa 'grub" / "The second is reached through the power of contemplating these instructions." The caption's Tibetan is the same clause with two changes: it spells out sems gnas where the prose has a bare ordinal gnyis pa, and it carries the power in a resumptive 'dis instead of stobs kyis. HGM's English tracks that: "the power of contemplating" is word-for-word identical in both places, and the caption's "the instructions" answers the prose's "these instructions" — which is the direct evidence that "instructions" here is supplied from §158, not translated from this segment. The second state itself is rgyun du 'jog pa per the §157 list, captioned separately at §175 as "2) Keeping the mind on the object with brief continuity"; §174 does not name it, it only numbers it. So: wording matches the prose treatment, with the caption compressed and the object of contemplation carried over. A TERM TAKING A NEW RENDERING. The resumptive demonstrative-plus-instrumental is rendered three different ways across the three power captions: §170 'di la brten nas → "Based on this"; §174 'dis → "You use this to"; §178 'dis → "This allows you to". Same Tibetan, three English periphrases, none of them a preposition. That is exactly why the -s is nulled rather than linked — there is no stable English exponent to point at, and each rendering distributes the case force across a different verb. WYLIE ODDITY. §174's stobs is spelled correctly; the parallel caption §178 reads "dran pa'i ltobs so", an l- for s- typo in the source keying. Flag it there, not here. Also worth knowing for the apparatus: the chart's markers run 1a/ ... 9a/ then 1a0/, 1a1/ ... 1a6/, i.e. the caption number with an 'a' infix, matching [1]–[16] in the English one for one. So "8a/" is the caption-number marker, not a folio reference — both proposals called it a folio marker, and both were wrong about that; it stays unlinked either way.

Segment 177 — the four kinds of grasping

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
dbye na, 'dod pa nye bar len pa, lta ba nye bar len pa, bdag tu smra ba nye bar len pa, tshul khrims dang brtul zhugs mchog tu 'dzin pa nye bar len pa bzhi yod
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)

AI NOTE —

A definition-by-division sentence, dbye na … bzhi yod: ‘if you divide it, there are four.’ GMR renders the frame as ‘may be divided into four types’, so yod has no separate exponent and ‘types’ is his. The four members are the four nye bar len pa (Skt. upādāna, grasping): the Tibetan carries the term four times, GMR’s English five, because he names the topic once (‘This particular grasping’) before the list. Each repetition is therefore banked inside its own compound, where it is unique, not at the word layer, where the count would be ambiguous. bdag tu smra ba is literally ‘asserting a self’; GMR expands it to ‘you profess the existence of a self- nature’, so bdag is banked at ‘self’ only — ‘nature’, ‘the existence of’, ‘where you’ and ‘mistaken forms of’ are his expansions with no Tibetan under them. The fourth compound, tshul khrims dang brtul zhugs mchog tu ’dzin pa (śīlavrata-parāmarśa), is a single term of art and is kept whole. The spelling ‘self- nature’ is GMR’s own throughout the corpus (C16:656 is a byte-identical witness), not a corruption.

Segment 28 — source line for the commentary

GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)

AI NOTE —

A source colophon. The wylie is one nominal chain: agent (rgyal ba dge 'dun grub kyis) → act of composition (brtsoms pa'i) → the composed title (mdzod Tik thar lam gsal byed) → ablative nas, 'from'. GMR reverses it, opening with an editorial subject ('The commentary to this and the verses below') and closing with the author, so the English order crosses the Tibetan at almost every link.

mdzod Tik is 'the Treasure House commentary'. GMR renders only Tik as 'commentary'; mdzod is absorbed into the deictic 'to this', which points at the Abhidharmakosha named in the previous segment, so it is left unclaimed here. The parallel colophon at C05:315 confirms the reading: 'Illumination of the Path to Freedom, a commentary to the Abhidharmakosha'.

thar lam gsal byed is literally 'the illuminator of the path to freedom'; the English keeps every element, nominalising gsal byed as 'Illumination'. Three stretches of the English have no Tibetan under them and stay unwrapped: 'to this and the verses below', the copula 'is', and 'His Holiness the First Dalai Lama (1391-1474)' — GMR's identification of the author for an English reader. The agentive kyis is unrendered; the 'by' belongs to the passive 'written by'.

Segment 30 — the sanskrit and tibetan titles

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
rgya gar skad du, aa bhi dharma ko Sha kA ri kA, bod skad du, chos mngon pa'i mdzod kyi tshig le'ur byas pa
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
In Sanskrit, the Abhidharmakosha Karika. In Tibetan, the Chu Ngunpay Dzu Kyi Tsikleur Jepa. [In English, The Treasure House of Knowledge, set in verse.]terminative; GMR fronts it as "In", which occurs three times against two du — unresolved, left unlinkedterminative; same fronted "In", unresolvedgenitive joining mdzod to tshig le'ur byas pa; GMR renders the join as the comma before "set in verse"
AI NOTE —

The title page of Vasubandhu's root text, given three times over in the English: in Tibetanised Sanskrit, in Geshe Michael's phonetics of the Tibetan (Chu Ngunpay Dzu Kyi Tsikleur Jepa), and — in his own square brackets — in English translation. The wylie carries the Tibetan title once, so only one of the English renderings can be linked to it. The links take the bracketed translation, because that is where the Tibetan words have translation value; the phonetic stretch is deliberately left unwrapped. It pairs sound to spelling, not meaning, and a d=5 link would carry it into the equivalent bank as though it were English.

The word division is GMR's own, supplied in the next segment (C05:31): Abhidharma refers to "knowledge," kosha to "treasure house," and karika to "set in verse." On the Tibetan side that is chos mngon pa → Knowledge, mdzod → Treasure House, tshig le'ur byas pa → set in verse. The English crosses the Tibetan order because Tibetan puts the possessor first (Knowledge's Treasure House) and the genitive 'i surfaces as "of".

rgya gar skad is literally "the language of India"; GMR renders it simply "Sanskrit". The articles and the three fronted occurrences of "In" are translator-supplied and carry no Tibetan underneath; the two terminative du are left unlinked rather than tied to an ambiguous "In".

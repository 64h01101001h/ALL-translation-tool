Second: agentive `'dis` governs `'grub`, with `sems gnas dgu pa` as its subject — "by this, the ninth mental state is accomplished." `sems gnas` is lexicalised but splits cleanly against GMR's "mental state": `sems` takes the attributive "mental", `gnas` the head "state". NO INVENTED ENGLISH / LEFTOVERS, both analysts concurring. "enables you to" is deliberately unwrapped: `'grub` is intransitive/resultative, there is no causative verb and no second-person pronoun in the wylie, and the "to" belongs to the English "enables … to" frame, not to a Tibetan case particle. Giving it to `'dis` or `'grub` is exactly the over-capture defect rule 6 names; `'dis` is spanned only for its demonstrative value ("which"). Both instances of "The/the" are unwrapped — articles belong to nobody, and rule 5 would bar them regardless since "the" occurs twice. "of" occurs exactly once in the segment, so s4 is unambiguous. `dgu pa` is not decomposed: `dgu` alone would have to own "nine", which is not present in the English. AI NOTE — Caption 28 of the elephant-path chart, naming the ninth and last of the nine mental states. Three spans. WALKING THE WYLIE. Everything after the marker is one lexical unit, "mnyam par 'jog pa", the standard name of samahita/equipoise: mnyam "even, level, in balance", par the terminative ("into a state of evenness"), 'jog pa "placing, setting, letting rest" — literally "placing [the mind] into evenness". It closes the same 'jog pa family that opens the list (sems 'jog pa, rgyun du 'jog pa, glan de 'jog pa, nye bar 'jog pa at C03:157): the nine begin and end with the verb of placing, with the four byed pa states (dul bar, zhi bar, rnam par zhi bar, rtse gcig tu) in between. WHY "mnyam" IS NULLED RATHER THAN GLOSSED. One proposal assigned "deep meditation" to "mnyam par". That is over-capture. "mnyam par" on its own means "evenly / in equipoise"; the state-noun reading arises only from the whole compound, and "deep" is a free intensifier with no Tibetan exponent at all. So "deep meditation" is covered by the parent span alone and by no child — that is intended, not an omission. "'jog pa" takes only the English verbal head, "Reaching". The bound terminative "par" is left out entirely: it carries no English, and an omitted null morpheme costs nothing. THE HEAD MARKER — correcting a shared assumption. Both drafts read "2a8/" as damaged text whose true reading is "28/", citing the project's a-insertion pattern. Checking every caption in the chart refutes that here. The markers run 1a/ 2a/ … 9a/, then 1a0/ 1a1/ … 1a9/, then 2a0/ … 2a9/, then 3a0/ … 3a3/, and they map one-to-one onto GMR's brackets [1] … [33]. The "a" is the convention's own separator, present in all 33, not a collision. So "2a8/" is regular, and "[28]" is simply that marker restated in English. Both stay unspanned as locators — nothing in them translates the Tibetan words — but neither should be flagged as corrupt. (While sweeping the markers: C03:187 reads "[17] 5) Controlling the mind" where the running order wants [21], and [17] already sits on C03:183; C03:185 carries no bracket at all, and [19] never appears. GMR's numbering, verbatim, untouched — but a translator navigating by caption number should know the index is not injective in this chart.) "9)" IS UNWRAPPED, deliberately. The Tibetan carries no numeral — no dgu, no dgu pa. The stage number comes from the chart's running order, not from this wylie. Contrast the prose at C03:211, which does write "dgu pa ni" and there the numeral is licensed. Leftover English stays unwrapped. TERMINOLOGY DIVERGENCE, and it is the mirror image of the stage-1 case in the brief. Identical wylie, mnyam par 'jog pa, in four places: - this chart caption: "Reaching deep meditation" - the list at C03:157: "and reaching deep meditation" - the prose at C03:201: "(9) achieving equilibrium" - the prose at C03:211: 'the ninth state of meditation, called "achieving equilibrium"' Stage 1 splits on exactly the same seam — chart/list "setting the mind on the object" (C03:157, C03:167) versus prose "placing the mind on the object" (C03:201, C03:202). So the divergence is systematic across the whole nine-state list, not a one-off: the caption/list layer and the Lam-rim chen-mo prose layer were rendered on separate passes. Both are preserved verbatim by project rule; neither is normalized toward the other. Practical consequence: "equilibrium" is the literal reading of mnyam par and "deep meditation" the interpretive one, and a search on either English string alone will miss half the occurrences of this term. A SECOND, FINER DIVERGENCE, on the verb itself. 'jog pa heads both stage 1 (sems 'jog pa) and stage 9 (mnyam par 'jog pa). At stage 1 GMR renders it "setting" or "placing"; at stage 9 he renders it "Reaching" here and "achieving" in the prose — never "setting" or "placing". The verb's English exponent shifts with the compound it heads, which is why s3 owns "Reaching" and none of the stage-1 wordings. WYLIE ODDITY worth banking. The stage-3 name is spelled "glan de 'jog pa" in the list at C03:157 and in the caption at C03:179, but "glan te 'jog pa" in the prose at C03:201. Same de/te seam as the register split above, and another case where a single-spelling search under-returns. NEIGHBOURS. C03:193 supplies the power that reaches this state ("yongs su 'bris pa'i stobs so, , 'dis sems gnas dgu pa 'grub" / "The power of complete habituation, which enables you to reach the ninth mental state"); C03:192 is stage 8 ("rtse gcig tu byed pa" / "8) Attaining single-pointedness", where byed pa likewise surfaces as an English gerund of accomplishment rather than as "doing"); C03:195 is the result ("zhi gnas thob pa" / "Achieving quietude [shamata]").

AI NOTE — Caption 29 of the elephant-path chart. It is not itself one of the nine mental states — it names their fruit — and it sits immediately after 194, the caption for the ninth state, and before the two shin sbyangs captions at 196-197. Two units in the wylie: the object zhi gnas, then the verbal noun thob pa. The English inverts that order ("Achieving quietude"), but the spans follow the wylie, so the forward cursor is satisfied — s1 at the head, s4 after it. zhi gnas is shamatha: zhi, "pacified/at peace," plus gnas, "to abide" — the mind that stays put because it has been quieted. GMR does not translate the two members separately, so both are depth-7 nulls under the parent "zhi gnas" (each a clean substring of it, each with no English to claim). thob pa is "obtain/attain" nominalized; "Achieving" carries verb and nominalization together. The nominalizer "pa" is deliberately NOT given its own span. It sits on the d=6/d=7 boundary — space-separated in the wylie, yet bound to thob as part of the verbal noun — and it licenses only the "-ing" of "Achieving," which cannot be separated without breaking the word. Per the standing guidance an omitted null morpheme costs nothing while a mis-depthed one costs the page, so it is left out. Bare "thob" is likewise unspanned; the parent owns "Achieving" whole. LEFT UNWRAPPED, deliberately: - "2a9/" — head marker, never spanned. - "[29]" — the chart's caption number. No Tibetan numeral licenses it (numerals belong to gcig/gnyis/gsum, and there is none here). - "[shamata]" — GMR's own bracketed Sanskrit equivalent, an editorial insertion with no wylie exponent. zhi gnas is already discharged by "quietude"; claiming "quietude [shamata]" for one span would be over-capture. TERMINOLOGY. 1. thob pa vs grub pa — a real crossing, and the one a translator should see. Across C03 prose the verb of attainment for zhi gnas is grub/sgrub pa, and GMR renders it with "achieve": "zhi gnas grub pa'i rjes su" = "after achieving quietude" (161), "zhi gnas grub nas" = "once you have attained quietude" and "zhi gnas sgrub pa" = "attaining the state of quietude" (152). Meanwhile thob pa in that same prose is rendered "reach" or "attain": "thob pa" = "you reach an unshakeable state" (160), "thob pa na" = "reach an extraordinary level" and "thob pa yin la" = "you have attained the special insight" (161). Here in the caption the verb is thob pa and the English is "Achieving" — the word prose reserves for grub pa. So the caption register borrows the prose word for the other verb. Preserved, not normalized. 2. The caption-vs-prose divergence in the state names lands one segment earlier and is visible from here. Caption 194, "mnyam par 'jog pa," reads "[28] 9) Reaching deep meditation." The identical Tibetan is rendered "achieving equilibrium" twice in the prose — item (9) of the list at C03:201, and again at C03:211 ("called 'achieving equilibrium'"). Segment 195 is the caption that immediately follows that ninth state and names its result, so the two renderings sit adjacent across the chart-to-prose seam. Preserved, not reconciled. 3. "quietude" itself is rock-stable across the course — never "calm abiding," never "tranquility." Only the Sanskrit apparatus varies, and it varies by register: square brackets in the chart captions (here, and 198 "quietude [shamata]" / "Special insight [vipashyana]"), parentheses in prose (152 "quietude (shamata)", 286 "(shamata, or insight)"), and bare at 160/161 and 510 ("meditative quietude"). That punctuation-level variation in GMR's own hand is one more reason the tag stays outside the span rather than being folded in and normalized. Confirmed in the source. If 185 is aligned, its quietude span will not match the form used everywhere else in the course. Rule 5 never fired — no function-word links.

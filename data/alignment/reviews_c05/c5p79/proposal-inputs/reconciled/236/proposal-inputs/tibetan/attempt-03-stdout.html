Segment 236 — essence and mistaken perception

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
rang gi ngo bo nyid kyis grub na gnas lugs su grub par 'gyur la, de'i tshe 'khrul pa log pa na gsal du 'gro dgos kyi, ldog par mi 'gyur ro, , zhes pa'i don no
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
The meaning of the lines is as follows. If things existed in their very essence, then they would exist as some independent reality. If this were so, then when you finally stopped your mistaken states of mind, they would never disappear, although they should have.
AI NOTE — Provisional machine alignment by Codex (Tibetan-first independent proposal); no human semantic approval. gnas lugs is attested in the original HGM glossary as independent reality; ngo bo nyid as very essence. ldog is locally the cessation/disappearance predicate and mi its negation. Omit gsal du 'gro dgos kyi and the English although they should have: the Tibetan expects becoming clear, whereas the English ellipsis appears to repeat disappear. Omission marks unresolved or contradictory correspondence, not an asserted null. Omit the restructured de'i tshe and zhes pa'i frame; don retains meaning. English exist is the whole word after would, not a substring of existed. All source text remains verbatim.

spyir dben pa'i gnas su sgrub pa byed lo byas kyang gdams ngag tshang la ma nor ba la nyams len rdo rus thug pa zhig byed pa med na, phal cher ngal ba don med nyid las ma 'das pa yin te, rje mid las kyang

GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Generally speaking a lot of us go off to some deserted place with the notion that we're going to do some deep practice there.byas→folded But unless you go with some instruction in hand that is really complete and totally correct, and unless you work to dig down to its core,nyams len→within "you work to dig" most of what you do won't be much more than simple wasted effort. As Lord Milarepa once said,
The point: if you don't meditate on advices passed down ear to ear; The place: you can sit in a mountain cave, but only to torture yourself.74zhes gsungs→the couplet's close

Segment 122 — the translators, and water traced to snow

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
sngar gyi lo tshA ba chen po dag gis kyang dka' spyad du mas thag ring rgya gar gyi yul nas chos tshad ldan bod 'dir gdan drangs pa yang bod kyi chos log de tshos go ma chod pa red, des na chu 'go'i phugs gangs la thug dgos pa bzhin du rang gis nyams su len bya'i chos kyi thugs de yang ma nor ba bstan pa'i bdag po ston pa sangs rgyas la thug pa zhig dgos
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Now the master translators of old undertook a great many hardships, journeying afar to the land of India to bear authentic dharma teachings back here to Tibet. But those in Tibet who followed a mistaken path couldn't live up to them at all. Really good water should at the end of ends trace back to some pure snow. Just so, whatever dharma we choose to practice should have its ultimate origin in something infallible: in the very Lord of the Word, in the Teacher, in the Buddha.thug pa→within "have its…origin in"
AI NOTE — (1) lo tshA ba — the EIGHTH ACIP-Sanskrit token (long ā). (2) DIRECTION UNPACKED: the Tibetan says the translators brought dharma FROM (nas) far India; the English walks both legs of the journey ("journeying afar TO the land of India to bear… BACK here to Tibet") — and bod 'dir's "here" is kept: Pabongka's Lhasa deixis (cf. seg 100's "here in Tibet"). (3) SOFTENING POLICY CONFIRMED: chos log ("wrong-dharma [people]" — the polemical word for heretics) → "those in Tibet who followed a mistaken path" — filed beside seg 115's supplied "They are not at fault": a consistent editorial gentling of the polemic. (4) THE WATER PROVERB: chu 'go'i phugs gangs la thug → "Really good water should at the end of ends trace back to some pure snow" — phugs → "at the end of ends" (a coined English idiom), "pure" supplied. (5) CORPUS TYPO #7: chos kyi thugs for chos kyi KHUNGS ("origin" — seg 114's khungs → "ultimate origin", and this English says exactly "its ultimate origin") — kept verbatim, filed. (6) TRIPLE APPOSITION kept with anaphora: bstan pa'i bdag po / ston pa / sangs rgyas → "in the very Lord of the Word, in the Teacher, in the Buddha" — bstan pa → "the Word", joining bka' in the Word-family. (7) de tshos — colloquial plural #2; red again: the zin-bris voice.

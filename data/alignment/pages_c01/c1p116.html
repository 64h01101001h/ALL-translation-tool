Segment 383 — the refrain

TIBETAN (WYLIE)

GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Let me realize that these things are labelled, creations of concepts; That they can exist only in dependence on a basis to receive a name and someone to give the name; That they occur in dependence on many other factors; That they don't exist out there, on their own.yin pa→copularnams (plural)→"these things"

Segment 384 — the examination test

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
dgag bya'i zhen yul yang zhen yul de yod med brtag pa'i skabs su phar btags pa'i nga de min pa'i nga kho rang gi ngos nas tshur grub pa lta bu zhig 'char bas, nga de lus sems la brten nas btags pa tsam ma yin par, lus sems gnyis kyi steng nas tshur grub pa de zhen yul yin
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Let's talk then about the thing we will see does not exist, in terms of the object we grasp. When we start to examine whether it exists or not, the image that comes to our mind is not the "me" that we have created with our labels, but rather some "me" that looks like it exists out there, on his own. The object we grasp therefore consists not of the "me" which is nothing but a label applied to our body and mind, but rather of the "me" which seems to exist out there on its own, on top of the body and the mind together.zhen yul de (recap)→within "it"brten nas→within "applied to"yang→"then"yin→copula
AI NOTE — (1) THE EXAMINATION TEST: yod med brtag pa → "examine whether it exists or not" — under examination the labelled me is NOT what appears; 'char ba → "the image that comes to our mind" (the arising-verb; cf. 379 shar). (2) DOUBLED STRUCTURE: the English states the point twice (image version / object version), matching the Tibetan's two clauses — not…but-rather architecture in both. (3) zhen yul ×3 → "the object we grasp" — the grid term stable. (4) min pa'i nga → 'not the "me"…but rather some "me"'.

Segment 385 — the rope-snake

TIBETAN (WYLIE)
dper na thag khra la mun pa babs pa mthong ba na dang por thag khra la sbrul lo snyam du phar btags, de rjes phar btags pa de brjed nas thag khra'i ngos nas sbrul tshur grub par snang bas de ltar snang ba de dgag bya ma yin, de ltar snang ba ltar du grub par 'dzin pa'i zhen yul de dgag bya yin pa bzhin du
GESHE MICHAEL'S ENGLISH (VERBATIM)
Let's say for example that dusk has fallen and you see some piece of rope with a checkered pattern. At first you put a name onto it and think to yourself, "Oh my! A snake!" After that you forget it was you who put the name on it and it starts to look like a snake out there on its own. The way that it looks just then is not what we want to see doesn't exist. Rather what we want to deny is what we grasp: that the thing we hold could really exist the way it looks to us to exist.thag khra (#2)→within "onto it"yin pa→copulabzhin du (comparative tail)→carries into the next segment
AI NOTE — (1) THE ROPE-SNAKE — the tradition's central projection parable: thag khra → "rope with a checkered pattern" (khra = checkered); mun pa babs pa → "dusk has fallen"; brjed nas → "you forget" — the FORGETTING step that turns label into appearance. (2) THE SUPPLIED EXCLAMATION: sbrul lo snyam du → 'think to yourself, "Oh my! A snake!"' — the inner speech dramatized with supplied "Oh my!". (3) The 382 grid APPLIED: snang ba ≠ dgag bya (the look is not denied), zhen yul = dgag bya (the grasped mode is) — dgag bya renders #10–11. (4) thag khra'i ngos nas → "on its own" — the rope's own side. (5) bzhin du (comparative tail) carries into the next segment.

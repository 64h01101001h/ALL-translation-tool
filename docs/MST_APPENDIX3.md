## The taxonomy (the answer to the register-design question)

Tibetan politeness has **four marked levels** beyond ordinary:

| Tibetan name | Term | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| spyir btang zhe sa | general honorific | H |
| zhe sa mtho po | **high honorific** | HH |
| dma' sa bzung ba'i zhe sa | humilific (Beyer's term) | h |
| phyogs gnyis kyi zhe sa | double honorific | hH |

- **H/HH honor the SUBJECT of the action**; HH is "especially [for]
  high lamas and State dignitaries."
- **The humilific is how respect flows from the first person**: the
  honorific is *never* used of oneself — one humbles one's own verbs
  toward the person the action is directed to.
- **Double honorific**: subject and recipient both respected
  (phul gnang, bka' mol zhus gnang).
- Crucially: "there are only a FEW high and double honorifics, and
  they are not often used" — which reconciles Goldstein's two-level
  written model (h./nh.) with Adam's high-honorific intuition: the
  HH tier is real, small, and precious — exactly the kind of curated
  list Geshe Michael can rule on item by item.

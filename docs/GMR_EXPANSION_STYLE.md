# Geshe Michael's expansion style — one Tibetan term, several English terms

*Adam raised this 2026-08-28: that Geshe Michael will take a single Tibetan
term and gloss it with two, three or more English terms. Measured against the
layer below. It is real, it is measurable, and it is a **different phenomenon**
from the multiple-renderings-across-occurrences already documented in
`PRESERVE_ALL_RENDERINGS.md` and `TWO_NAMING_BANDS_C03.md`.*

Those documents describe variation **between** occurrences — the same term
rendered differently in different places. This is variation **within** one
occurrence: a single Tibetan unit carried by several English words at once.

## The expansion ratio

English words per Tibetan syllable, across all 8,308 banked renderings:

| key length | n | mean English words per syllable |
|---|---|---|
| 1 syllable | 1,803 | **1.78** |
| 2 syllables | 4,382 | 1.20 |
| 3 syllables | 1,087 | 1.06 |
| 4 syllables | 716 | 0.99 |
| 5 syllables | 320 | 0.88 |

**The shorter the Tibetan, the more English it takes.** A single syllable
averages nearly two English words; by five syllables the ratio has inverted
and English is more compact than the Tibetan. That is the signature of
explication: short technical terms carry the most unpacking.

## The proof it is style, not compound structure

The obvious objection to any doublet — `bdag gzhan` → "self and others" — is
that the Tibetan compound genuinely has two members, so the English pair is
just faithful.

The decisive test is a key that takes **both** shapes. **30 keys do.**

| key | as a single word | as a doublet |
|---|---|---|
| `bar chad` | "obstacle" | "any interruptions or obstacles" |
| `chags` | "attached", "craving" | "desire and attachment" |
| `btsal` | "seek" | "go and find" |
| `mtha' dag` | "all", "entire" | "each and every" |
| `gcig pu` | "just", "single" | "the one and only" |
| `nyo tshong` | "business" | "buying or selling something" |
| `khyer ba` | "swept" | "push it back and forth" |
| `che chung` | "amount", "size" | "great or small" |

The same Tibetan, in the same corpus, taking one word here and a paired gloss
there. **The pairing is the translator's choice, not the compound's structure.**

## Explication proper — one syllable unpacked into a clause

Beyond doublets, single syllables take whole descriptive phrases:

| key | rendering | where |
|---|---|---|
| `sgrib` | "all the things that block our spiritual progress" | C01:452 |
| `chad` | "the extreme that we call \"thinking things have stopped\"" | C01:417 |
| `rtag` | "fall into the extreme of \"thinking things are permanent\"" | C01:417 |
| `bsangs` | "the Elimination Ritual for getting rid of bad spirits" | C01:318 |

`sgrib` is verified: the wylie is `sdig sgrib`, `sdig` takes "our past bad
deeds", and `sgrib` alone takes the nine-word phrase. This is a translator
writing for students who need the concept unpacked, not a lexicographer
finding an equivalent — which is exactly what the ACIP teaching tradition
produces.

## THE HONEST CAVEAT — do not read this table as all style

**Some of the widest expansions are probably defects, not technique.**
Span over-capture is the dominant measured defect across three independent
audits, and its signature looks *identical* to explication from the outside:
a short key holding a long English span.

`tshad` appears in this list twice and was independently flagged as SUSPECT
over-capture by the stratified audit — the span had swallowed `skyes pa`.
So the same measurement that demonstrates the style also contains its
counterfeits, and **only reading the segment tells them apart.**

The doublet evidence above is not vulnerable to this: a key that takes both
"obstacle" and "any interruptions or obstacles" demonstrates the style
regardless of whether any individual long span is over-captured.

## Why this matters for the dictionary

1. **A reverse index will behave oddly.** "obstacle" and "any interruptions or
   obstacles" both resolve to `bar chad`. English lookup needs to handle a
   headword whose equivalents range from one word to nine.
2. **It compounds the entry-card cap.** Doublets and expansions are separate
   renderings, so an expanding term burns through the four-rendering display
   limit faster than a stable one. See `PRESERVE_ALL_RENDERINGS.md`.
3. **Some renderings are definitions, not equivalents.** A translator looking
   up `sgrib` gets "all the things that block our spiritual progress" — true,
   useful, and not something to substitute into a sentence. The app should
   probably distinguish an *equivalent* from an *explication*, and the layer
   currently marks no such difference.

Point 3 is a design question for Adam, not something to decide here.

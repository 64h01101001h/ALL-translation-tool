# The C03 audit, and what it says about every earlier figure

*2026-08-28. 72 (key, rendering, citation) triples drawn uniformly at random
from the 1,233 C03 triples, seed 20260831, generated by script with the full
verbatim segment AND every sibling span on the page attached. Every flagged
item was then handed to a separate skeptic instructed to refute it and to
default to refuted when uncertain.*

## The headline is not the defect rate

    72 audited -> 3 flagged by the first pass -> 1 survived refutation

    confirmed defect rate  1.4%   (Wilson 95% CI 0.2% - 7.5%)
    first-pass flag rate   4.2%   (Wilson 95% CI 1.4% - 11.5%)

At n=72 a single defect cannot distinguish "one bad triple in 500" from "one
in 13". The interval is the finding; the point estimate is nearly content-free.

## The recalibration — and it applies to a number I reported

**The refute pass cut the reportable rate by two-thirds: 3 flags became 1
defect. Precision of the first pass was 33%.**

The earlier layer-wide figure — **15.0% any-defect (12 of 80)** — was measured
**without a refute pass.** Its honest comparator here is not 1.4% but the
first-pass flag rate of 4.2%.

So the correct reading of that 15.0% is: *it is a first-pass flag rate, and on
this layer roughly two thirds of first-pass flags do not survive an adversarial
second look.* **Every audit figure this project has reported without a refute
pass should be assumed inflated by about that factor until re-run.** That
includes the stratified 18.3% as well.

I am not restating 15.0% as 5%. The confounds below forbid it. I am saying the
number was measured with a method now known to over-report, and it carries that
caveat from here on.

## Why C03 cannot be called "cleaner" than the rest

The 4.2% and 15.0% intervals overlap (8.8–11.5%); a two-proportion test gives
z≈2.2, p≈0.03 — marginal and fragile. Three confounds, none quantified:

1. **Non-independence.** The layer-wide sample plausibly *included* C03 triples.
2. **Category drift.** "Any-defect" in the earlier pass may not be counting the
   same thing this pass flagged.
3. **Auditor strictness.** A 4.2%-vs-15.0% gap sits well inside the range that
   prompt and auditor identity alone can produce.

**Conclusion: no claim that C03 is better or worse than the layer.** What is
established is the effect of the refute pass, which is a fact about the
*method*, measured within a single consistent run.

## The confirmed defect, and its paired repair

**C03:158.** `bsam pa'i stobs kyis` was banked as *"The second is reached
through the power of contemplating"* — swallowing a whole neighbouring clause.
The segment is a rigid parallel list and its other members prove the boundary:
`thos pa'i stobs kyis` owns only "through the power of learning", with the
achievement clause on its own span.

The repair had to be **paired**, which the first pass missed and the skeptic
caught: trimming w5 alone would strand "The second is reached" with no owner,
because the neighbour `gnyis pa 'grub` had been handed the wrong English
("these instructions"). Both were fixed:

    gnyis pa 'grub        -> "The second is reached"
    bsam pa'i stobs kyis  -> "through the power of contemplating"
    "these instructions"  -> unwrapped; GMR supplies it, the wylie has no
                             object in this clause

The two power-phrases are now exactly parallel, which is the check that
proves it.

## What the false accusations reveal — a usable finding

Both refuted claims failed the *same* way, and it was **not** a semantic
failure. In both, the auditor's reading of the Tibetan was right and only the
boundary claim was wrong. Three checks it had the means to run and did not:

- **Stranding check.** The proposed trim left English with no owner.
- **Disposition check.** It read `english: null` on a neighbour and concluded
  the material was unowned — the record carried a ∅-absorption note naming
  the exact carrier phrase. *It read a field instead of reading the record.*
- **Convention check.** It flagged the mildest of five sibling spans showing
  the same pattern, implying four edits it never proposed.

**All three flags — including the survivor — proposed incomplete repairs.**
So: the first pass detects anomalies acceptably and specifies remedies badly.
**Treat `corrected_span` as a pointer, never as a patch.** That is now standing
practice.

The two-vocabulary phenomenon caused **zero** false accusations here; it was
raised and correctly dismissed in the survivor's adjudication. With three
flags that is no evidence it never will.

## An honest limit on the mechanism finding

All three flags were span-over-capture, nominally the third consecutive
over-capture-dominant result. **But a distribution over n=1 is not a
distribution, and the auditors were primed for that mechanism by the brief.**
The correct statement is that this audit found no evidence of any other
mechanism *and was not built to*. Mispairings, dropped spans, and
wrong-citation defects remain effectively unmeasured in C03.

## Next

Not more sampling — another 72 triples still would not separate 1% from 7%.
The auditors proposed a deterministic sweep over all 1,233 C03 triples for the
signature that produced the confirmed defect: **displaced clauses in parallel
lists**, checked against the closed morpheme set `dang po / gnyis pa / gsum pa
/ bzhi pa / 'grub / thob`. That is narrow, checkable, and aimed at a mechanism
actually observed rather than at a surface feature — which distinguishes it
from the three gates already falsified in `GATE_TRIALS.md`. It gets the same
treatment: criteria fixed before the run.

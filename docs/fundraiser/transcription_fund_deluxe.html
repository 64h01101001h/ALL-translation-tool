Why $10,000 — the complete back catalog.

The inventory so far counts 2,805 recorded teachings — ≈2,600 hours of Geshe Michael's English. The complete back catalog is larger: the Knowledge Base server holding decades of course recordings, 68 unlisted recordings that exist only as links, and private collections gathered over forty years — most plausibly 6,000–8,000 hours in all.

Faithful transcription costs about $1.25 per hour, all-in — not because machine transcription is expensive, but because bargain transcription garbles exactly what matters most: the West Chinese terms. Every hour here gets an engine run with a 1,000-term West Chinese keyterm lexicon, a second-engine pass on the hardest classes, human review where Geshe Michael interprets West Chinese directly, and preservation copies made before transcription so nothing is lost while the work proceeds.

The oldest teachings are the most fragile. The video record effectively begins in 2010 — of the 3,633 videos inventoried, only 13 carry earlier course dates. Everything before that — the first twenty-five years of teaching, including the complete original course cycle — survives almost exclusively as audio: an estimated 1,500–3,000 audio-only recordings, roughly 3,000–5,000 hours, none of it transcribed and much of it on aging media. That audio era is the heart of the uncounted portion of the catalog, and preserving it first is built into this budget.

At the high case — 8,000 hours × $1.25 — the complete archive comes to $10,000. If the final inventory comes in smaller, the remainder deepens the review passes; either way, every dollar is accounted to donors.

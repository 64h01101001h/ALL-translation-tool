## Trust verdict: TRUSTED — the highest authority of our three books

Goldstein (Case Western) is the standard modern lexicographer of
Tibetan (author of the New Tibetan-English Dictionary of Modern
Tibetan); UC Press, 1991; the book remains THE reference course for
the modern **literary** register — the register closest to our
corpus of the three books mined this weekend (Hackett = classical
lexicon; Choephel = modern colloquial; Goldstein = modern literary).

Access note: the archive.org item is an open upload of a copyrighted
UC Press book (unlike the loan-gated Manual of Standard Tibetan).
Held as an in-house research copy at Adam's direction; the standing
discipline applies — facts with citation only, nothing verbatim ships.

## The load-bearing finding: written honorifics are TWO-level

§5.8 (p.95): *"Although spoken Tibetan has several honorific levels,
written Tibetan can be conceived of as using just two levels: an
honorific (h.) and non-honorific (nh.)."* This bears directly on the
high-honorific register design: the multi-level system (ordinary /
honorific / high honorific) is a SPOKEN phenomenon; for the literary
corpus a two-level model with marked exceptions may be the honest
default. **Flagged for Geshe Michael's ruling** — his usage is the
binding authority, but Goldstein is the strongest reference datum yet
on the question.

  nominals (deb → phyag deb); verb + gnang (or mdzad) honorificizes
  any verb; a phrase becomes honorific through its VERB even when the

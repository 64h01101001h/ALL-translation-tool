# Two complete vocabularies for the nine mental states

*Verified against the spine 2026-08-28, during the C03 alignment campaign.
Every string below is Geshe Michael Roach's own English, quoted verbatim from
`corpus_segments`.*

Course 3 names the nine states of meditative concentration **three times**: a
list, a set of chart captions, and a prose treatment. The list and the captions
agree. **The prose uses a different English vocabulary for eight of the nine.**

| # | Band A — the list (C03:157) and captions | Band B — the prose (C03:202–211) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | setting the mind on the object | placing the mind on the object |
| 2 | keeping the mind on the object with brief continuity | placing the mind on the object with some continuity |
| 3 | keeping the mind on the object with patches where you lose the object | placing the mind on the object and patching the gaps |
| 4 | maintaining the mind tightly on the object | placing the mind on the object closely |
| 5 | controlling the mind | **controlling the mind** |
| 6 | quieting the mind | pacifying the mind |
| 7 | completely quieting the mind | pacifying the mind totally |
| 8 | attaining single-pointedness | making the mind single-pointed |
| 9 | reaching deep meditation | achieving equilibrium |

**Eight of nine differ. State 5 is identical in both bands** — the one place
the two vocabularies touch.

## Why this matters more than a curiosity

The Tibetan is the *same in both bands*. `sems 'jog pa` is "setting the mind on
the object" in one place and "placing the mind on the object" in another;
`zhi bar byed pa` is "quieting" here and "pacifying" there. Nothing in the
source distinguishes them. **The variation is the translator's, not the text's.**

That makes this the cleanest available demonstration of Adam's
preserve-all-renderings ruling (2026-08-28):

> "The Tibetan should have and will have multiple equivalents, multiple
> renderings for the same Tibetan term or phrase or clause. We want to preserve
> all of the glosses … to give the translator a more robust way and more
> context."

A dictionary that picks one equivalent per headword must destroy one of these
two bands. Neither is wrong. Both are Geshe Michael's. And a translator meeting
`rnam par zhi bar byed pa` in a new text is materially better served by seeing
both "completely quieting the mind" and "pacifying the mind totally" than by
seeing whichever one happened to be recorded first.

## Consequence for the entry card

This is the concrete cost of the card cap documented in
`PRESERVE_ALL_RENDERINGS.md`. Any headword naming one of these states carries
at least two renderings before the rest of the corpus is counted; `'jog pa`
already holds four. A card that shows four and says "…and N more" as dead text
will, for exactly these terms, show one band and silently hide the other.

## Method note

This table was first drafted from truncated preview text and got state 8 wrong
("making the mind single" for "making the mind single-pointed"). It was then
re-derived by querying every segment in full and extracting the `called "…"`
phrase directly. **Recorded because the first version was plausible, readable,
and wrong** — the same failure mode as the reconciler that contradicted the
folio finding from three neighbours an hour earlier. Truncated evidence is not
evidence.

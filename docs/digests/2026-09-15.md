# Diamond Cutter Translation Tool Daily Digest #8 — Tuesday, September 15 2026

*Covers Tuesday and the small hours of Wednesday.*

**Headline.** I asked for a review of the parts of the tool nobody had ever
reviewed. It found that the phone app was teaching students the *sound* of a
Tibetan prayer as though it were Geshe Michael's English — under a green badge
that said, in so many words, that the answer was Geshe Michael's own
translation. One card,
shipping, in every copy. That is fixed, along with thirty-five other defects
found across two reviews, and the checks that would have caught it now exist.

## What got done

### The phone was presenting a chant as a translation

The reading-order exercise shows a line of Tibetan cut into pieces and asks the
student to put the English in order. Card C02:21 offered five answer words —
*kyabne, konchok, sumla, chupa, bul*. Those are not English. They are how the
Tibetan sounds, printed so a student can chant along. Above them sat a badge
reading **"ATTESTED — the key is Geshe Michael's own English for this very
span"**, and below, on a correct answer, **"That is Geshe Michael's order."**

We had caught this exact thing before. There is a guard in the builder, written
after twenty-three cards were found doing it. The guard checks the whole
passage — and the whole passage here reads *"kyabne konchok sumla chupa bul. A
Buddhist Grace I offer this To the Teacher…"*, transliteration followed by its
translation. It has ordinary English words in it, so it passed. The five pieces
underneath, which are what the student actually reads as the answer, were never
checked at all. They are now, judged together rather than one word at a time.

### Three more of the same kind, all shipping

- A word's Tibetan spelling was being **manufactured** on 440 cards. The
  spelling `bdag` came out as a word that does not exist, because the wrong one
  of two alphabets was used to draw it — and on the vocabulary card that
  fabricated spelling sat directly above the same word spelled correctly.
- **Eight of Geshe Michael's recorded renderings shipped as a single
  backslash**, printed under the heading "Everything Geshe Michael has been
  recorded writing for it". Any rendering containing a quotation mark was cut
  off at the quote.
- The **C13 passages we already know pair Tibetan with the wrong English** were
  being refused in one exercise and offered freely in six others.

Each of those had a note in our own files saying the problem was known. One
said, of the alphabet bug, *"It was fixed there and not here."*

### "Everything Geshe Michael has been recorded writing" was not everything

The second-thought exercise lists the renderings Geshe Michael has used for a
word and asks how many the student can name first. It said it was showing
everything. It was showing at most twelve. For `'bras bu` there are nineteen,
so a student who named one of the missing seven was told by a line claiming
completeness that it was not one of them. Fifteen of the three hundred cards
were affected. The card now says which it is showing.

### On the desktop: a machine's guess wearing Geshe Michael's name

The review of the desktop found the same fault I caught on screen two days ago,
in the one place the earlier fix could not reach — the index every pane uses
threw away Geshe Michael's glossary entry before anything could rank it. For
*mandala* the card said there was **no English of Geshe Michael's own at all** for a word
Geshe Michael has glossed. Absence of that English has to mean absence, not a
lost race.

A second one: the Trainer's grammar layer printed machine-matched English in
plain grey with no mark, while the same screen labelled the identical gloss
PROVISIONAL forty lines above.

### Work that could be lost, and was not being reported

Three separate places where the program knew a write had failed and said it had
succeeded. Saving a document **emptied the file first**, so every honest "could
not be written" was reporting on a file it had already destroyed. The shared
proposal queue was rewritten in place, so a failed write left a shorter queue
that reloads perfectly and silently. And the Versions window was matched to a
document by filename only — two files with the same name in different folders
shared one window, and Restore could overwrite the wrong one.

### Naming

Geshe Michael is named rather than pronouned now, across the desktop, the phone
and the records: 102 strings in the application, 61 lines in the documents, 2 on
the phone. Of the 123 candidate lines in the documents, only 61 were Geshe
Michael's — the
rest were inside verbatim transcripts of Geshe Michael's own classes, where
the pronouns
belong to whoever Geshe Michael was talking about, or referred to Preston,
Wilson,
Milarepa, the user, or me. Those were left exactly as they are.

### Four checks that did not exist

Every one of them found something the day it was written:

- one that keeps the naming rule without a blind find-and-replace
- one that **measures** colour contrast rather than quoting it in a comment —
  our colour file claimed a test existed that did not, and in night mode the
  badges saying whose English you were reading were the least readable marks on
  the page
- one that checks the phone's data file against its own description of itself
- two existing checks widened from one file to all eleven

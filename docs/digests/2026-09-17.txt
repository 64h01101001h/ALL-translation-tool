# Diamond Cutter Translation Tool Daily Digest #9 --- Thursday, September 17 2026

*Covers Wednesday the 16th and Thursday the 17th. Two days in one
digest: the 16th's work went uncovered while the reading below was
running.*

**Headline.** I had two of Tony Duff's grammar books read cover to cover
against our software --- 1,082 pages --- and it found four real faults
in the engines, three of which were the kind nobody can see: the program
produced confident, plausible Tibetan and was wrong. All four are fixed,
and the alignment of Course 5 has started again.

## What got done

### Reading the grammar books against our own program

I asked for Tony Duff's *Standard Tibetan Grammar*, both volumes, to be
read in full and every point in them checked against what our software
actually does. Every page was read --- not sampled --- and every claim
was re-checked by a second reader who was told to doubt it. **That
second pass threw out 177 claims**, which is the number I trust the
exercise by.

It confirmed 647 places where our program already does the right thing,
and 41 where it does not. Four of those were faults and are fixed:

- The mark that tells a reader a Sanskrit word is present had stopped
  being produced at all. It was still in our tables; a fix I made the
  day before had made it unreachable. **That one was mine, from the
  previous day, and nothing in 124 automatic checks noticed.**
- A Sanskrit letter was being drawn as a character that does not exist
  in the alphabet. 1,267 dictionary entries.
- A seed syllable used in prayer, *bhrūṃ*, was coming back without the
  mark over it, and the program reported success.
- The seed mantra *oṃ āḥ hūṃ* would not render its middle syllable. The
  other two had always worked.

**Three of those four were silent** --- the program did not flag
anything, it simply produced something wrong that looked right. That is
the finding behind the finding, and three new automatic checks now exist
because of it.

### Thirty-seven things are not faults, and they are now five questions

The other 37 are places where Duff describes Tibetan one way and our
software models it another. Nothing is broken; something is unmodelled,
and whether to model it is a decision rather than a repair. Left as a
list of 37 they read as a mess. They are not: they collapse into **five
questions**, and answering those five closes all of them. That brief is
written and waiting for you.

**Three of Duff's rules were measured and deliberately NOT
implemented**, because doing what the book says would have made the tool
worse. One example: enforcing the classical rule about which letters may
end a word would have made the program refuse 175 correct entries ---
Sanskrit written in Tibetan letters, where the rule does not apply. The
measurements are recorded so the question is not re-opened without new
evidence.

### The first step for a beginner, measured at last

On a hike in September I asked for the one thing a beginner needs first:
being able to tell where one word stops and the next begins. That work
had been blocked since, because two honest attempts to count word
frequency had disagreed by two and a half times, and a rule was set that
no figure could be published until the disagreement was settled.

It is settled, and the answer is measured:

- **The most common 2,000 words cover 76% of everything we hold.** The
  most common 4,000 cover 86%.
- **Thirty-two connecting particles alone are a fifth of all running
  text.** Those are exactly the small words that show a reader where one
  word ends --- the cheapest thing on the list, and the first thing to
  teach.
- The choice that was feared would move the answer moves it by three or
  four points, not by two and a half times.

I published the first version of these figures and **they were wrong by
three points**. Two spellings of the same word were being counted
separately, and it was visible on the first line of output. The page now
carries the wrong numbers and the reason beside the right ones.

### Course 5 alignment resumed

The full-depth reading of Course 5 stopped on 8 September at segment 165
of 511, at my call, while the app went toward release. It has started
again. **Segment 168 of 511 now**, and the first new page is done to the
full standard: two independent analysts on each sentence, their
disagreements settled by a third against the evidence already banked.

On one sentence the two analysts agreed on only 25 of 44 alignments,
which is what the two-reader method is for. Two proposed alignments were
**dropped rather than banked** --- each would have passed every
automatic check and still been false.

### A defect in Geshe Michael's dictionary, found by reading the screen

I noticed the program showing the word *gus* --- reverence --- as "i".
It is not the program's doing: the dictionary itself stores "i" first
for that word. Measured: of 12,004 entries, ten lead with a single
letter, and **eight of the ten are correct** --- "i" is the pronoun *I*
stored in lower case, and several Tibetan particles genuinely correspond
to English endings. Two are wrong, *gus* among them.

That question also produced a tool: a way to narrow 11,968 entries to 55
worth a person's eye. Reading those 55 found **ten glosses that do not
describe their headword**, each confirmed against two independent
dictionaries.

## Open questions for leadership

- **The five questions** in the decision brief. The largest: whether we
  should model the traditional Tibetan account of letter gender, from
  which the classical grammarians derive tense and other things. We
  currently use a Western description instead. It is not wrong; it is a
  different axis.
- **Whether "particle" is the right word.** Duff argues it mistranslates
  the Tibetan, which means "phrase linker". My own advice is to find out
  what Geshe Michael's courses say first: if they say particle, the
  students' vocabulary should win.
- **One reading needs a person who knows Sanskrit.** Our software and
  the Illuminator disagree about a cluster of letters, and our own
  dictionary has nothing to say --- not one of its 105,634 entries uses
  it.
- **What "all of Geshe Michael's translations" means.** The 42,199
  aligned passages are a portion. Establishing what is missing needs the
  organisation's records, not the program's.

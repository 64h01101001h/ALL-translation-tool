# Diamond Cutter Translation Tool — daily digest, Tuesday 9 September 2026 (DRAFT — covers Monday evening so far)

**Headline.** After Monday's digest went out, the afternoon and evening
went to making the tool feel like a complete editor: the menus were
brought to parity with Microsoft Word and Sublime Text — File, Edit,
Selection, Find, Goto and View — every item built, wired and covered by
automated tests, and the dictionary's aligned equivalents now rank by how
often Geshe Michael actually used each rendering.

## What got done

- **File menu, complete.** New Document, New from Template, Close, Save,
  Save As, Save All, Save as Template, Move, Rename, Print, Revert, Close
  All, and a five-tab Properties window (General · Summary · Statistics ·
  Content · Custom). Statistics understands Tibetan text: folios,
  syllables and shads alongside words and characters. Properties are kept
  in a small companion file so they survive renames. Encodings for legacy
  input-centre files (Windows-1252, MacRoman, UTF-16 and others) can be
  chosen on open and on save; undecodable bytes are counted and shown,
  never guessed at.
- **Edit and Find.** Paste Special converts whatever is on the clipboard
  between ACIP, Wylie and Tibetan script on the way in. The new Find menu
  follows Sublime Text: incremental find, regular expressions, case and
  whole-word matching, search inside a selection, highlight-all with a
  count, replace with preserved capitalisation, find-in-files through the
  Search pane, and the F3 family.
- **Selection and Goto.** Expand the selection to a line, word, paragraph,
  bracket pair, quotation, whitespace run — and, unique to us, to a
  **folio** (between @ markers) or to the enclosing **phrase** in the
  Overlay's nesting. Goto a heading in the text's outline, a line, a
  folio, a word's dictionary entry or its concordance; jump back and
  forward through where the caret has been; bookmarks appear as dots in
  the margin and persist per document.
- **View.** Line numbers in the margin, word wrap, line-ending choice
  (LF / CRLF / CR, written back on save), Focus mode, ribbon and status-bar
  toggles, full screen, spelling-doubt navigation on F6, and a status bar
  that shows line, column, script, encoding and line ending.
- **Dictionary ordering.** For every headword the aligned English
  renderings are now listed most-attested first, with counts. For THAMS
  CAD the file reads: all ×30, every ×14, each and every ×10, All ×5. The
  label remains "most attested", never "most probable".
- **Stability.** Two defects found by the test battery on the way were
  fixed the same hour: a false "corrupt image" verdict in the packaging
  script caused by a stray system helper, and a crash at application
  shutdown in a new status-bar hook.

## Adam's decisions and actions

- Chose Microsoft Word and Sublime Text as the two references for the
  tool's menus and preferences, and supplied screenshots of every menu.
- Kept the audit's rule: every item is built, adapted or skipped for a
  stated reason; multi-caret editing and page-layout notions were skipped
  as having no honest meaning on a Tibetan text file.
- Asked for the aligned equivalents to be ranked by frequency.
- Filed three larger ideas for after the release: a **Textual Research**
  workflow (user-built collections, word and variant studies across
  editions and authors, place and lineage queries), an **interactive map**
  of monasteries and holy sites built from openly licensed place data,
  and companion **iOS/Android reader** apps.
- Decided the desktop copy need not be re-pressed until the menu work is
  complete.

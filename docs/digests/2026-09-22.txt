**Headline.** The aligned dictionary passed **fifty thousand attested links**.
Along the way it found something that matters more than the milestone: a

## What got done

### A defect that wrote its own dictionary entry, and then cleared itself

Course 5, line 335, spells the fourth non-virtue as *ma byin lan*. The correct
spelling is *ma byin len* --- "taking what is not given". The proof is not a
judgement call: the same root verse is restated in prose two lines later and
spells it correctly, another line of the same course spells it correctly, and
across everything we hold the correct spelling appears in fourteen places in
seven courses while the wrong one appears in two --- this line, and one
re-ingested copy of the same file.

**Two independent readers cleared the line anyway**, and they cleared it for a
reason that sounds excellent: the misspelling *is* a headword in the
dictionary. It has a definition. It is marked as attested.

Its record of where it occurs lists exactly those two places --- this line and
its copy. **The entry was built from the error.** So the error now launders
itself: anyone who checks the spelling against the dictionary finds it
confirmed, because the dictionary learned it here.

The correct sibling entry carries its Sanskrit, a scholarly gloss, and
thirteen occurrences across seven courses. The two sit side by side.

**Then it happened again, and this time we went looking.** Line 346 of the same
course writes one phrase two different ways twenty words apart. Applying the
same test: the rarer spelling is also its own dictionary headword, also built
from exactly the four places that carry it. That one is recorded as a variant
rather than a fault, because unlike the first it does appear in two genuinely
independent documents --- and the entry says which half is certain and which is
not.

**Why this matters beyond two words.** The rule we now work by: before letting
a reference work settle a question, ask what that reference work was *built
from*. An entry whose entire evidence is the line you are auditing is not
evidence. It is an echo. Both entries are recorded by number so that whoever
cuts the next dictionary release can decide whether to retire them.

### The work remaining is ten times smaller than I said it was

This is a correction to my own figures and it is the most practical item here.

I had been sizing the reading against everything we hold --- forty-two
thousand passages. The standing instruction is the eighteen courses, which is
nine thousand. Subtract Course 13, which is set aside because its English is
paired with the wrong Tibetan, subtract what is already read, and subtract the
passages whose text is **exactly identical** to something already read ---
Course 16 is half duplicate, Course 7 a fifth --- and what actually remains to
be read is about **four thousand passages, not forty-two thousand.**

Both of those corrections are free and mechanical. Duplicate passages do not
need a second reading; they need copying, and we already have evidence that is
safe --- two hundred and twenty-three comparable duplicate pairs already read
independently agree completely, two hundred and twenty-two of them perfectly.

I would rather report this as my own error than let a wrong number keep
shaping decisions about what this costs.

### Fifty-three lines of the help text broke a rule, because the checker was told not to look there

In July the rule was set that Geshe Michael is to be named rather than
pronouned. A check enforces it across the program, the documents and the
digests. It excluded one folder --- correctly, because that folder holds the
dictionary and the corpus, which are nobody's to edit.

That folder also holds the **help text the program shows on screen**, and the
blanket exclusion hid fifty-three lines in it --- more than the whole rest of
the project put together. Fifty-one are fixed. Two are correct as they stand
and are recorded with reasons: one refers to the scholar who gave us a licence
in writing, and one is a worked example about nobody in particular.

The lesson is the ordinary one: a check that skips what the reader actually
sees is pointed the wrong way.

### Three copies of the manual had been describing an older program since August

Chasing the above turned up three distribution copies of the user manual,
nearly two thousand lines, untouched since the 22nd of August while the
manual itself moved under them. They were advertising a smaller program --- no
keyboard reading controls, no second pronunciation system, no glossary links
--- and one of them told the reader to open a pane that had been renamed weeks
earlier.

They are generated from the real manual now, and a check compares them to a
fresh generation, so they cannot drift again.

### The checks themselves: thirteen of seventeen findings closed

Last week's audit of our own checks found seventeen faults in them. Thirteen
are now closed, each proved by deliberately breaking the thing it watches and
confirming it complains. Four remain and are named so nobody has to hunt.

Two are worth reporting plainly because they were faults of mine. One check
had been quietly certifying that our build records came from a clean source
tree when it was structurally incapable of ever saying so. And one limit I set
myself --- in the very commit that fixed that check --- was set one notch above
what it actually measured, which is a notch of room for something to go wrong
unnoticed. Both are fixed, and a check now refuses any limit set above its own
measurement.

# Geshe Chodrak dictionary lane — verification & etext hunt

## The author and the work — VERIFIED
- **Geshe Chodrak (dge bshes chos kyi grags pa / chos grags), 1898–1972.**
  Buryat Mongol scholar working in Lhasa; compiled the
  Tibetan–Tibetan dictionary **brda dag ming tshig gsal ba** in
  1946, with the assistance of Gendun Chophel; **published 1949**
  from the Lhasa Horkhang house (Horkhang Sonam Pelbar, per
  Treasury of Lives). Later Chinese editions add Chinese glosses
  (separately copyrighted material — avoid).

## Copyright verdict — honest, two jurisdictions
- **Life+50 jurisdictions (incl. China, the country of origin):**
  d. 1972 → the original Tibetan text entered the public domain
  **1 January 2023**. The "PD-2023 math" in the TODO holds.
- **United States — the wrinkle:** published abroad in 1949 and
  still under copyright in the source country on the URAA date
  (1 Jan 1996), the work's US copyright was restored to 95 years
  from publication → **US term runs to end of 2044**. A
  US-distributed app should treat the text as NOT clearly free in
  the US until 2045. (Research summary, not legal advice.)

## Etext hunt — results
- **Christian Steinert's open dictionary set: NOT present.** The
  public directory (68 dictionaries, incl. the Tibetan–Tibetan
  tshig mdzod chen mo, dung dkar, dag tshig gsar bsgrigs, etc.)
  contains no Chodrak file.
- **PKTC (Padma Karpo Translation Committee): the only known
  etext.** They hand-keyed the dictionary and sell it as a digital
  dictionary (also the iOS "Geshe Chodrak Dictionary" app). Their
  keying is their own edition — licensing from PKTC is the
  practical lane regardless of the underlying text's PD status,
  and Adam's PKTC outreach draft (pending send) already covers
  this together with the Das question.
- **Scan-and-OCR lane (fallback):** BDRC likely holds the 1949
  Lhasa printing in scan form; our own OCR pipeline could digitize
  it. Blocked by the US-term wrinkle above for a distributed app;
  viable for in-house research use in PD-2023 jurisdictions only.
  Park until the PKTC answer arrives.

## Ruling for the app (self-ratified, consistent with the survey's
red-flag discipline)
Do not ingest any Chodrak text until either (a) PKTC licenses
their edition, or (b) the US term question is resolved for a
scan-derived text. The lane stays on the licensing queue; nothing
ships on a guess.

# Translator workflow questionnaire — Mixed Nuts translation team (draft, 2026-09-08)

*Purpose: before the Diamond Cutter Translation Tool goes public, learn
how the team actually translates today, where the pain is, and what they
would want from a tool — so that what ships earns its place. Answers feed
the release audit: a function nobody asked for and nobody uses does not
survive the steelman.*

*Send as email from Adam; answers by reply (or a shared form if easier).
Aim: 15 minutes to answer. Every question optional.*

---

**Subject:** A few questions about how you translate — 15 minutes that will shape our new tool

Dear friends,

Over the past weeks I've been building a desktop tool for our translation
work: it puts Geshe Michael's English equivalents, the whole corpus of his
published translations, the reference dictionaries, the BDRC woodblock
scans and a translator's drafting bench in one place, and it is about two
weeks from a first public release.

Before it goes out, I want to make sure it fits the way *you* work rather
than the way I imagine you work. Would you take about fifteen minutes to
answer the questions below? Reply to this email in any form — short
answers, long ones, a voice memo, a photo of your desk. Skip anything
that doesn't apply. Nothing you say will be quoted with your name.

Thank you. What you tell me decides what gets built next and what gets
cut.

With gratitude,
Adam

---

## Part 1 — How you work now

1. **Walk me through your last translation session.** From opening the
   Tibetan to closing your laptop: what did you open, in what order, and
   what did you have on screen at once?

2. **What do you translate from?** A typed ACIP/Unicode file, a PDF of a
   pecha, a BDRC scan, a printed book, Geshe Michael's own file? Where do
   you get it, and what do you do to it before you can start?

3. **Which dictionaries and references do you actually reach for**, in
   order of frequency? (Geshe Michael's glossaries, Hopkins, Das, Jäschke,
   Monlam, 84000, Rangjung Yeshe, THL, a Sanskrit dictionary, class notes,
   something else?)

4. **When you need to know how Geshe Michael rendered a term**, how do you
   find out today? How long does it take? How often do you give up?

5. **Where does your draft live** — Word, Google Docs, Pages, a text file,
   paper? How do you keep the Tibetan and the English side by side, and
   how do you keep track of the terms you've decided on?

## Part 2 — Where it hurts

6. **What is the slowest or most tedious part** of your process? The thing
   you would pay to never do again.

7. **Where do errors creep in?** Misread letters, wrong particle, a term
   rendered two ways in one text, a lost footnote, a citation you could
   not trace? Tell me about a real one.

8. **What do you do when the typed text and the woodblock disagree**, or
   when a word is not in any dictionary?

## Part 3 — What you wish existed

9. **If a tool could do three things for you tomorrow, what would they
   be?** Be greedy and specific.

10. **What would make you *distrust* a tool like this?** (For example:
    machine-generated glosses that look official; hidden edits to Geshe
    Michael's English; anything that guesses silently.) What labels or
    guarantees would you need to see?

11. **Which of these would you use, and which are noise?** Tick or strike:
    - click a word, see Geshe Michael's equivalent and every place he used it
    - the outline (sa bcad) of the text extracted automatically
    - quotations from the root text detected and marked
    - the BDRC scan following your cursor page by page
    - a drafting bench with the Tibetan clause-anchored to your English
    - a terminology checker that warns when you render one term two ways
    - the published footnote and bibliography banks, insertable in one click
    - Tibetan → Wylie → ACIP → Unicode conversion, Sanskrit in all its scripts
    - pronunciation (Geshe Michael's convention) for reading aloud
    - reading trainer and drills for students
    - OCR of scans into typed Tibetan
    - a review report before a draft goes to the editor

12. **Anything I haven't asked** that I should have?

## Part 4 — Practical

13. **What computer do you use** (Mac / Windows / Linux; how old; screen
    size)? The first release is Mac only — would a Windows version matter
    to you, and how much?

14. **Would you be willing to try an early build for a week** and tell me
    what broke? If yes, I'll send you the installer and a two-page start
    guide.

# ALL Translation Tool — Authority & Admin Guide

*For the authority (Geshe Michael / Adam). The design principle this
guide serves: nothing enters the official data without a ruling —
the team proposes, the authority decides, and the software records
who ruled and when.*

## Enabling the authority role

The authority role is a switch in **Settings** (View menu →
Settings…, ⌘, on a Mac) under Team & proposals — or in the setup
fields at the top of **Community → Propose**; they are the same
settings in both places. Turn it on, make sure your name is entered
(every ruling is stamped with it), and confirm the shared proposals
folder points at the team folder. The role change takes effect on
the next launch; from then on the **Approval** pane appears under
the Community tab, with a live count of pending items on its tab.

## The Approval queue (Community → Approval)

Every pending proposal appears with its evidence, source citation,
and proposer's name. For each item:

- **Approve / Decline / Defer** — one click each, plus an optional
  note. Your note travels back to the proposer ("My proposals —
  what happened?" in the Propose pane shows them your comment).
- **Filter by kind** to take one category at a time.
- The list **keeps your scroll position** between rulings, so
  working down a long queue is comfortable.

## What each ruling actually does

The data boundary is enforced in the software itself — different
kinds of proposal have different consequences, and none of them
ever edits the corpus or the texts.

**Register kinds — honorific (all four levels), pronunciation
exception, abbreviation.** Approving applies the item **in the app
immediately**, stamped with your name and the date, for everyone
using the shared folder. Declining a machine-derived pronunciation
removes it. These live in the tool's own registers, so no export
step is needed.

**Dictionary kinds — word/phrase rendering, idiom / fixed
expression, note.** Approval marks them approved but the app never
writes them into the dictionary or corpus. Use **"Export approved
dictionary candidates…"** to produce the package for the data
project, which admits them into the next versioned release. Until
then they remain labeled candidates.

**SPELLING flags** (filed from the Overlay's "Show spelling doubts"
list) have their own filter and their own meaning:

- **Approve** = you confirm it is an input error. It goes into the
  export for the input teams — the app itself never edits the
  texts.
- **Decline** = you rule the form **valid**. From the next launch
  on, the software stops doubting that form everywhere — no more
  red wave on it, for the whole team. A decline here is not a
  rejection; it is a positive ruling that teaches the software.

## The Rulings archive

Every decision — approve, decline, defer, with its note, date, and
your name — is kept permanently in the **Rulings archive**. It is
the audit trail and, over the years, a record of the authority's
own judgments. Nothing is ever silently discarded.

## Recommended cadence

Take **one kind per sitting**: filter to a single category and rule
it whole — pronunciations one week, honorifics the next, spelling
flags after an input batch comes in. Each sitting stays short, the
judgments stay consistent within a category, and the queue never
becomes a wall.
